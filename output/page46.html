Romano: Defender an ‘absolute priority’ for Milan whilst Cardinale continues Endrick ‘obsession’

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

By: Ben Dixon

AC Milan are expected to be busy in January, and Gerry Cardinale’s words today have only fuelled those beliefs.

It was clear to everyone that Milan ended the summer unsatisfied with the squad, especially after the last-minute pursuit of Goncalo Inacio. So, reports emerging about January targets in the days after were not a shock. In fact, they were a welcome statement of intent from the club early on.

Since then, there have been several links to a number of interesting players and with two months to go until January, there will be several more, especially after Cardinale’s statements today. However, there have been a few repeated names that haven’t disappeared, with Inacio’s being one of them.

In the attack, the name of a Brazilian starlet has been thrown around constantly and it seems that it won’t be stopping anytime soon.

Cardinale pushing for January additions

Speaking on his YouTube channel tonight at length, Fabrizio Romano looked at a few names linked with the Rossoneri, and MilanNews have relayed his words.

“I’d also add that Cardinale had some very important words about the transfer market, because over the past few months, we’ve often said that Milan did a lot of good things during the summer transfer window, but lacked others. So, Milan needs to strengthen their squad.

“They finished the summer transfer window by looking for central defenders, but then failed to sign him, and in the end, Tomori was reinstated.

“Milan certainly intends to make a move in January. Cardinale confirmed this: ‘ We’ll have to be aggressive in the January transfer window. In the summer, I wasn’t able to do everything I wanted, so I’ll have to strengthen the team, I’ll have to strengthen the bench.’

“It’s clear that when he talks about the bench, he’s talking about rotation because Milan are involved in multiple competitions and need more depth in the squad. This was also evident against Bologna when the team looked very tired in the second half; they’re clearly feeling the strain of playing so many games in a short time.

“Milan will be active in the winter transfer window, so much so that they’ve already started making calls. The central defender is absolutely a priority , so they’ll sign a new defensive reinforcement and have already started contacting him. It’s clear that there are some names that AC Milan already know very well by heart.

“I’ll name one: Gonçalo Inacio is a player Amorim mentioned at the start of last summer’s transfer window. He’s a player AC Milan has been working on, so he’s a name that should always be kept among their favorites. He’s not the only one; AC Milan is also evaluating others.

“I believe that the central defender is one of the things AC Milan wants to do in January, but not the only one, because there’s the possibility of seeing something more. Obviously, this depends on the opportunities and the right names.

“Cardinale’s idea remains to make some additions, but he wants to do it sensibly and with a clear approach. He doesn’t want to sign someone just for the sake of signing them. AC Milan wants to add the right pieces who can be useful today, but also for the future.

“Many of you continue to ask us about the AC Milan striker, because it’s clear that at the moment, even in terms of alternatives, Camarda hasn’t found much space so far and when he did find one he wasn’t seen, so it’s clear that many of you are asking us about the striker.

“I’ll tell you one thing about Endrick: the Brazilian is an absolute obsession of Cardinale; he’s an absolute obsession of AC Milan. In the sense that AC Milan tried to sign him last summer, but found the doors closed.

“Real Madrid didn’t want to release him and that remains Mourinho’s idea even now. The Portuguese continues to repeat in the press and elsewhere: ‘I don’t want Endrick to go, Endrick will be successful here at Real Madrid, we’re counting on Endrick.’

Photo by Justin Setterfield/Getty Images

“So Real Madrid, through José Mourinho, is sending signals of absolute permanence. It’s clear that what AC Milan did in the summer was to position themselves; that is, AC Milan knew in August that they couldn’t sign Endrick because Real Madrid had already closed the doors, but they still chose to show their appreciation and to position themselves if a door were to open, whether in January or next summer.

“Right now, Real Madrid have no intention of releasing him. However, Milan will be there when and if a door opens. He won’t be the only one, because Endrick is liked by Roma, Aston Villa and there are also many other teams following him, but this is the scenario today.”

Chivu discusses poor derby record, new San Siro and Milan’s quality: “At our best”

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By: Ben Dixon

Ahead of tomorrow’s game against AC Milan, Inter boss Cristian Chivu spoke to the media about his team’s chances.

For Inter, this is perhaps the biggest derby in the recent run. After all, they’re without a win in seven games and if they fall to defeat, they’re inviting more pressure in the title race. Right now, though, they sit in a very comfortable position.

Having dropped points once this season – a draw against Roma – they’re rightly the favourites heading into this game, especially with the injuries plaguing Ruben Amorim’s side. Nevertheless, there are never really favourites in these games.

Chivu’s pre-Milan words

Speaking before the game on Friday, Ruben Amorim spoke about a number of things, admitting that his team still needed to improve this season and he’s not the only side of the derby to say that.

In his own press conference this evening, Chivu admitted the same, as MilanVibes relayed.

How are you approaching the derby?

“We’re coming prepared and aware of how important this match is for everyone. In a derby, you shouldn’t think about the standings, but only about doing everything you can to win.

“Our approach will depend on the team’s physical and mental condition, but also on the opponent’s characteristics. We know Milan’s quality, their dribbling, and their ability to hurt us through technique and speed. We’ll have to be ready and stay calm at all times.”

Seven derbies without a win…

“If we haven’t won a derby in such a long time, it means we need to ask ourselves some questions and figure out what we can improve on. I don’t think we lacked commitment, because in matches like this, incidents can also have an impact, but we definitely need to improve.

“We can’t settle for recent results, nor for last season’s, nor for what we’ve done so far this season: we need to show our best.

Photo by AC Milan

“The derby is a match that can give a lot to the winner and take a lot away from the loser, but we’re only at the tenth matchday, and last season’s history shows that the result of a derby doesn’t always determine what happens next.

“In a tournament, every match has its own importance. There are no favourites, and the pressure is on both teams: we need to avoid getting caught up in thoughts that only risk sapping our energy.”

On the team’s centre of gravity…

“Our pressing is often misinterpreted. We don’t go looking for a man in every area of ​​the pitch, but rather work on references. In recent games, we’ve tried to be more organised, choosing when to attack and when to wait. We can’t have the same attitude throughout the entire match: we need to understand the moments and always find the right balance.”

The match against Venezia…

“We need to rediscover that hunger that allows us to go beyond individual qualities. We can’t think that individual talent is enough to resolve every situation. We need to be more aggressive in duels, in pressing, and in defensive runs, putting in even more sacrifice.

“This is precisely what struck me about Nicola’s Venezia: the aggressiveness with which he approached tackles and the determination to win the ball back. These are characteristics we must always have, too.”

On Thuram…

“Thuram is fine and has no energy issues. He’s a player with great leadership, capable of changing situations on the pitch and making a difference. It’s precisely this responsibility that gives him extra motivation, and I’m confident he’ll have all the energy he needs tomorrow.”

On the match against Shakhtar…

“When you’re forced to come from behind, you inevitably expend a lot more energy. It also happened against Shakhtar, when we managed to win despite being down to ten men. The game we really messed up was the one against Brugge.

“As for Venezia, there are issues we’ve addressed in the dressing room that the players are well aware of. They can’t always be 100%, but mentally they give it their all. Sometimes, though, just playing well isn’t enough: you also have to know how to sacrifice yourself and find other solutions.”

On Carlos Augusto and Bastoni…

“I have to deal with injuries and find the best combinations through rotation. The situation forces me to carefully evaluate my options: otherwise, I’d have to rely on some young players, but this type of match isn’t the ideal context for launching them.”

Will Martinez play in goal tomorrow?

“We’ll see tomorrow…”

On scoring the first goal…

“I’m not a coach who prioritizes not conceding goals at all costs. I demand balance, maturity, and the ability to read the different moments of the game from my players. It bothers me when someone tries to exploit the situation or forgets the team principles, especially when it comes to pressing, running, and sprinting: trotting along doesn’t get you anywhere.

“They say a 0-0 draw is a good result, but if you win 1-0, aren’t you still satisfied? At Inter, on the other hand, you always tend to find something wrong.”

On the new stadium…

“It’s a trick question. I know there are those who are very attached to San Siro, and I am myself, but I’m also in favor of innovation. There are fundamental aspects to consider, such as revenue and the possibility of owning a stadium.

“Nowadays, in football, without financial resources you can’t move forward. So I welcome the changes, even though I admit I have a nostalgic side too. New things can be scary at first, but once you get used to them, they can bring great satisfaction.

“As for the possibility of coaching there in 2031, I prefer not to commit myself. Leading Inter is demanding: judgments often turn into sentences, and it won’t be easy to stay that way for long. Perhaps, if the sporting culture changes, it will be different.

“Who knows, maybe I’ll get to that point with a few less gray hairs and without having to end up in the hospital…”

Rare meteorite from Mars gives us a view into its ancient crust

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

Crater counts tell us that most of the Martian surface is more than 3 billion years old, but pieces of Mars we have on Earth tell a different story. The three main classes of Martian meteorites—shergottites, nakhlites, and chassignites—are all 2.4 billion years old or younger. Until now, only two genuinely ancient Martian meteorites were known: Allan Hills 84001, found in Antarctica, and a jumbled rock from northwest Africa nicknamed Black Beauty. Now we have a third one.

A team led by Yang Liu, a planetary scientist at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, has shown that a Martian meteorite called Teghaza 001, found in the Sahara Desert in Mali in 2022, formed at least 4.1 billion years ago. “It's very old and similar to some of the oldest rocks on Earth,” Liu says.

A filtered collection

“We don't fully understand why old Martian rocks are so rare on Earth," Liu said. Part of the answer may lie in how a piece of Mars gets here. It takes a large impact to launch a rock off the planet, and not every rock can survive that. “If the impact hits into loose powder, it may not get cemented to become a rock and fly off Mars' gravity,” Liu said. “So the natural impact already acts as a filter. Weak rocks do not make it off Mars.”
