David Beckham at AC Milan: Proving he was a footballer before a superstar

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

<p>Eighteen years have passed since David Beckham completed his first loan move to <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/ac-milan">AC Milan</a>. The switch was unusual, but it proved that Beckham still cared deeply about the game.</p> <p>When David Beckham signed for LA Galaxy in 2007, it was hard to see the move as anything more than a commercial decision.</p> <p>Because while the MLS project was clearly exciting for Beckham the businessman, it did not seem to offer much of a footballing challenge to <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/nostalgia/tribute-david-beckham-appreciated-clutch-player-now/">Beckham the player</a>. Rather, the 31-year-old appeared to be heading for an early retirement.</p> <p>But just a year and a half after signing for Galaxy, a 33-year-old Beckham was on his way back to Europe. Not to fully abandon his $250million Galaxy deal for good but to join Italian giants AC Milan on loan during the MLS off-season.</p> <p>The deal made sense for all involved: Galaxy could temporarily lower their wage bill, Milan got themselves a star player, and Beckham, crucially, got the chance to prove his value to the English national team.</p> <p>A decade on from that first Milan move (the midfielder would return the following year for a second spell), Beckham&#8217;s sojourn in Italy remains hard to assess.</p> <h3><strong>Galliani&#8217;s dream team</strong></h3> <p>At first glance, Beckham&#8217;s move to Milan appeared just as money-driven as his move to LA Galaxy.</p> <p>That was mostly Milan&#8217;s fault. When rumours of the transfer began to circulate in December 2008, Milan vice-president Adriano Galliani described the deal in less-than-romantic terms.</p> <p>&#8220;Football today is about full stadiums and sponsors and superstars like Beckham fill them up,&#8221; Galliani said. &#8220;With him, <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/quick-reads/remembering-seven-times-kaka-one-worlds-best-footballers/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Kaká</a> and <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/nostalgia/rivaldo-ronaldo-ronaldinho-milans-mixed-success-with-the-three-rs/">Ronaldinho</a>, it will be a dream team.&#8221;</p> <p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-32326" height="526" src="https://d3nfwcxd527z59.cloudfront.net/content/uploads/2019/01/02091204/David-Beckham-signs-for-AC-Milan-Adriano-Galliani.jpg" width="700" /></p> <p>Certainly, that trio was a dream team for the San Siro ticket office. But Galliani&#8217;s comments didn&#8217;t say much about what Beckham could offer <em>on the pitch</em>.</p> <p>In fact, Milan already had some of the finest creative midfielders in the game, including Andrea Pirlo and Clarence Seedorf, as well as the &#8220;dream team&#8221; Brazilians.</p> <p>Arranging the superstars into some kind of tactical order was the responsibility of Carlo Ancelotti, who said he was excited to work with &#8220;a serious athlete&#8221; and &#8220;great professional&#8221;. But it was still not obvious how Beckham would fit into his team.</p> <p>Understandably, journalists were keen to press him on the matter.</p> <p>&#8220;Milan have often started winning cycles by playing many offensive players together,&#8221; Ancelotti noted. &#8220;It&#8217;s necessary for everyone to sacrifice themselves, but it&#8217;s certainly worth a try.&#8221;</p> <p>True to his word, the Italian tried it sooner than anyone expected. Although Beckham hadn&#8217;t played a competitive game for two months, he was thrown in at the deep end, making his debut away at Roma on January 11, 2009.</p> <p>Ancelotti went gung-ho: Beckham lined up alongside Pirlo and Seedorf in central midfield, Ronaldinho and Kaká played ahead of them, and <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/quick-reads/revisiting-world-soccers-50-exciting-teenagers-10-years/">promising teenager Alexandre Pato</a> led the line.</p> <p>The match finished 2-2, with Beckham putting in a decent showing.</p> <h3><strong>Winning over sceptics</strong></h3> <p>Ancelotti revealed he had picked Beckham earlier than expected &#8220;because he demonstrated he was a very intelligent, serious and balanced player&#8221;. The midfielder, he said, had also &#8220;inserted himself well in our group&#8221;.</p> <p>But it didn&#8217;t take long before that &#8220;seriousness&#8221; turned into something a little more fun.</p> <p>On January 25, in his third match in Italy, Beckham scored his first Milan goal, the final strike in a 4-1 win over Bologna.</p> <p>The manner of the goal was as weird as seeing the Englishman in a Milan shirt: after being played in by Seedorf, Beckham struck the ball with the <em>outside</em> of his boot. Beckham was back, but he had a few new tricks up his sleeve.</p> <p></p> <p>That game marked the start of a purple patch.</p> <p>Just three days later, in a home match against Genoa, he had his second goal — one that was much more on-brand.</p> <p>In the 33rd minute, Beckham took a free-kick from a seemingly un-shootable angle, bending the ball into the near corner like only he could do.</p> <p><span class="mce_SELRES_start" style="display: inline-block; width: 0px; overflow: hidden; line-height: 0;">﻿</span></p> <p>After the match, which finished 1-1, Ancelotti said Beckham had enjoyed &#8220;another great game&#8221;. More importantly, praise was forthcoming from another Italian manager, England boss Fabio Capello.</p> <p>&#8220;I was happy with Beckham&#8217;s performance,&#8221; Capello said. &#8220;He seems well integrated in the Milan team.&#8221;</p> <p>If Beckham&#8217;s Milan switch was primarily an attempt to woo the England boss, it seemed to be going to plan.</p> <h3><strong>Seeing the positives</strong></h3> <p>To some extent, Beckham&#8217;s Italian job was a success. After joining Milan in January 2009, he earned call-ups for five of England&#8217;s six remaining World Cup qualifiers.</p> <p>By December 2009, ahead of the second loan, his place in the national side seemed secure.</p> <p>&#8220;If he plays well and is fit I will include him in my squad [for the World Cup finals],&#8221; Capello said. &#8220;He&#8217;s serious, he&#8217;s a great professional and he&#8217;s crazy about the World Cup.&#8221;</p> <p>By March 2010, however, those comments became redundant. Beckham tore his Achilles tendon in a match against Chievo, ruling him out of the World Cup and ultimately ending his international career.</p> <p>Italian football had helped him into Capello&#8217;s plans, but his body had betrayed him.</p> <p>Nor was the injury the only negative aspect of Beckham&#8217;s time with Milan.</p> <p>During his first Milan stint, Beckham gravely offended LA Galaxy fans by extending the loan beyond the initially proposed duration. The extension resulted in him missing a big chunk of the 2009 MLS season.</p> <p>When he eventually returned to the Galaxy team in July 2009, he was jeered and subjected to taunts from his own fans. He responded by attempting to confront members of the crowd, and the relationship between fans and player remained tense for some time.</p> <p><img alt="" class="alignnone size-full wp-image-32327" height="466" src="https://d3nfwcxd527z59.cloudfront.net/content/uploads/2019/01/02091303/LA-Galaxy-banners-David-Beckham-AC-Milan.jpg" width="700" /></p> <p>But while Beckham in Milan failed to result in Beckham at the 2010 World Cup, the unusual loan move did go some way to improving the player&#8217;s reputation among football&#8217;s more critical observers.</p> <p>If the Galaxy switch had signalled the player&#8217;s inevitable drift into matters beyond football, the Milan loan was a sincere attempt to claw his way back into the game. His motivations could hardly be doubted: the 2009 loan extension was made possible by Beckham paying some of his own wages.</p> <p>Ultimately, Beckham did not help Milan to any notable successes — they finished third in Serie A in both of his seasons there — nor did he help himself to a final World Cup appearance.</p> <p>He did, however, achieve something of value.</p> <p>By giving up holidays and wages to play in a competitive league, Beckham proved once and for all that he was a footballer first and a superstar second.</p> <p><strong>By Benedict O&#8217;Neill</strong></p> <p></p> <hr /> <p><strong>READ NEXT: <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/nostalgia/tribute-david-beckham-appreciated-clutch-player-now/" rel="noopener" target="_blank">A tribute to David Beckham, England&#8217;s man for the big occasions</a></strong><a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/nostalgia/revisiting-the-golden-age-of-pepsi-adverts-ft-beckham-carlos-and-totti/" rel="noopener" target="_blank"></a></p> <p><strong>TRY A QUIZ:<a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/quizzes/can-you-name-psgs-xl-from-david-beckhams-final-game-in-2013"> Can you name PSG’s Xl from David Beckham’s final game in 2013?</a></strong></p> <p>The post <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com/nostalgia/david-beckham-at-ac-milan-proving-he-was-a-footballer-before-a-superstar">David Beckham at AC Milan: Proving he was a footballer before a superstar</a> appeared first on <a href="https://www.planetfootball.com">Planet Football</a>.</p>

AlpineBSD announced – an AI-free FOSS OS

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p>Just days after Daemonarchy, there's another new OS in the BSD family – and one that is heading determinedly in almost the opposite direction.</p><p><a href="https://wiki.alpinebsd.org/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">AlpineBSD</a> is so new that it barely exists yet. There are no installation media available, though it's possible to <a href="https://wiki.alpinebsd.org/installation/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">manually bootstrap an installation</a>. What AlpineBSD does already have is an <a href="https://wiki.alpinebsd.org/ai-policy" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">AI policy,</a> and the plan is to avoid "the output of generative AI models (e.g. LLMs)" as far as possible:</p><p>"Any software whose code, documentation, binaries, or other assets include, in any part, the output of a generative AI tool such as an LLM, is considered 'tainted' for our purposes."</p><p>The core idea is to combine the kernel of NetBSD with "a userspace inspired by Alpine Linux and the objective of making it possible to use as little AI-generated software as possible."</p><p><a href="https://www.theregister.com/software/2024/05/18/gentoo-and-netbsd-ban-ai-code-but-debian-doesnt-yet/1434665" target="_blank">Back in 2024</a>, the NetBSD project made its position on AI plain. Its <a href="https://www.netbsd.org/developers/commit-guidelines.html" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">commit guidelines</a> say that "code generated by a Large Language Model […] is presumed to be tainted code."</p><p>Back in July, Linus Torvalds stated that <a href="https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/07/15/linus-torvalds-tells-ai-haters-to-fork-off/5271894" target="_blank">"Linux is not one of those anti-AI projects</a>". Since then, NetBSD has seen a <a href="https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/10/13/netbsd-may-have-found-a-new-role-the-ai-free-foss-os/5302818" target="_blank">spike in donations and interest</a>.</p><p>For someone mainly used to Linux, trying to install NetBSD is a little like hopping in a time machine to 30 years ago, and we speak from personal experience of Linux in the mid-1990s. As we mentioned when <a href="https://www.theregister.com/software/2023/07/20/slackware-turns-30/453790" target="_blank">Slackware turned 30</a> a few years ago, back in 1996, this vulture tried Slackware 3.0 on his own hardware, and we never did manage to install it.</p><p>We <a href="https://www.theregister.com/os-platforms/2026/08/20/netbsd-11-lands-with-risc-v-support-and-lightning-fast-vm-boots/5289713" target="_blank">tried NetBSD 11 a couple of months ago</a>, and despite considerable help from the folks on the NetBSD-users mailing list, we failed to get the CDE desktop to start.</p><p>As an example, installing software on NetBSD is not the easy, comfortable task it is on Linux. It does <em>have</em> a package manager for handling binary packages: it's called <a href="https://pkgin.net/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank"><span class="font-RobotoMono ">pkgin</span></a>, but as the <a href="https://www.netbsd.org/docs/misc/index.html#pkgin" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">NetBSD FAQ mentions</a>, it's an optional extra. You must manually tick a box in the setup program to install pkgin with the rest of the OS, and when we <a href="https://www.theregister.com/software/2022/08/10/netbsd-93-keeps-it-hardcore-old-school/343707" target="_blank">tried NetBSD 9.3 four years ago</a>, we didn't know that, overlooked that checkbox, and had to find a way to add pkgin later. (It is quite tricky to install a package manager when you don't have a package manager to install it with.) </p><p>This time around, we discovered that <span class="font-RobotoMono ">pkgin</span> sometimes doesn't complete the installation process for you – it just leaves a text file in a destination directory somewhere, and you're expected to know that, go there, read it, and do any further steps yourself.</p><p>The NetBSD community has been quietly going its own way since 1993. It has its own ideas about what is normal, and its users expect that. Some folks on the mailing list told us that they never use binary packages anyway – they compile what they need from source code.</p><p>AlpineBSD's <a href="https://git.sr.ht/~sircmpwn/absd-ports" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">ports repository</a> and <a href="https://sr.ht/~sircmpwn/alpinebsd/sources">source code</a> are hosted on Sourcehut, under the account of <a href="https://sr.ht/~sircmpwn/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">sircmpwn</a>, better known as <a href="https://drewdevault.com/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Drew DeVault</a>.
