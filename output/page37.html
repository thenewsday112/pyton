Right now, though, they sit in a very comfortable position.</p><p>Having dropped points once this season – a draw against Roma – they’re rightly the favourites heading into this game, especially with the injuries plaguing Ruben Amorim’s side. We know Milan’s quality, their dribbling, and their ability to hurt us through technique and speed. We’ll have to be ready and stay calm at all times.”</p><p><strong>Seven derbies without a win…</strong></p><p>“If we haven’t won a derby in such a long time, it means we need to ask ourselves some questions and figure out what we can improve on. These are characteristics we must always have, too.”</p><p><strong>On Thuram…</strong></p><p>“Thuram is fine and has no energy issues. Rare meteorite from Mars gives us a view into its ancient crust

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

<p>Crater counts tell us that most of the Martian surface is more than 3 billion years old, but pieces of Mars we have on Earth tell a different story. The three main classes of Martian meteorites—shergottites, nakhlites, and chassignites—are all 2.4 billion years old or younger. Until now, only two genuinely ancient Martian meteorites were known: Allan Hills 84001, found in Antarctica, and a jumbled rock from northwest Africa nicknamed Black Beauty. Now we have a third one.</p> <p>A team led by Yang Liu, a planetary scientist at NASA's Jet Propulsion Laboratory, has shown that a Martian meteorite called Teghaza 001, found in the Sahara Desert in Mali in 2022, formed at least 4.1 billion years ago. “It's very old and similar to some of the oldest rocks on Earth,” Liu says.</p> <h2>A filtered collection</h2> <p>“We don't fully understand why old Martian rocks are so rare on Earth," Liu said. Part of the answer may lie in how a piece of Mars gets here. It takes a large impact to launch a rock off the planet, and not every rock can survive that. “If the impact hits into loose powder, it may not get cemented to become a rock and fly off Mars' gravity,” Liu said. “So the natural impact already acts as a filter. Weak rocks do not make it off Mars.”</p><p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/rare-meteorite-from-mars-gives-us-a-view-into-its-ancient-crust/">Read full article</a></p> <p><a href="https://arstechnica.com/science/2026/10/rare-meteorite-from-mars-gives-us-a-view-into-its-ancient-crust/#comments">Comments</a></p>
