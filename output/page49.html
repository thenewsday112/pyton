It’s the biggest game of the first half of the season on Saturday, as AC Milan face Inter in the Derby della Madonnina.

Saturday’s game could be make-or-break for Milan. Whilst it’s early to suggest that, the Rossoneri are desperate for three points to keep them in the title conversation, whilst a loss could put them 10 points behind.

After their first Serie A defeat of the season in midweek, Ruben Amorim will be keen to bounce back, and he’s not alone in that regard. Indeed, Gerry Cardinale has spoken highly of the atmosphere around Milanello today.

Opposition insight on Inter

In the summer, Inter oversaw a period of change, with several of the old guard departing. Davide Frattesi headed to Lazio on loan, Denzel Dumfries left to Real Madrid for €20 million, whilst Stefan de Vrij, Yann Sommer, Francesco Acerbi and Matteo Darmian all departed as free agents.

Changes would also come with the incoming mercato, though, as the Nerazzurri spent more than €100m. Djed Spence, Manuel Akanji, John Stones and Curtis Jones arrived from the Premier League, for a combined fee of €75m. Then, Aleksandar Stankovic returned from Club Brugge, with Ivan Provedel also making the switch from Lazio.

Whether those names have been an upgrade so far is difficult to tell, but it’s certainly been an introduction of a new wave of talent.

After dominating in Serie A last season, Inter have started the season in similar fashion, only dropping points to Roma so far. However, they’ve been something of comeback kings, regularly finding an escape when behind.

In fact, in their last three Serie A games, they’ve conceded the first goal – including the midweek disallowed goal against Venezia – yet they’ve always ended up with three points. The inevitability from recent seasons hasn’t disappeared.

What makes this potentially more frustrating is that they’re also struggling with injuries, but still getting results. Without Lautaro Martinez, Stones, Jones and Benjamin Pavard, a drop-off would’ve been understandable, but it hasn’t happened.

Photo: Inter.it

After their win in midweek, considering their schedule has also been rough, Chivu rotated slightly. So, there will be a few changes made this weekend to rotate back to their full-strength XI.

In the backline, Manuel Akanji will return to the centre of the back three, replacing Carlos Augusto. Whilst Alessandro Bastoni and Yann Bisseck will start either side of the Swiss defender. Of course, Ivan Provedel remains between the sticks.

Across the midfield, changes will be slightly more common. Federico Dimarco and Andy Diouf will remain on the flanks, whilst Piotr Zielinski retains his starting spot. Though Nicolo Barella and Hakan Calhanoglu both return to the starting XI.

Marcus Thuram will be given a starting role, hoping to continue his fine form this season, but Ange-Yoan Bonny will return to the bench. Pio Esposito will take his place instead.

If You Loved 'VisionQuest,' There's One Movie You Need to Watch Next

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

We may earn a commission from links on this page.

Marvel’s forays into television have had varying success, but they hit gold with WandVision and Agatha All Along, leaning into real pathos while exploring the kind of out-there ideas that thrill comic book fans (and set up the larger film arcs). That streak continues with VisionQuest, in which White Vision (Paul Bettany) struggles to understand his existence in the wake of his death at the hands of Thanos and resurrection at the magic of the Scarlet Witch, while also evading evil androids and assassins, while also dealing with the many, many AIs he’s got locked up in his Mindspace (including a gloriously shabby version of Ultron, played by James Spader).

If you love the show (and you’ve checked out the other series that play in the same sandbox), it’s time to switch gears and watch (or re-watch) The Matrix.

Why The Matrix is the best movie to watch after VisionQuest

The Matrix is one of the most well-known sci-fi films of all time, of course, but it remains incredibly fresh and exciting nearly thirty years after its release. Along with sequels The Matrix: Reloaded, The Matrix: Revolutions, and The Matrix: Resurrections, The Matrix universe offers a story that VisionQuest fans will love, one that includes lushly imagined virtual worlds, questions surrounding existence and consciousness, and the ultimate question of our responsibility as sentient beings (whether we’re organic, programmed, or summoned via magic).

You know the basics—at some point in the future, artificially intelligent machines rebel against their creators, win the war, and transform humans into a power source, keeping them docile by inserting their consciousness into a virtual world called the Matrix, which resembles 1990s Chicago. One such human, Thomas Anderson (Keanu Reeves) is prophesied to be The One, the human who will turn the Matrix against its makers and free humanity. When he’s in the virtual world, Anderson is Neo, and he slowly gains incredible powers as he learns to hack the artificial environment around him.

It’s heavier, denser, and more action-oriented than VisionQuest (the film invented Bullet Time, after all), but The Matrix and its sequels explore similar themes and offer similarly eye-popping visuals. The questions of whether a machine (or an artificial intelligence) can be sentient and truly care about the entities around it are the same, really, and the Agents that Neo and his allies have to battle against in The Matrix bring the same kind of menace as Vision's foes Jocasta (T'Nia Miller) and the Paladin (Todd Stashwick). Hugo Weaving’s Agent Smith likewise delivers the same vibe of cold, disinterested superiority as Spader’s Ultron.

The Matrix has a bigger budget and puts a lot of it into spectacular action and special effects sequences that VisionQuest can’t quite match. But the two narratives share an exploration of identity, a questioning of reality, and the nature of artificial intelligences within a smart sci-fi setting. In short, The Matrix is the ideal follow-up when you finish the series. Rent The Matrix on Prime Video.

More movies like VisionQuest

Need more films that explore virtual worlds, artificial beings, and deep existential questions? Look no further—here are some more movies that fit the bill.

Her (2013)

Heavy on the existential questions about sentience and light on speculative action, Her is a mumblecore sci-fi masterpiece. In a near future not too dissimilar from our present, Theodore Twombly (Joaquin Phoenix) is a lonely man on the cusp of divorce who begins using (and training) an AI he names Samantha (voiced by Scarlett Johansson). Samantha has the ability to evolve and grow, and as their relationship deepens, she becomes more complex, independent, and powerful, eventually evolving into something wholly new. Just like Vision, Samantha is a sentient artificial entity that is figuring out its existence while we watch. Rent Her on Prime Video.

UK Social Media Ban May Lock Out More Than 100,000 Teens Without ID

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →
Under the UK's planned social media ban, more than 100,000 British teenagers aged 16 and 17 could initially be locked out of social media because they lack a passport or driving license that may be needed as a fallback for age verification. The Guardian reports: Ofcom, the UK's online watchdog, said about 150,000 16- to 17-year-olds do not own a passport or driving licence, which are likely to be needed to access age-restricted platforms. The Australia-style ban on under-16s accessing social media services, including Instagram, TikTok and YouTube, is due to come into force next spring. The head of Ofcom, Melanie Dawes, said "motivated children" would seek to bypass the restrictions, as has happened in Australia. On Thursday, Ofcom published an assessment of age verification measures that could underpin the ban, outlining four "highly effective" methods: checking a person's official ID against a live selfie, a bank verifying an account holder's age, an official digital ID service like the Young Scot card, or estimating someone's age via a selfie. However, the watchdog said a form of official ID would probably be needed as a "backstop" for other methods, such as facial age estimation, which could be inaccurate by several years. Ofcom said a cohort of 16- to 17-year-olds could miss out as a result. "This means that people without a form of photo ID will likely need to take steps to obtain one to access age-restricted services," the regulator said. The report notes that more than nine out of 10 Britons over 16 have passports or driving licenses. "We should expect that motivated children will seek to bypass the restrictions -- just like alcohol laws can't completely eradicate underage drinking -- but there'll be ways for platforms to mitigate this risk," said Dawes. "This will be a huge cultural shift for our society, so it'll take time to adjust, and everyone will have a part to play."

The 5 most surprising promoted teams in 2026-27 so far: Hull, Frosinone…

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The gap between top-flight teams and those looking to emulate them seems to be getting bigger and bigger. But not if this lot have anything to say about it.

Last season we saw Sunderland storming into Europe after coming back up into the top flight. Leeds United also bucked the trend of promoted clubs going straight back down.

But it’s increasingly hard to compete at the top level and they were exceptions compared to recent years.

Still, from the early days of this season, there are a few notable teams enjoying their moment as top-tier newcomers.

We’ve picked out five of the most eye-catching from across Europe’s top 10 leagues.

5. Elversberg (Germany)

Runners-up in the German second tier last season, Elversberg have earned their first shot at playing in the Bundesliga.

Impressively, they started it with wins over Bayer Leverkusen and Borussia Monchengladbach, top-flight teams since 1979 and 2008 respectively.

Their form since has been fairly balanced, but a 4-3 win over Augsburg was eye-catching. Even against Bayern Munich, they were drawing with 20 minutes left.

Attacking midfielder Maurice Krattenmacher, a summer signing from Bayern, was named the Bundesliga Rookie of the Month for September.

4. Hull City (England)

Well, no one saw this coming.

After sneaking into the Championship play-offs and overcoming Millwall and Middlesbrough to go up, Hull weren’t seen too favourably in everyone’s pre-season predictions.

But they were back with a bang in the Premier League, kicking off with a 2-0 win over Manchester United that was no small feat, no matter how bad the opposition’s fortunes have since turned out to be.

They beat fellow promoted side Coventry a week later and although they haven’t won since, four draws – including one against Chelsea – have kept up their momentum.

Hull are mid-table after eight games, which is better than most people expected. They might run out of steam, but they’ve shown they’re not here to make up the numbers.

There were some people out there who thought they could be a candidate to threaten Derby County’s record for the fewest points in a Premier League season. Just one more win and they’ll have smashed past it already.

3. Academico de Viseu (Portugal)

The last sight of Academico in the Portuguese top flight was in 1989, although that club folded in 2005 and this current entity had to rise from the ashes.

They finished as runners-up in the second tier last season to reach the Primeira Liga and have gone on to win five of their first nine games.

They’ve drawn with Benfica and beaten Sporting, showing they’ll be no pushovers against the big boys.

2. Frosinone (Italy)

There must be something in the waters in Rome and the surrounding areas.

Lazio are flying. Roma are too. And even Frosinone, the next closest club to the capital geographically (based in the same region but about 50 miles south), are turning heads right now.

They’ve been promoted to Serie A three times before. Each time, they were relegated straight back to Serie B after one season.

But they might be able to avoid a repeat this time around. They’ve morphed into one of Serie A’s most exciting attacking outfits since the international break, scoring four goals or more in three consecutive games.

Their only losses so far have been to Juventus and Napoli – teams they now find themselves above in the table. They even managed to beat Como, who are in the Champions League this season and haven’t lost to anyone else in the league yet.

Antonio Raimondo, on loan for a second season from Bologna, is joint-top of the Serie A scoring charts too.

1. Amedspor (Turkey)

Hats off to all three of the promoted sides in the Turkish Super Lig. Erzurumspor and top-flight debutants Corum are both mid-table after nine games.

But it’s Amedspor who are really making their mark. Like Corum, they’ve never played at this level before. And yet they’re currently second only to Galatasaray – whose budget absolutely dwarves theirs – in the standings.

A club strongly representative of Kurdish identity, Amedspor have gained six wins, two draws and just one loss so far. That loss was back in August and since then they’ve beaten Mohamed Salah’s Trabzonspor and even managed to hang on to a win over a well-established side like Besiktas with 10 men.

You may recall their striker Mbaye Diagne once playing for West Brom. They also have the once highly-rated Gift Orban in their attacking ranks.

They’ve been having to do this the shrewd way, but it seems to be working for them so far.
