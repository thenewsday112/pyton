Swansea City winger Ronald was so close to joining Millwall on deadline day, but just over a month later he has stepped up at the Den for the Welsh outfit.

Swansea City winger Ronald came close to leaving the club on deadline day, with a proposed move to Millwall breaking down after the two clubs failed to agree personal terms.

The Brazilian was reportedly travelling towards London before the transfer collapsed, leaving him to return to South Wales and face an uncertain future at the Swansea.com Stadium.

Photo by Lewis Clarke, via Geograph Britain and Ireland / Wikimedia Commons, licensed under CC BY-SA 2.0.

The 25-year-old had been the subject of an agreed £2m transfer, but the deal ultimately fell through, forcing him to put the disappointment behind him and fight for his place under Vitor Matos.

However, rather than allowing the failed transfer to derail his season, Ronald has continued working towards earning a regular role. Competition has increased following the summer arrival of Joseph Opoku and Moussa Yeo.

Ronald responds positively to failed Swansea exit

Having come so close to leaving Swansea, Ronald could easily have become frustrated by the situation, particularly given the uncertainty surrounding his first-team future.

Instead, he returned to training and remained part of Matos’s plans, with the manager publicly backing his attitude and commitment.

Ronald has continued to contribute when called upon, including a goal against Derby County and an assist against Burnley earlier in the campaign. Those moments have provided evidence that he can still offer something valuable to the Welsh side.

His key moment of the season though came against Millwall, the team he was so close to joining. The Brazilian bagged a last minute winner at the Den to earn a crucial three points, which kept the Swans top of the Championship at the time.

Matos’s faith in Ronald could prove important

Ronald has made 122 appearances for Swansea since arriving in January 2024, recording 13 goals and 13 assists. Those numbers have not always matched his energetic performances, with his dribbling, crossing and defensive work often more noticeable than his finishing.

However, his contributions this season suggest there is still a useful role for him to play. Matos previously described Ronald as “part of the family”, praising his work rate and desire despite the uncertainty surrounding his future.

His ability to provide energy and attacking threat could prove valuable as Swansea attempt to maintain their position among the Championship’s leading sides. Nevertheless, Ronald’s long-term future remains worth monitoring, with his contract running until 2027 and the club holding an option for another year.

The summer recruitment drive means regular starts are far from guaranteed, but his experience and willingness to contribute could make him an important squad member. After coming so close to leaving on deadline day, Ronald has responded professionally to a disappointing situation.

Rather than allowing the failed move to define his season, he has continued to fight for his place and give Matos another reason to keep him involved. Swansea City head to Blackburn Rovers this weekend.
