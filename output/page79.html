AWS Weekly Roundup: Prime Day 2026 by the numbers, Claude Haiku 5.5, Amazon Nova 2.5 Sonic, and more (October 13, 2026)

📅 Oct 13, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Some numbers are hard to picture until you sit with them for a moment. Last week we published the Prime Day 2026 metrics, and the one that stuck with me is 2.3 quadrillion: the daily requests Amazon ElastiCache peaked at during the event. Prime Day ran back in June, so this post always lands as a retrospective, and that gap is part of what makes it worth reading. By the time the numbers reach you, the only evidence that any of it happened is that nothing went wrong.

The rest of the week pulled in a different direction: smaller, cheaper, and more constrained. Claude Haiku 5.5 arrived on Amazon Bedrock at roughly 75 percent less than Haiku 4.5, Amazon Nova 2.5 Sonic made voice agents quicker on their feet, and Strands Box showed up to put real boundaries around what an agent is allowed to do. Now, let’s get into this week’s AWS news…

Headlines
All the numbers: Amazon Prime Day 2026 powered by AWS is the latest entry in a tradition that goes back to 2016, and the scale is still difficult to hold in your head. Over the four days of Prime Day, Amazon DynamoDB processed more than 59 trillion requests while peaking at 192 million requests per second, and kept single-digit millisecond responses throughout. AWS Lambda handled over 2.3 trillion invocations per day. Amazon EBS peaked above 24.8 trillion I/O operations, moving more than an exabyte of data daily. Amazon ECS launched an average of 158.3 million tasks per day on AWS Fargate, a 47.7 percent jump over last year, and AWS Graviton powered up to 49 percent of the Amazon EC2 compute that Amazon.com used.

The number I keep coming back to, though, isn’t a throughput record. The team ran more than 44,000 AWS Fault Injection Service experiments ahead of the event, more than six times what they ran in 2025. Those are deliberate failures injected on purpose, months in advance, so that the trillions of requests above turn into an ordinary shopping afternoon. If you’re preparing for your own peak moment, AWS Countdown Premium is built for exactly that kind of planning.

Last week’s launches
Here are some launches and updates from this past week that caught my attention:

  • Claude Haiku 5.5 is now available on AWS – You can now use the fastest model in the Claude 5.5 family on Amazon Bedrock and Claude Platform on AWS, at around 75 percent less than Claude Haiku 4.5 for most tasks. It’s the first Haiku with effort controls, so you can tune cost against intelligence per task instead of fixing one setting for a whole workload. It pairs naturally with Claude Opus 5.5: Opus plans and makes the judgment calls, and because Haiku is fast and cheap, you can run many Haiku subagents in parallel for routing, classification, and small code changes across many files.
  • Amazon Nova 2.5 Sonic for voice agents – You can now build real-time voice agents on a speech-to-speech Amazon Nova model with better reasoning, instruction following, and tool-calling accuracy, plus lower latency. That combination matters for multi-step work: a support agent can look up an order, check return eligibility, and start the return inside one conversation. It keeps expressive voices in seven languages, controllable turn-taking, and a 256K context window, and pairs with Strands Bidi Agents, now generally available, to get a production voice agent going in a few lines of code.
  • Amazon GuardDuty RDS Protection now detects data exfiltration and destruction – Amazon GuardDuty now looks past login anomalies to the queries themselves on Amazon Aurora PostgreSQL and Amazon RDS for PostgreSQL. RDS Protection for data activity builds behavioral baselines and uses ML-based anomaly detection plus threat intelligence to flag unusual data access, with no agents, extra infrastructure, or database configuration changes. This closes a real gap: once an attacker has valid credentials from phishing or a misconfiguration, their queries can look indistinguishable from your application’s.
  • AWS Lambda supports OAuth for self-managed Apache Kafka event sources – You can now authenticate Lambda Kafka consumers through your enterprise identity provider, such as Amazon Cognito or Okta. Event source mappings previously supported only SASL/PLAIN, SASL/SCRAM, and mTLS, which left teams in regulated industries unable to use Kafka ESM at all. Now you get the automatic scaling, batching, error handling, and event filtering of Kafka ESM with the same identity governance you apply everywhere else. It works with self-hosted clusters and managed offerings including Confluent Cloud, Aiven, and Redpanda.
  • Amazon Redshift adds Apache Iceberg materialized views – You can now pre-compute expensive joins and aggregations once in Amazon Redshift and store the result as an Iceberg table in Amazon S3, registered in the AWS Glue Data Catalog. You create them with familiar SQL, and the results are immediately queryable by any Iceberg-compatible engine, including Amazon Athena, Apache Spark, Trino, and Snowflake. Redshift keeps them current by recomputing only what changed. There’s a good deep dive on the AWS Big Data blog if you want the details.

For a full list of AWS announcements, be sure to keep an eye on the What’s New with AWS page.

Other AWS news
Here are some additional posts and resources that you might find interesting:

  • Introducing Strands Box: AI agent sandboxes powered by Dogwood – As more of us let agents run shell commands and call APIs without approving every step, isolation alone stops being enough. Strands Box is an open source sandbox (Apache 2.0, developer preview) that combines OS-level containment with fine-grained policies written in Dogwood, so permissions can depend on what the agent has already done. You can say “let the agent post to the incident channel, but no more than three times every 10 minutes” and have that enforced from outside the agent’s process. It also keeps real credentials out of the agent’s environment by attaching them at an egress gateway.
  • AWS Continuum sets a new standard in autonomous code security – Related to that theme, AWS Continuum for Penetration Testing now integrates into your CI/CD pipeline in public preview, turning penetration testing into a deploy-time event instead of something that happens off to the side. Developers get findings with severity, affected endpoints, and remediation guidance right in the pipeline output, and the pipeline re-tests to verify fixes.
  • How Postman runs Agent Mode for 40 million developers on Amazon Bedrock – A look at the architecture behind a feature serving a very large developer audience. Worth reading if you’re moving an agentic feature from a demo to something that has to hold up under real traffic.
  • ICYMI: What landed for AI builders in September 2026 – If you lost track of a month that included a lot of model launches, this is a tidy catch-up in one place.

Announcing AWS Lambda Web Functions

📅 Oct 12, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Today, we’re announcing AWS Lambda Web Functions, a new form factor that lets you run standard HTTP web applications on Lambda without rewriting your code into an event handler. You can take a standard port-listening server built with Express, Hono, Fastify, Next.js, or any Node.js framework, deploy it with a single command, and Lambda automatically handles scaling, multi-region distribution, and active CPU billing. No adapters, no infrastructure to manage, and no framework lock-in.

With this launch, AWS Lambda now offers three distinct form factors, each built on the same Firecracker isolation technology and designed for a different programming model.

Lambda Event Functions are what you already know as Lambda functions. They use the event-driven, single-concurrent handler model with wall-clock billing. We’re introducing the “Event Functions” name to highlight that programming model: your function receives an event, processes it, and returns a response. The event-driven programming model is ideal for event processing, queue consumers, and invocations triggered by other AWS services.

Lambda Web Functions use an HTTP-native programming model with multi-concurrent execution and active CPU billing. Your code listens on a port and handles standard HTTP requests. That programming model is ideal for web applications, API backends, and interactive HTTP workloads where multiple concurrent requests and I/O-heavy processing are the norm.

Lambda MicroVMs provide isolated, stateful sandbox environments with customer-managed lifecycles for running untrusted or user-generated code.

The three complement each other. An application might use Event Functions for asynchronous backend processing, Web Functions for the user-facing API and server-side rendering layer, and MicroVMs for interactive code execution features, all within the same AWS account and sharing the same operational model.

Why Web Functions
Modern web applications need to serve global audiences with fast, consistent response times while giving developers the freedom to use standard frameworks and iterate quickly. Building this today typically requires assembling multiple infrastructure components, configuring multi-region deployments, setting up latency-based routing, and tuning billing models to avoid paying for I/O wait. Developers often find themselves adapting their application code to fit a specific compute model rather than deploying the HTTP server they already have.

Lambda Web Functions removes this complexity. Each execution environment handles multiple requests simultaneously (up to 128 concurrent requests per environment), which means a burst of incoming traffic can share warm environments instead of mapping each request to a new one. Web Functions meter the CPU time your code uses, so time spent waiting on a database query or an API call consumes little CPU. Memory is metered for as long as the execution environment exists. For I/O-heavy web workloads like server-side rendering, API backends, and AI inference endpoints streaming token responses, this can reduce compute costs.

Let’s try it out
To get started, I wrote a simple Express.js server. This is the same code I run locally with node index.js, no Lambda-specific adapters or wrappers required.

import express from 'express'; const app = express(); const [host, port] = process.env.AWS_LAMBDA_HTTP_ENDPOINT.split(':'); app.get('/', (req, res) => { res.json({ message: 'Hello from Lambda Web Functions!' }); }); app.listen(port, host, () => { console.log(`Server running on port ${port}`); });

The only difference from running locally is the AWS_LAMBDA_HTTP_ENDPOINT environment variable, which Lambda sets to the host and port your server must listen on. To run locally, set it yourself, for example AWS_LAMBDA_HTTP_ENDPOINT=127.0.0.1:3000 node index.js.

With my code ready, I deployed using the AWS Command Line Interface (AWS CLI):

aws lambda-web deploy --name my-web-app --code ./my-app

One command. No prior resource setup. The CLI bundled my source, created the Web Function, published its first revision, attached a Web Function endpoint, and returned a live HTTPS URL in under a minute.

A revision is an immutable snapshot of the code I deployed. The endpoint is the HTTPS URL that routes traffic to a revision, or splits traffic across revisions. Those are the resources I use to iterate and to control how traffic moves to new code.

I tested it immediately:

$ curl https://<domain-id>.<shard-id>.lambda-web.us-east-1.on.aws/ {"message":"Hello from Lambda Web Functions!"}

Next, I wanted my application to serve users in Europe and Asia with lower latency. I redeployed with multiple regions:

aws lambda-web deploy --name my-web-app --code ./my-app \ --regions us-east-1,eu-west-1,ap-northeast-1

Lambda Web Functions replicated my code to all three regions and configured latency-based routing automatically. Each request now routes to the Region with the lowest latency for the caller, on a best-effort basis, through one global URL (<domain-id>.lambda-web.global.on.aws).

When I updated my code and ran the same deploy command again, the CLI detected the existing function, uploaded the new revision, and pointed the endpoint at it. Total turnaround from code change to live: seconds.

For production workflows that require a more gradual rollout, I can shift traffic between revisions with a single API call:

aws lambda-web update-web-function-endpoint \ --function-name my-web-app \ --endpoint-name dev \ --auto-deployment-mode Disabled \ --revision-weights '[{"revisionId": "<current-revision-id>", "weight": 90}, {"revisionId": "<new-revision-id>", "weight": 10}]'

This sends 90% of endpoint traffic to the current revision and 10% to the new one, giving me a canary deployment without additional tooling.

Better together with Amazon CloudFront
For applications that combine static and dynamic content, Web Functions works well alongside Amazon CloudFront. You can select a Web Function endpoint as the origin for your CloudFront distribution. Static assets are served from the cache while dynamic requests route to your Web Function, giving you CDN performance for static content with serverless compute for the dynamic parts.

Web Functions improves the private-origin experience with CloudFront. With this launch, you attach CloudFront origin access control (OAC) to a Web Function endpoint instead. CloudFront signs requests to the origin, and the endpoint allows only your distribution to invoke it. You get the same private-origin pattern CloudFront already provides for Amazon Simple Storage Service (Amazon S3), without exposing the endpoint or adding custom authorization to your app.

Now available
AWS Lambda Web Functions is available today in US East (N. Virginia), US East (Ohio), US West (Oregon), Asia Pacific (Tokyo), and Europe (Ireland). Web Functions run Node.js 24 (nodejs24.x) on Arm64 at launch and support Amazon Virtual Private Cloud (VPC) connectivity, allowing your function to access resources that exist within a VPC.

To get started, deploy your first Web Function in under a minute:

aws lambda-web deploy --name hello-world --hello-world

For pricing details, visit the AWS Lambda pricing page. To learn more, see the Lambda Web Functions documentation.

Agents with the Federal Bureau of Investigation (FBI) on Thursday arrested an executive at a Canadian cybersecurity firm in connection with an investigation into the ShinyHunters hacking group that recently relieved the FBI of sensitive data on thousands of agents, multiple sources tell KrebsOnSecurity.

The New York Times reported today that the FBI has arrested a Canadian man in Pennsylvania on suspicion of assisting ShinyHunters. The Times story did not identify the man, nor did a statement on Twitter/X about the arrest from FBI Director Kash Patel.

One source close to the investigation told KrebsOnSecurity the Canadian person arrested this week was visiting Pennsylvania for a cyber insurance conference, and that the suspect’s company specialized in handling ransomware negotiations with cybercrime groups. Another shared that control over the ShinyHunters investigation has been centralized at an FBI field office in Texas.

An online search reveals the Cyber Risk Summit was held at the Loews Philadelphia Hotel between Oct. 5 and Oct. 7. The conference had several sponsors, but according to the summit’s website its biggest sponsor was a Canadian security company called Cypfer.

According to LinkedIn, one of Cypfer’s “ex-founders” was Edward Dubrovsky, who is now associated with another Canadian security firm and sponsor called CyberSteward. In a post to LinkedIn approximately one month ago, Dubrovsky said he had plans to attend the Cyber Risk Summit with the rest of the CyberSteward team.

[Update: Oct. 10, 9:58 a.m. ET: A spokesperson for Cypfer said Dubrovsky was not a founder or co-founder as his LinkedIn profile claims, but instead served as a managing director before resigning in December 2025].

Edward Dubrovsky’s LinkedIn profile.

Edward Dubrovsky’s book, which centers on the intricacies of ransomware negotiations.

Sources tell KrebsOnSecurity the FBI has been poring over devices that were seized last month when the Dutch police arrested the convicted cybercriminal Pepijn van der Stap in connection with the ShinyHunters investigation, and that charges against principals at other companies that specialize in ransomware negotiation may be forthcoming.

Immediately after Van der Stap’s arrest, another member of ShinyHunters named “Rey” assumed control over the group and began taunting the FBI over data the group stole from the agency’s online recruitment portal, which included each’s person’s unit and specialization, as well as medical and psychiatric records.

Last week, Reuters reported that Rey — identified as a teenager named Saif Al-din Khader — had been detained and was cooperating with FBI investigators. On October 7, we detailed how Rey was apprehended as the cybercrime group allegedly sought to extort a navigation and digital aviation unit that was divested by Boeing in late 2025.

Laurence Stewart is set to leave Chelsea by mutual consent after almost four years at Stamford Bridge, with the club opting to redistribute his responsibilities rather than appoint a direct replacement.

The departure is understood to be amicable and won’t take immediate effect. Stewart could continue attending matches before formally leaving, following discussions with co-owner Behdad Eghbali over the club’s evolving sporting structure.

The story was first reported by Matt Law of The Telegraph and Nizaar Kinsella of BBC Sport, with Ben Jacobs subsequently providing further details about how Chelsea intend to operate.

Winstanley, Jewell and Shields take on expanded roles

Stewart arrived from Monaco in October 2022 as technical director before becoming co-sporting director alongside Paul Winstanley in February 2023.

Chelsea’s day-to-day sporting leadership will now be shared between Winstanley, Sam Jewell and Joe Shields, each with defined responsibilities.

Winstanley will continue overseeing transfer negotiations, player contracts and several departments at Cobham, including medical and performance operations. Shields’ work centres on the academy and player pathways, while Jewell has wider responsibilities across the BlueCo operation, including Strasbourg.

According to Jacobs, discussions about restructuring began in April and accelerated following the appointment of Xabi Alonso as head coach.

Stewart had overseen investment in Chelsea’s data analysis operation, although Jewell and Shields have increasingly taken responsibility for using that information in recruitment and talent identification.

Dave Fallows will remain involved in an advisory capacity, rather than taking on a fixed day-to-day portfolio.

The changes are being described as a natural evolution of Chelsea’s sporting model. There is no suggestion that Stewart’s departure followed a breakdown in relations, and the club are not currently planning to recruit a replacement.

Chelsea sweat on key stars as Bournemouth test looms

Chelsea return to Premier League action this weekend with a growing treatment table and a lot of questions for Xabi Alonso to answer before Saturday’s visit of Bournemouth.

Joao Pedro the big worry

The main concern revolves around Joao Pedro. Chelsea’s current player of the season rescued a point against newly promoted Hull, but he suffered a knock and sat out the damaging 3-0 defeat at Brentford. The problem also prompted his removal from Brazil’s squad shortly after he had returned to it.

It is understood that he is undergoing specialist knee treatment in an attempt to expedite his recovery, although reports in Brazil suggest that he could be out for up to four weeks. No date has yet been set, but a return later in October looks most likely.

Palmer and Caicedo edging closer

There is more good news elsewhere. Cole Palmer was among the players called up to the latest England squad, which did not go down too well with Thomas Tuchel. 

The midfielder has been recovering from a persistent groin injury and has been working with a rehab coach. He is likely to be fit this weekend.

Moises Caicedo has also struggled with an injury this season, playing just 14 minutes across the campaign. He’s spent the majority of time on the bench recovering from a bad calf injury, which ruled him out for most of the season. 

He made his only appearance as a substitute against his former club Brighton, but the game was red-flagged for Ecuador’s friendlies, so he did not play. 

Alonso said before the Brentford match that the player would be ready to return. It seems to be the case, unless something went wrong.

Reinforcements on the way

Captain Reece James missed the Brentford game with yet another hamstring problem. Alonso said he only just missed out, so a return seems likely.

There may also be debuts. Marco Palestra, the £27m signing from Atalanta, has been sidelined since a training injury just before the opening match against Fulham. He is back in full training and was expected to feature in a behind-closed-doors friendly with Portsmouth. Chelsea lost 2-0. Emmanuel Emegha, injured after scoring against Bromley in July, is also pushing for involvement.

Less clear is the position of Jamie Gittens. He limped off with an ankle injury playing for England Under-21s against Kazakhstan and was seen on crutches. Mike Penders is fine after a quad scare on Belgium duty. Returning to the bench against Turkey.

A fixture that could shape the autumn

Chelsea badly need a lift. Defeat at Brentford before the break left Alonso’s side looking shaky, and a squad stretched by injuries has not helped.

Saturday brings a chance to steady things. If Palmer, James and Caicedo are available, with Palestra and Emegha potentially adding fresh options, the picture will look much brighter at Stamford Bridge. Joao Pedro’s absence will still sting. However, a strong response against Bournemouth would go a long way towards easing the pressure and restoring belief.

ShinyHunters Extorted Boeing Spin-off Prior to Arrests

📅 Oct 07, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A teenager from Amman, Jordan suspected of leading the prolific data theft and extortion group ShinyHunters has been detained and is reportedly cooperating with the FBI to identify other members of the hacking gang. KrebsOnSecurity has learned that the suspect, who uses the hacker handle “Rey,” was detained as ShinyHunters was in the process of extorting a business unit recently divested by the global aerospace company Boeing, which manufactures the fleet of planes used by the employer of Rey’s father — Royal Jordanian Airlines.

The logo for Jeppesen ForeFlight, a business unit divested last year by the aerospace firm Boeing.

On October 3, Reuters cited three unnamed sources saying a suspected ShinyHunters member in Amman named Saif Al-din Khader was detained by Jordanian authorities and was cooperating with the FBI. KrebsOnSecurity identified Rey as Khader in a November 2025 profile, in which the young man admitted working with multiple ransomware groups.

Rey was featured again in a September 28 exclusive about the Dutch police arresting 24-year-old convicted cybercriminal Pepijn van der Stap on suspicion of aiding in data thefts and extortions by ShinyHunters. The story noted that immediately following the Dutchman’s arrest on the evening of September 15, Rey assumed control over the ShinyHunters brand and boasted publicly about stealing highly sensitive data from the FBI and extorting the ransomware group Cl0p.

Rey taunted both the FBI and Cl0p with memes posted to his longtime account on Twitter/X, while simultaneously including images of the avatar used by Van Der Stap’s former hacker alias “Umbreon” in an apparent attempt to frame the Dutchman for both hacks.

A taunting meme uploaded to Twitter/X by Rey on Sept. 22. A giant sized version of the Pokemon character Umbreon can be seen in the bottom left.

As noted in our September 28 report, ShinyHunters gained access to the FBI site and other victims by exploiting a vulnerability (CVE-2026-35273) in PeopleSoft, a software-as-a-service platform from the tech giant Oracle that is broadly used by companies to manage hiring and human resources, benefits and payroll. Oracle quickly issued a fix for CVE-2026-35273, which ShinyHunters first began exploiting as a zero-day in June, and at the time Mandiant released web application firewall rules intended for organizations that couldn’t apply the security update quickly enough.

ShinyHunters told BleepingComputer in June that the original goal behind exploiting the PeopleSoft vulnerability was to breach the FBI’s own PeopleSoft database, but the hackers said those attacks were unsuccessful for some reason. In recent weeks, however, ShinyHunters turned to a well-known URL-encoding trick to bypass Mandiant’s suggested web application firewall rules.

In a report released Sept. 25, security experts at Mandiant and the Google Threat Intelligence Group (GTIG) confirmed that ShinyHunters had mass-exploited the PeopleSoft vulnerability to steal data from dozens of systems across a range of industries, including higher education, technology, healthcare, agriculture, transportation and government.

Reuters reported October 5 that the FBI has removed a contractor at Accenture over their failure to patch the FBI recruitment website hacked by ShinyHunters, which exposed sensitive data on more than 5,000 FBI personnel, including each’s person’s unit and specialization, as well as medical and psychiatric records.

‘REY’ MEANS KING, AS IN ROYAL

According to two sources familiar with the ShinyHunters investigation, a navigation and digital aviation unit recently divested by the global aerospace company Boeing was among the victims that ShinyHunters was in the process of extorting when Rey was apprehended by Jordanian authorities.

Those sources said the FBI’s investigation into ShinyHunters gained renewed urgency with the group’s attempted extortion of the former Boeing unit, which allegedly included the theft of sensitive information that sources said could pose operational safety and security risks.

In a brief statement shared with KrebsOnSecurity, Boeing acknowledged the extortion attempts by ShinyHunters, and said the incident concerned data stolen from Jeppesen ForeFlight, a subsidiary that Boeing sold in November 2025 to the private equity firm Thoma Bravo for $10.55 billion.

“We are aware of claims by a threat actor regarding data allegedly associated with Boeing and our former subsidiary Jeppesen ForeFlight,” a Boeing spokesperson shared. “We are actively reviewing the matter with the Jeppesen ForeFlight team.”

A spokesperson for Jeppesen ForeFlight shared a written statement in response to questions, saying the company has seen no impact on their end. “Based on our investigation to date into this claim and proactive security posture, there was no impact to our operations or products.”

Rey’s alleged involvement in attempting to extort the former Boeing unit is noteworthy because there is strong evidence that his father works for Royal Jordanian Airlines, which is mostly controlled by the Jordanian government and operates its long-haul fleet on passenger planes built by Boeing. Rey claimed on Telegram in early 2025 that his father was an airline pilot, although that could not be independently confirmed.

However, as noted in our November 2025 profile of Rey, his family’s shared computer was at one point compromised by password-stealing malware, and the data collected by that malware clearly shows Rey’s father used the same credentials to log in at multiple online portals for Royal Jordanian Airlines employees.

Royal Jordanian Airlines has not yet responded to a request for comment. In advance of our September 28 story, KrebsOnSecurity once again emailed Rey’s father to seek comment and update him on his son’s alleged activities. Neither of the Khaders have responded. But just hours after that request was sent, Rey began deleting his various social media accounts, including the Twitter/X account he previously used to taunt the FBI, Cl0p, and other ShinyHunters victims.

Rey may have nixed many of his social media profiles, but his cybersecurity blog on GitHub somehow escaped the purge, and it shows that Rey was fixated on the leaders of the Cl0p ransomware group. In March 2026, Rey’s blog featured a lengthy post that identified two Russian men as the core developers and hackers behind Cl0p.

Rey’s blog on GitHub. This post doxes two Russian men as the core operators behind Cl0p, one of the oldest and most established ransomware groups still in operation today.

MURDER FOR HIRE?

Meanwhile, news outlets in the Netherlands reported explosive new allegations leveled at Van der Stap, whose supposed personal transformation from convicted to reformed hacker has been widely covered in the tech news media. The Dutch daily RTL reported on Sept. 29 that investigators suspect Van der Stap tried to orchestrate at least two murders. According to RTL, the murders were allegedly to be committed abroad, and there are indications Van der Stap gave the order for these attacks.

Van der Stap was released from prison after serving the better part of a four year sentence for data theft and extortion activity that prosecutors said netted between €1.5 million and €2.7 million. In an interview with KrebsOnSecurity on September 9, Van der Stap described his new role as “offensive security lead” at the Dutch cybersecurity company Neo Security, saying the job involved probing client networks for security vulnerabilities.

Neo Security’s owner Benjamin Korper told Reuters he has hired an outside firm to investigate whether Van der Stap had hacked Neo Security or its customers, but that so far investigators have found no evidence he acted against his employer or clients. Korper said Dutch forensic investigators visited his office on September 15, the night Van der ⁠Stap was arrested in a dramatic police raid that reportedly involved flash bang grenades.

A screenshot of a Sept 16 story by the Dutch news outlet at5.nl, describing a police raid on Van Der Stap’s residence that reportedly used flash-bang grenades.

Prior to his first arrest in 2023, Van der Stap was working as a software engineer at the Amsterdam-based cybersecurity startup Hadrian, while volunteering at the Dutch Institute for Vulnerability Disclosure (DIVD) — even as he was hacking into and extorting a number of large organizations.

When asked in a recent interview why anyone should believe the word of a self-described “reformed” cybercriminal who had so casually deceived countless friends, co-workers and journalists for years, Van der Stap replied that his work spoke for itself and there was nothing he could say that would convince his worst critics.

“You can throw a bunch of nice words at someone, but you can’t convince them if they don’t want to be convinced,” Van der Stap told KrebsOnSecurity on Sept. 9. “I’m doing what I can to repay victims, and that’s all I can do. If someone doesn’t want to believe me, then that’s on them.”

FRANCHISING AND BURNING A BRAND

Cybercriminals aligned with ShinyHunters have been responsible for dozens of data breaches involving billions of stolen records, and breaches claimed by the group stretch back to at least 2019. But experts say the people recently operating behind the ShinyHunters name are not the same core members that populated the group in its early days, most of whom are French citizens who have been arrested (if not also imprisoned) on at least one prior occasion for alleged cybercrime activity.

More to the point, ShinyHunters has become something of a franchise. Think the Dread Pirate Roberts character in the 1980s cult movie classic “The Princess Bride,” only succession by death is replaced with succession by arrest, and there can be multiple simultaneous Dread Pirate Robertses. Sources close to the investigation say the FBI is focusing on a remaining handful of cybercriminal freelancers or affiliates who have been feeding the group stolen credentials to various software-as-a-service (SaaS) platforms used by major companies in exchange for a cut of any data ransoms later paid by victims.

In the days after the news broke of Van der Stap’s arrest, a cybercrime-focused chat server on Telegram that was allegedly operated by Rey erupted with hot takes, with most participants heaping ridicule on the teenage hacker after he publicly backed down from threats against the FBI and Cl0p, and again when the ShinyHunters’s darknet website suddenly went offline. Several commentators accused Rey of resurrecting the ShinyHunters brand after its core members were rounded up in France, and making a mockery of the group’s name and reputation ever since.

“He bought the old forum PGP key and used it to make new Breachforum websites and Telegram channels larping as ShinyHunters to ransom companies and then sell the used data or resell his forum when he goes broke,” one member recounted.

A relatively new Telegram channel called “The Battle” has been doxing and needling Rey and other alleged ShinyHunters members for several weeks, and it has gained a considerable readership among the cybercrime communities operating on Telegram. One of the coordinators of that harassment campaign repeatedly portrayed Rey as clueless greenhorn who sought to ride the coattails of a cybercriminal brand that has long enjoyed a reputation for ruthlessly selling or publishing data stolen from victim companies who refuse to give in to extortion demands.

“Rey (Saif Al-Din Khader) made a serious mistake when he started pretending to be a member of ShinyHunters,” wrote the administrators of The Battle server on Telegram. “That group had already been dismantled, with many of its members either arrested or imprisoned, yet Rey still chose to use its name while carrying out his crimes. We’re aware of claims that [Rey] caused over $200 million in damages and helped around 5–6 friend groups in the community make money by using Shiny Hunters group aliases to negotiate deals for a 25–30% cut over the past few months.”

In an interview with The Register, ShinyHunters claimed they hacked the FBI to counter the agency’s narrative in a May 2026 alert that advised victims against paying a ransom to the group, which came off looking unprofessional and capricious in the FBI’s advisory.

A flash notice on ShinyHunters released by the FBI on May 15, 2026.

The public notice warned the group has been known to pursue a number of different victim harassment strategies, from sending threatening text messages and phone calls to victims and their family members to in some cases swatting victims. The FBI warned ShinyHunters members “may also falsely claim to have sensitive or compromising information, including embarrassing photographs or videos of victims, which frequently do not exist.”

The hackers told The Register their attack on the FBI “demonstrated our technical capabilities and directly refuted the misinformation disseminated by the FBI, journalists, and industry researchers.” At the same time, the group’s leaders seemed to acknowledge that the FBI’s warning materially harmed their prospects for convincing victims to pay, saying “this was fundamentally a public relations and marketing initiative for our business.”
