‘Come on, referee’ – Ex-PGMOL chief calls out Darren England over one incident in Liverpool win

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Darren England has been criticised for not issuing a yellow card in response to one contentious incident in Liverpool’s Carabao Cup tie against Chelsea on Wednesday night.

The referee booked four players over the course of the match at Anfield, but he didn’t reach for his pocket when Emmanuel Emegha inexplicably charged at Virgil van Dijk off the ball and shoved the Reds captain to the ground.

The veteran defender shrugged off questions about that incident afterwards, although a former chief executive of the PGMOL (now Pro Ref) believes that the Blues striker should’ve been cautioned for his indiscetion.

Hackett: England should’ve booked Emegha

Speaking to Football Insider, Keith Hackett said: “Virgil van Dijk had every right to be annoyed with the referee and his opponent. The referee took no action for what was a very aggressive run and push that met the criteria for being reckless.

“Van Dijk ended up on the floor, so this player is very fortunate not to receive a yellow card. Come on referee, you had a reasonable game, but you could have done better here.

“This could have easily caused a mass confrontation. I am surprised and disappointed that a yellow card was not issued to the Chelsea player.”

Emegha was lucky to avoid a yellow card

Hackett is right – Emegha was fortunate to avoid a yellow card for his shove on Van Dijk, especially considering some of the bookings that England handed out earlier in the match.

For example, Lewis Koumas was harshly cautioned after just 10 minutes for a minor foul on Maxence Lacroix, which was far less of an indiscretion than what the Chelsea substitute did to his fellow Dutchman in the second half.

There was no discernible reason for the Blues attacker to run at the Liverpool captain and barge him to the ground, and as Hackett said, it could easily have led to a large-scale brawl between players from both teams.

We don’t want to see cards being dished out unnecessarily, but Emegha’s petulance would have deserved a booking, certainly more so than Koumas for his foul on Lacroix.

Organizations running regulated and security-sensitive workloads need trusted evidence of which authenticated users performed management operations on their hardware security modules (HSMs), what actions they took, and when. Today, we are announcing Hardware Audit Logging for Azure Cloud HSM, which collects audit records generated by the HSM itself for supported management operations. Security, compliance, and operations teams can use this hardware-originated evidence for monitoring, compliance reporting, and forensic investigations.

Learn more about Azure Cloud HSM

Why hardware-generated audit evidence matters

HSMs protect some of an organization’s most sensitive cryptographic assets. The security of those assets depends not only on how keys are generated and protected, but also on how the HSM is configured and administered over time. Authentication events, user changes, policy updates, backup operations, and key lifecycle activity can all be important when teams investigate an incident, validate operational controls, or prepare evidence for an audit.

Traditional platform and application logs remain valuable, but regulated organizations often want evidence that originates as close as possible to the protected cryptographic boundary. By sourcing audit information directly from the HSM, Azure Cloud HSM provides records tied to the device performing the protected operations. This gives customers an additional layer of evidence for understanding administrative activity and validating the records incorporated into their monitoring and compliance processes.

Monitor HSM management operations with hardware-generated audit logs

Hardware Audit Logging captures a broad range of HSM management activities, including:

  • User authentication and session events.
  • User and administrator management.
  • Policy and configuration changes.
  • Key lifecycle operations.
  • Backup and restore activities.

Together, these events help organizations build a more complete view of how the HSM is being operated. Security teams can examine authentication and administrative activity, compliance teams can use the records as evidence for control assessments, and operations teams can investigate configuration or lifecycle changes that may affect a protected workload.

Independent, end-to-end audit verification

Customers can access human-readable audit records, the corresponding HSM-signed binary audit data, signatures, and HSM certificates needed to independently verify the authenticity and integrity of signed audit batches. This customer-controlled verification path helps connect the records used in monitoring and reporting back to the signed evidence produced by the HSM.

This approach is important because auditability is not only about collecting readable events. It is also about establishing confidence in where the records came from and whether the signed audit batches remain intact.

Maintain consistent audit coverage for supported management activity

Audit logging is enabled as an immutable HSM partition policy. This design helps maintain consistent coverage of supported management activity and reduces the risk that logging could be disabled as part of routine HSM administration. For organizations that separate cryptographic administration, security monitoring, and compliance responsibilities, continuous hardware-originated logging can support clearer oversight across those functions.

The capability is designed to complement an organization’s existing governance model. Customers remain responsible for configuring access to their monitoring environment, defining retention requirements, establishing alerting and review processes, and determining how audit evidence should be used within their control framework. Hardware Audit Logging provides the HSM-originated records and verification materials that those processes can use.

Integrate HSM audit data into security operations

Hardware Audit Logging is designed for organizations with stringent compliance and security requirements, including financial services, payment processing, government, critical infrastructure, and other highly regulated industries. These organizations may need to demonstrate that sensitive cryptographic systems are administered through controlled processes and that important management activities can be reviewed after the fact.

Audit events are delivered to Azure Monitor Log Analytics, enabling customers to incorporate HSM management activity into broader monitoring, investigation, and security operations workflows, including Microsoft Sentinel. Centralizing these events alongside other relevant operational and security data can help teams correlate HSM activity with events elsewhere in the environment, apply queries and analytics, and support established investigation and reporting workflows.

For example, a security operations team may want to review unexpected authentication activity, an administrator change, or a policy update in the context of related Azure activity. A compliance team may need evidence that privileged management operations were logged during a defined assessment period. An incident response team may need to reconstruct the sequence of HSM management events surrounding a security investigation. Hardware-originated audit records provide an important source for each of these scenarios.

Strengthen HSM auditability with verifiable evidence

Azure Cloud HSM provides more than log visibility. Readable audit records support day-to-day operations. It also provides HSM-signed binary evidence, signatures, certificates, and verification utilities behind those records, which lead to an independent way to trace each record back to the HSM that signed it.

It also allows for keeping that evidence outside the logging workflow and checking it cryptographically whenever an audit or investigation calls for it, instead of relying only on delivered text records. Teams get an audit trail they can verify for the workloads that matter most.

What does Azure Cloud HSM Hardware Audit Logging provide

It provides hardware-generated records for supported HSM management operations, sends audit events to Azure Monitor Log Analytics, and gives customers signed binary audit data and verification materials for independent validation.

Azure Cloud HSM Hardware Audit Logging availability

Hardware Audit Logging is rolling out across Azure Cloud HSM public regions and is automatically enabled for newly provisioned Azure Cloud HSM resources where the feature is available. Customers should review current Azure Cloud HSM regional availability and product documentation for the latest availability details and configuration guidance.

Arsenal face a major test of their Premier League title credentials when they travel to Liverpool on Sunday, with Mikel Arteta waiting on several key players ahead of the Anfield clash.

The Gunners will be looking to bounce back after a shock defeat in the Carabao Cup midweek against Fleetwood Town. However, injuries have hampered their preparations ahead of the big clash with four players injured.

Noni Madueke, Declan Rice, Riccardo Calafiori and Christos Tzolis are all in doubt for the game against Liverpool and could affect Arteta’s decision when selecting his team.

Saka absence leaves Arsenal short of attacking options

Saka will miss Sunday’s Premier League fixture because of a quad injury, leaving Arsenal without one of their most important attacking players.

Madueke could provide a timely boost after missing the Fleetwood defeat with an ankle problem. The injury is not believed to be serious, and Arsenal hope the winger will recover in time to face Liverpool.

Arsenal have a disappointing midweek performance to overcome and need to make sure they utilise their attacking options. Both Kai Havertz and Viktor Gyokeres struggled against Fleetwood.

Havertz had a difficult evening; however, Arteta can still include the German in one of the most important fixtures of the season.

Rice and Calafiori fitness concerns

Rice will be another major talking point heading into Arsenal’s visit to Merseyside.

The midfielder was absent for the Fleetwood game after appearing to have hamstring issues in his appearance against Everton last weekend, but there is hope that he can be in the squad on Sunday.

It would give Arteta a real boost in the midfield going up against Liverpool.

Calafiori is also a doubt. He sustained an injury in the latter stages of Arsenal’s win against Everton. The extent of the injury has not been confirmed.

If the Italian fails to recover in time, then Myles Lewis-Skelly could be brought in to play as left back. However, the Gunners will have to see how Calafiori is before they decide what backline they’ll be using.

William Saliba is also out with an injury.

‘Ask him’ – Virgil van Dijk shrugs off bizarre incident with Chelsea player in Liverpool triumph

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Virgil van Dijk has refused to be drawn on an incident involving him and Emmanuel Emegha in Liverpool’s Carabao Cup fourth-round clash against Chelsea on Wednesday night.

The Dutch striker came on for his senior Blues debut midway through the second half at Anfield, and within a few minutes he initiated a bizarre tussle with his compatriot.

After the Reds captain played the ball backwards to Trey Nyoni, the Chelsea substitute charged straight at our number 4 and shoved him to the ground, with the pass having been made at least a second earlier.

Van Dijk shrugs off Emegha incident

Speaking to reporters after the match, Van Dijk was asked about the curious incident with Emegha, and the Liverpool skipper swiftly shut down the question.

He replied (via Liverpool Echo): “Ask him. I don’t know. Maybe he’s trying to get into the national team as well and trying this way. I don’t know. You have to ask him. When he comes by, just ask him.”

Why did Emegha feel the need to shove Van Dijk?

We’re at a loss for an explanation as to why Emegha ran at Van Dijk well after the ball had been played, and he came from enough of a distance to have stopped himself in his tracks and realised that he’d have been better off not getting involved.

What was the Chelsea striker’s motivation to run at his fellow countryman and shove him to the ground? Beats us…

The Liverpool captain seemed momentarily irritated over the bizarre altercation but mostly bemused, just like he was at Malo Gusto’s comical play-acting after making the slightest of contact with the French left-back earlier in the second half.

Van Dijk had the last laugh as it was his team who prevailed on penalties, and although the 35-year-old had been substituted before the shootout, he’d done his bit for the Reds with a solid performance and an important interception to thwart Danny Welbeck.

We eagerly await Emegha’s explanation as to why he went over and accosted the veteran defender for no apparent reason…
