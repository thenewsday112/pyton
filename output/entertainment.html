Reba McEntire Addresses Happy's Place's Dolly Parton Dedication After Death

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Reba McEntire found a way to honor Dolly Parton by dedicating an episode of Happy’s Place to her late friend.

“When we were filming that episode, it was the week when Dolly passed,” McEntire, 71, exclusively told Us about the Friday, October 30, episode of the show. “So I had asked if we could dedicate the show to Dolly, and everybody said yes. That was an absolutely wonderful episode.”

McEntire’s costar — and real-life fiancé, Rex Linn — recalled when they learned about Parton’s death.

“When Dolly passed that night, we had maybe our best live show. Before we were on our way to work, Reba was responding to so many texts. Reba and Dolly were really close, and she got sad in the car, so I said, ‘We got Dolly with us tonight,'” Linn recalled. “That night we had a rock ‘n’ roll show, and Reba even brought it up. She said, ‘We’re going to dedicate this show to Dolly tonight.’ We were running on all cylinders. The crowd was going crazy. So we know that she was with us. It was pretty cool.”

Miley Cyrus and More Stars Mourn Dolly Parton After Her Death at 80

Parton’s rep confirmed her death via a statement in August.

“Global icon and humanitarian, Dolly Parton, known to the world as a beloved singer, songwriter, philanthropist, actress, playwright and author died peacefully today in Nashville, Tennessee,” the statement read. “A rhinestone life that shone bright enough for the world to see, Dolly will forever stand as an inspiration not only through her timeless music and prolific songwriting, but also her wit, warmth and kindness that made us all feel like family.”

The statement continued: “Dolly Parton’s legacy is one of love, compassion and resilience. With a seven-decade career, she inspired multiple generations of artists and fans with her music and an unwavering commitment to making the world a better place. Her songs will continue to resonate with people of all ages, and her philanthropic work will have a lasting impact.”

Parton’s rep later revealed that she died “after bravely facing a brief battle with cancer.”

Dolly Parton Through the Years: Revisit the Country Icon’s Life in Photos

As McEntire mourned her friend, the cast of Happy’s Place was honored to celebrate Parton’s “joy” with their show. McEntire recalled previously finding an opportunity to share the screen with Parton on her Reba sitcom.

“I remember sitting on the couch with her in between takes, and I said, ‘Dolly, are you ever going to retire?’ She said, ‘Where could I find a better job than what I’m getting to do now? No, I don’t see me slowing down,'” the singer recalled. “And she was true to her word. She was working on the day she died.”

Melissa Peterman, who also appeared on Reba, gushed over getting to work with Parton.

“She was so warm. She was up for anything. She came to play and was so generous with her time,” she told Us. “She just took time to make everyone feel so special, and that was just lovely.”

Netflix Cancels Several Shows After Only 1 Season

Prime Video’s Hotel Costiera was another major TV loss. The show, which premiered in September 2025, followed a former Marine Daniel (Jesse Williams), who worked as a fixer at a luxury hotel while also tasked with finding the owner’s daughter.

“I lost 13 pounds shooting this show and I was eating pasta and bread all the time,” Williams exclusively told Us Weekly at the time. “I hear that story time and time again. People come here or Europeans go to America and they immediately get sick and they’re eating organic [food in the United States]. It’s a different world. So I was lucky in that respect here.”

He continued: “I started thinking I was going to have to be super rigorous. But I think exhaustion [from filming] helped me not put on many [pounds]. But also the sad truth is it’s not America. So it’s real ingredients and it doesn’t bloat or inflame you so much.”

Hotel Costiera also allowed Williams to fully immerse himself in Italian culture.

“Language is a big barrier. I learned a little bit of it. But being there a few months in, I was able to get through the first two-thirds of a conversation. And I was able to surprise people in being able to get in and out of expressions and conversations,” he shared with Us. “Moving through Rome — and especially Naples — which I really grew to love, that is a place where it gives you back the love that you give and the respect that you give.”

Williams recalled when he felt more like a local, saying, “There were absolutely moments in restaurants where I am coming across people who know my face and I’ve made some impact on them. But then they hear me talk to them in Italian, which throws them off completely. They embraced me in that way. So I had a really nice welcome.”

Which 'Criminal Minds' Stars Are — And Aren't — Returning After Cast Exit?

Sir Patrick Stewart, an actor equally at home onstage at the Royal Shakespeare Company and on the bridge of the Starship Enterprise, has died at the age of 86. Unexpectedly for most of us, in part because he kept his final illness private, but more so because it once seemed as though nothing could fell the man who looms so large in our cultural consciousness.

The thoughtful, erudite performer who had a troubled childhood in working-class Yorkshire; the committed socialist who was eventually knighted by Queen Elizabeth; the advocate who put his money and his considerable voice toward the fights for LGBTQ+ acceptance and animal welfare and against domestic violence, his presence and performances meant the world (the galaxy, even) to myself and so many others. But those who knew him personally can offer better tributes; perhaps it's best left to Shakespeare's Player King, performed by Stewart himself opposite David Warner's Hamlet back in 1965:

Where joy most revels, grief doth most lament;Grief joys, joy grieves, on slender accident.

Much of Stewart's finest work was on the stage; other performances remain rather shockingly hard to come by (whither I, Claudius?). Nonetheless, he left a true legacy behind him, well beyond Captain Picard and Professor X. Here are 10 of his most iconic other roles.

Jeffrey (1995)

A goofy, feel-good gay rom-com with the HIV/AIDS crisis still very much in the foreground, Jeffrey was a near-impossibility in the mid-1990s: a movie that neither villainized its queers nor treated them as pitiable martyrs. Steven Weber and Michael T. Weiss are the leads, joined by a cast that includes Christine Baranski, Victor Garber, Nathan Lane, Olympia Dukakis, and Sigourney Weaver but, for all those names, Stewart is the undeniable standout as Sterling, the irrepressible gay best friend: a swishing, mincing, sassy, high-camp character who steals every scene without ever becoming a caricature. He's the leader of the "Pink Panthers," a squad of queers who defend locals from gay bashers, and that is the key to the character's power: He's as gay as they come, but not ashamed and not even a little weak. This movie, and the presence of Captain Picard himself in it, might have saved my life back in 1995. Stream Jeffrey on Prime Video, Tubi, and The Roku Channel.

November’s Night Sky Notes: Light-Day Away NASA’s Voyager mission took advantage of a once-every-175-year alignment of the outer planets for a grand tour of the solar system. The twin spacecraft revealed details about Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus and Neptune – using each planet’s gravity to send them on to the next destination. Voyager set the stage for such ambitious orbiter missions as Galileo to Jupiter and Cassini to Saturn. Today both Voyager spacecraft continue to return valuable science from the far reaches of our solar system. NASA/JPL-Caltech

Twin Travelers

In 1977, NASA launched twin spacecraft Voyager 1 and 2, taking advantage of a once-every-175-year planetary alignment of the four largest planets in our solar system: Jupiter, Saturn, Uranus, and Neptune. Using six gravity assists between them, this Grand Tour gave us up-close views of the gas and ice giants, including several moons. By the 1980s, the two probes went their separate ways at Saturn. As Voyager 2 continued toward Uranus and Neptune, Voyager 1 grabbed one more portrait of the solar system (excluding Mercury and Mars), including a final image of the pale blue dot we call home.

The Golden Record Cover NASA/JPL-Caltech

Playlist

“Hello from the children of planet Earth.”

Every good road trip needs some music to match. In this case, each craft carries the Golden Record, a 12-inch phonograph record with spoken greetings in 55 languages, 27 musical tracks, 116 images, and other sounds from Earth. Each record is protected by an outer cover featuring etchings showing our Sun’s location relative to 14 pulsars, along with instructions on how to play it. The design used various metals to protect the information encoded from space radiation: the record used copper and gold plating, and the cover used aluminum. The cover was electroplated with an ultra-pure sample of the isotope uranium-238. Uranium-238 has a half-life of 4.468 billion years.

Are We There Yet?

Did you know that a light-minute is a measure of distance, not time? In August 2012 and November 2018, Voyager 1 and 2 crossed the heliopause, the boundary where the Sun’s solar wind meets the interstellar medium. Using NASA’s Deep Space Network, Earth communicates with both spacecraft and tracks each on its journey. At the time of this article, the Canberra antenna tracked Voyager 1 at 25.80 billion km, or just over 16 billion miles.

This graphic shows the position of NASA’s Voyager 1 and Voyager 2 probes, outside of the heliosphere, a protective bubble created by the Sun that extends well past the orbit of Pluto. NASA/JPL-Caltech

On November 18, 2026, 49 years after its launch, Voyager 1 will cross the one-light-day mark, 16.1 billion miles from the Sun. You may have heard the term light-year in your favorite sci-fi movie or show, or when stargazing and referring to an object outside of our solar system.
