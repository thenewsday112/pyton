Creating AI value with enterprise context

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Across a business, any number of AI platforms, systems, and tools may be at work simultaneously—for example, AI-powered customer platforms, conversational assistants, and workflow and process automation tools. Each one is sophisticated, advanced, and quick to respond. But, when set to work, the outcomes and actions that emerge may be wrong, confused, or lacking a proper understanding of the business. Many organizations find themselves in this situation—and it could be one of key reasons they struggle to extract returns on their AI investments.</p> <figure class="wp-block-image size-large is-resized"><a href="https://atlan.com/resources/mit-report-ai-business-value/?utm_source=mit-technology-review&amp;utm_medium=paid&amp;utm_campaign=mit-report-ai-business-value&amp;utm_content=report-cta" rel=" noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="wp-image-1147797" height="2000" src="https://wp.technologyreview.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/MIT_Atlan-Cover-28-26.png?w=840" style="width: 472px; height: auto;" width="1555" /></a></figure> <div class="wp-block-buttons is-content-justification-left is-layout-flex wp-container-core-buttons-is-layout-b192c3d7 wp-block-buttons-is-layout-flex"> <div class="wp-block-button is-style-fill is-style-fill--1"><a class="wp-block-button__link has-mittr-white-color has-text-color has-background has-custom-font-size wp-element-button" href="https://atlan.com/resources/mit-report-ai-business-value/?utm_source=mit-technology-review&amp;utm_medium=paid&amp;utm_campaign=mit-report-ai-business-value&amp;utm_content=report-cta" rel="noopener" style="border-radius: 4px; background-color: #2026d2; font-size: 16px;" target="_blank">DOWNLOAD THE REPORT</a></div> </div> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">An MIT Technology Review Insights poll finds that while every organization surveyed is using AI today to some degree, fewer than one in five (16%) are extracting transformative value from their deployments. The largest percentage of respondents (40%) say they are seeing moderate gains in specific tasks or teams, while 21% report meaningful gains, leaving 23% that are seeing minimal returns or none at all.</p> <figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><a href="https://atlan.com/resources/mit-report-ai-business-value/?utm_source=mit-technology-review&amp;utm_medium=paid&amp;utm_campaign=mit-report-ai-business-value&amp;utm_content=report-cta" rel=" noopener" target="_blank"><img alt="" class="wp-image-1147799" height="675" src="https://wp.technologyreview.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/MITTR2026_FigureSocials3.png" width="1200" /></a></figure> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Companies are mostly still piloting AI use cases (according to 38% of respondents) or using it to assist employees’ work with tools like co-pilots or chat assistants (35%). Just 12% of enterprises have AI agents in production and 3% say they are using AI to do most of the work in key processes.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">“There’s a big gap between what the models are capable of and how the models are actually being useful inside organizations,” says Prukalpa Sankar, co-founder of Atlan. “For every real-life intent and purpose, artificial general intelligence is already here. A context layer is the architectural infrastructure that sits between an organization’s business systems (for example, systems of record and data, and its AI platforms). It translates enterprise intelligence into governed, machine-usable context for AI agents or models and helps knowledge compound. “Without a context layer for AI, it’s almost like you’re hiring the smartest intern in the world, blindfolding them, and throwing them into your organization,” Sankar says.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong><a href="https://atlan.com/resources/mit-report-ai-business-value/?utm_source=mit-technology-review&amp;utm_medium=paid&amp;utm_campaign=mit-report-ai-business-value&amp;utm_content=report-cta" rel="noopener" target="_blank">Download the report</a></strong></p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>This content was produced by Insights, MIT Technology Review’s custom content arm, not its editorial staff. Systems Thinking Is Vital in the Age of AI

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<img src="https://spectrum.ieee.org/media-library/illustration-of-a-robotic-and-a-human-hand-with-different-color-bands-wrapped-around-both-hands.jpg?id=68528703&amp;width=1245&amp;height=700&amp;coordinates=0%2C125%2C0%2C125" /><br /><br /><p>Modern engineering projects increasingly combine human judgment with machine capability. An engineer can define a problem, then ask an artificial intelligence tool to search relevant research and technical standards, generate design options, test them in simulation, and compare the results. The engineer can then bring the results to specialists who understand the parts AI cannot judge on its own, such as whether a design can be built safely, whether it makes sense in a particular setting, or how it might affect its users. The final decision about whether and how to move forward remains a human responsibility.</p><p>AI can help engineers do early project steps that once required several people with different technical skills. The engineer’s role is therefore expanding from performing each technical step to deciding how people, machines, and specialists’ knowledge should work together on the project.</p><h2>The problem, not the title</h2><p>Engineering has always been divided into disciplines, and for good reasons. Electrical, mechanical, civil, software, and other engineering fields require deep knowledge that takes years to develop. AI does not replace that need. It simply changes how easily engineers can explore the parts of a problem that sit outside their own specialty.</p><p>Consider a software engineer working on a <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/medical-mobile-app-ieee-verified" target="_self">medical device</a>. AI can help the engineer review relevant biomedical research, understand unfamiliar terms, analyze data, and build an early model.</p><p>That does not make the engineer a physician or biomedical expert. It allows the engineer to ask better questions before working with the experts.</p><p>The same is true in <a href="https://ieee-pes.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">energy</a>, transportation, manufacturing, and many other fields. A problem might begin as a software issue and then quickly involve hardware, safety measures, economics, human behavior, and regulations.</p><p>Engineers do not need to master every field; they need to recognize which expertise the problem requires and bring the right people and tools together.</p><h2>The importance of systems thinking</h2><p>Systems thinking thus becomes especially important. It requires looking at the whole problem: understanding how its parts affect one another, identifying constraints and failure points, and making trade-offs.</p><p>AI adds a new element to the process. Engineers must decide not only how the technical parts fit together but also what work to give to AI, what a person must check, when a specialist should make a decision, and when the problem needs to be redefined.</p><p><span>An engineer’s value is not in knowing everything. It is in knowing how to frame the problem, how to ask the right questions, how to test the answers, and when another expert must lead the project.</span></p><p><span></span>This aspect is crucial because technical challenges in engineering are seldom static. As new insights emerge, objectives might shift, unseen limitations can surface, and the initial premise might prove flawed. Rather than rigidly following the starting specifications, an effective engineer adapts and refines the scope as a deeper understanding of the system develops.</p><p>Thus, engineering extends beyond mastering technical concepts; it is an adaptable, structured approach to navigating evolving challenges.</p><p>New evidence can change the goal, reveal a hidden constraint, or show that the original hypothesis was wrong. Effective engineers go beyond merely acting on the initial problem statement. They update it as the system becomes better understood.</p><p>In that sense, engineering is not only a body of technical knowledge, it is also a disciplined way of thinking about a project.</p><h2>Human judgment is important</h2><p>AI can produce convincing answers even when its assumptions are weak. It can generate code that works in one test but fails in another. It can summarize research without understanding which evidence is strongest, or optimize a design for the wrong goal. Such limits make human expertise more important, not less.</p><p>Machines are useful where speed, scale, search, pattern recognition, and data generation matter. People are essential where context, values, responsibility, and judgment matter. The challenge is to decide where each belongs.</p><p>An AI system might generate several designs for a medical sensor, for example, and predict how each will perform. An engineer can test the predictions and reject designs that violate technical requirements. A clinician can determine whether the sensor can be realistically used. Patients and practitioners might reveal problems that neither the engineer nor the developer considered. The final solution emerges from combining the perspectives.</p><p>The engineer is still a builder and specialist but increasingly also a coordinator of knowledge.</p><p>An engineer’s value is not in knowing everything. It is in knowing <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/6-guidelines-governing-ai" target="_self">how to frame the problem,</a> how to ask the right questions, how to test the answers, and when another expert must lead the project.</p><h2>IEEE’s vital role</h2><p>The broader form of engineering creates an important role for IEEE. Its <a href="https://www.ieee.org/communities-connection/societies-councils-and-communities/societies" target="_blank">societies</a> already bring together expertise in various technologies, <a href="https://ea.ieee.org/ea-programs" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">engineering education</a>, <a href="https://standards.ieee.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">technical standards</a>, and other fields. That makes IEEE well placed to help engineers work across specialties without weakening the value of specialization.</p><p>IEEE could create more <a href="https://ieee-collabratec.ieee.org/" rel="noopener noreferrer" target="_blank">communities</a> organized around problems that require expertise from several fields. Examples include <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/medical-mobile-app-ieee-verified" target="_self">trustworthy clinical AI</a>, resilient infrastructure, energy reliability, scientific discovery, and safe autonomous systems. An engineer could begin in a home discipline and then work with specialists from other IEEE communities as a project requires.</p><p>IEEE also can help <a href="https://spectrum.ieee.org/two-new-ai-ethics-certifications" target="_self">set practical standards</a> for human-machine collaboration. Engineers will need clear guidance on when AI-generated work must be checked, how uncertainty should be documented, when a specialist must make a decision, and who is responsible when a system uses both human judgment and machine output.</p><p>Those questions are becoming part of everyday engineering. They should not be left to each company or project manager to answer independently. A professional organization can provide common expectations that follow engineers as they move between jobs, industries, and technical fields.</p><h2>A larger definition of the engineer</h2><p>AI does not make engineering disciplines less important; it makes the connections between them easier to use. Deep expertise gives engineers the foundation to judge what is sound. Systems thinking helps them understand how different parts of a problem fit together. AI can extend engineers’ ability to explore, test, and communicate across fields. Human judgment gives the work direction and responsibility.</p><p>An engineer is not defined only by a job title or a fixed set of tasks. An engineer is someone trained to understand complex systems, define and redefine problems, bring together the knowledge and tools needed to solve them, test whether solutions work, and take responsibility for the outcome.</p><p>That is what it means to be an “unbound” engineer.</p><p>The boundaries between specialties become easier to cross, and machines might take on more of the technical work, but the core of engineering remains human: deciding what problem is worth solving, what evidence is good enough, how the pieces fit together, and what to do when the answer is uncertain.</p>

GitHub Universe 2026: Evolving every layer of the stack

📅 Oct 28, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our jobs as developers are changing. In a couple of years, we&rsquo;ve gone from writing every line of code to working with AI assistants to orchestrating fleets of agents. But the goal hasn&rsquo;t changed: ship completed software work. A feature, a bug fix, a security fix. Completed work, not more code, is the output that matters.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today at <a href="https://github.com/events/universe/recap?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-k2k&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">GitHub Universe</a>, we shared our vision for GitHub as the most comprehensive software delivery system in the world. We introduced products that bring that vision to life and showed how we&rsquo;re rebuilding the infrastructure underneath it.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-our-vision-from-idea-to-completed-software">Our vision: From idea to completed software</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <a href="https://commandline.microsoft.com/new-agentic-software-delivery-primitives/">SDLC is no longer a linear path</a> from planning to coding to testing to deployment. A code change, a request from a colleague, or a production metric can trigger work anywhere in the system. Most of us (and our agents) jump between different stages, depending on the task at hand. Developers set the direction, make decisions, and stay accountable for what ships. Agents implement and verify changes. And everything feeds into and out of a continuous learning machine that makes the next cycle better.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">This agentic software delivery system solves for three things:</p> <ul class="wp-block-list"> <li><strong>Velocity:</strong> get from idea to completed software as quickly, efficiently, and cost-effectively as possible</li> <li><strong>Intelligence:</strong> combine context across the platform with a continuous learning engine, so the system gets better as you use it</li> <li><strong>Quality:</strong> make completed work secure by default and catch bugs before they ship</li> </ul> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">GitHub is built for developers and their agents. We&rsquo;re also making the underlying primitives available, so you can build your own bespoke solutions. You stay in control of the work, the tools, and the cost.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-new-features-and-products-that-move-the-work-forward">New features and products that move the work forward</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Today at Universe, we showed some of the latest products and features in action, starting with the GitHub Copilot app.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-github-copilot-app-from-issue-to-merge-in-one-app">GitHub Copilot app: From issue to merge, in one app</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <a href="https://github.com/features/ai/github-app?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-app-feature&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">GitHub Copilot app</a> is designed from the ground up for agentic software delivery. It connects your agents and tools across projects, so you can direct the work without making every handoff. And it&rsquo;s for more than producing code: you can write specs, present design discussions, or analyze telemetry in one agent-first surface.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-copilot-fusion-multi-model-orchestration-at-its-finest">Copilot Fusion: Multi-model orchestration at its finest</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Different tasks need different models. Developers shouldn&rsquo;t have to manage every model choice to get the right result at the right price. <a href="https://docs.github.com/copilot/concepts/models/copilot-fusion?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-fusion-docs&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">Copilot Fusion</a> evaluates what a task needs across reasoning, code generation, debugging, and tool use, then chooses how to execute across one or more models. In the Artificial Analysis Coding Agent Index, Fusion Max scored within two points of Opus 5.5 Max at half the cost and 40% less time per task.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-copilot-projects-give-copilot-a-goal">Copilot projects: Give Copilot a goal</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Available natively in the GitHub Copilot app starting today, <a href="https://docs.github.com/copilot/how-tos/github-copilot-app/working-with-projects?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-projects-docs&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">Copilot projects</a> let you run agent work across multiple repos in fully managed, parallel sessions. You have one place to track progress, make decisions, and direct what happens next. That means you can spend less time managing execution and more time deciding what&rsquo;s worth building.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-session-continuity-keep-working-across-surfaces">Session continuity: Keep working across surfaces</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Your work shouldn&rsquo;t restart when you switch devices or tools. <a href="https://docs.github.com/copilot/how-tos/copilot-on-github/use-copilot-agents/manage-and-track-agents?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-session-continuity-docs&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026#continue-a-session-from-another-client">Session continuity</a> lets you start and follow the same cloud agent session across the GitHub app, VS Code, the CLI, and the web. Start from your phone at standup, steer it from VS Code at your desk, and check in on it from github.com at lunch. The agent keeps working in the cloud. Our multi-model harness that enables it is powerful, fast, and cost-efficient.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-copilot-automations-learning-from-the-results">Copilot automations: Learning from the results</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://docs.github.com/copilot/concepts/agent-capabilities/agent-powered-workflows-and-operations/about-automations?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-automations-docs&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">Copilot automations</a> let you create AI-powered workflows within your repo. They run in the cloud, and you can kick them off manually or set them up to respond to an event or run on a schedule. That lets you coordinate agents to handle tasks such as issue triage and code review. And now, automations can learn from their outcomes and propose improvements for you to review and approve. That&rsquo;s the learning engine in practice. Use the results of the completed work to make the next run better.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-copilot-code-review-max-a-deeper-look-at-what-ships">Copilot code review Max: A deeper look at what ships</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">More code means more pull requests and more demand for review. Moving faster only helps if we can maintain the quality of what ships. <a href="https://docs.github.com/copilot/concepts/agent-capabilities/agent-powered-workflows-and-operations/code-review?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-code-review-overview-docs&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">Copilot code review</a> surfaces meaningful, actionable bugs that developers need to catch. Today, we introduced Copilot code review Max, which posted the highest F1 score on ReviewBench for high-severity issues. Lite and Balanced keep everyday reviews fast; the new Max provides a deeper look when a change needs it.</p> <h3 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-copilot-features-without-a-separate-enterprise-subscription">Copilot features without a separate enterprise subscription</h3> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Starting today, GitHub Enterprise customers can use all Copilot features without buying a separate subscription. That includes GitHub Copilot app, code review, automations, and more. You pay for the AI credits you use.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">This is available through <a href="https://github.com/enterprise/contact?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-contact-sales-form&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">GitHub Sales</a> today, with self-serve coming soon. Existing Copilot Business and Copilot Enterprise customers can keep their current plans or move over when they&rsquo;re ready. GitHub also remains open and extensible. Keep using the tools your team depends on, including Cloudflare, Datadog, Figma, and more.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-our-continued-infrastructure-investments">Our continued infrastructure investments</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">All of this depends on a foundation that&rsquo;s fast, reliable, and built to scale. Agents are changing the volume and shape of software development. Adding capacity matters, but capacity alone won&rsquo;t get us there. We&rsquo;ve also had to change the architecture.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">So far this year, we&rsquo;ve added:</p> <ul class="wp-block-list"> <li>4 million CPU cores</li> <li>160 PB of high-speed storage</li> <li>50 Tb/s network capacity</li> </ul> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">We&rsquo;re also migrating <a href="https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/the-bridge-to-agent-scale-git-infrastructure/?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-git-infra-blog&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">to a multi-region deployment in Azure</a>, while continuing to serve incredible levels of traffic from developers around the world. We expect to complete the migration early next year. Those investments give us more room to grow. The next step is building the systems that handle the work.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">We&rsquo;ve scaled Git for years as activity on GitHub has grown. The agentic era calls for a different ceiling on throughput and reliability. Three principles guide our new <a href="https://github.blog/engineering/architecture-optimization/building-git-infrastructure-for-agent-scale-development/?utm_source=gh-universe-blog-git-architecture-blog-1&amp;utm_medium=blog&amp;utm_campaign=universe-2026">Git architecture</a>: simplify the primitives, decouple storage from compute, and minimize coordination.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Git&rsquo;s just one example of the work we&rsquo;re doing to scale the platform. We&rsquo;ve done a lot of work across GitHub&rsquo;s AI gateway, the harness, and much more.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-beyond-the-first-1">Beyond the first 1%</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Our jobs are changing, but developers are more important than ever. I think all the software we&rsquo;ve written to date will amount to less than 1% of the software we&rsquo;ll write in the next five years. The opportunity isn&rsquo;t just to produce more code. It&rsquo;s to solve problems we couldn&rsquo;t solve before.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That&rsquo;s why we&rsquo;re connecting the full software delivery system, giving developers better ways to work with agents, and evolving the infrastructure for what comes next. And we&rsquo;re doing it on GitHub, where we can build together.</p> <p>The post <a href="https://github.blog/news-insights/company-news/github-universe-2026-evolving-every-layer-of-the-stack/">GitHub Universe 2026: Evolving every layer of the stack</a> appeared first on <a href="https://github.blog">The GitHub Blog</a>.</p>
