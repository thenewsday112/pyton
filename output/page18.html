Today’s Papers – Halloween derby shivers for Inter and Milan, Spalletti summons Yildiz

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Tonight’s Milan-Inter derby dominates the Italian sports papers, while Juventus go on retreat before tomorrow’s clash with Napoli.

La Gazzetta dello Sport

“100 di questi derby” – “100 of these derbies”

The pink paper calls the Halloween clash at San Siro, which kicks off at 20:45 CET [19:45 GMT], “a historic match, and a spine-chilling one”. It promises a “beautiful duel” between Adrien Rabiot and Nicolò Barella in midfield. Milan risk falling 10 points behind the leaders, while Inter carry a taboo of seven derbies without a win. Rúben Amorim has a message for Cristian Chivu: “I don’t envy you.” The paper also includes a six-page special on a century of derbies at San Siro.

Christian Pulisic has extended his Milan contract until 2031. “I’ll stay here for another five Portuguese coaches,” he joked.

Elsewhere:

Kaká: “I trust Ramos. Just don’t be afraid.”
Iván Zamorano: “It’s Pio’s time. Thuram has transformed.”
Architect Norman Foster on the new San Siro project: “A monumental stadium designed for Milan, inspired by the Galleria.”

Corriere dello Sport

“La paura del Diavolo” – “The Devil’s fear”

The Rome paper argues it’s Milan who risk everything tonight, as they drop 10 points behind if Amorim loses to Chivu. Chivu goes with Marcus Thuram and Pio Esposito up front, while Pulisic is billed as the “anti-Inter”. Gerry Cardinale warmed up for the game by saying: “I dream of the Scudetto and I won’t touch the coach.” Chivu lost both derbies last season, and the paper quotes: “This time we’re more provincial.”

Juventus “must only win” against Napoli tomorrow, with Luciano Spalletti under pressure. The squad has been on retreat at the JHotel since yesterday. Juve are expected to stay in a 3-5-2 with Nick Woltemade and Randal Kolo Muani. Gleison Bremer is a doubt, so Pierre Kalulu could move into the back three. Andrea Cambiaso is out, with Juan Cabal on the left.

Massimiliano Allegri sticks with the 4-3-3 for “attacking Napoli”. Scott McTominay is still looking for his first goal against Juventus, with 31 in 88 games for the Partenopei. Alex Meret returns in goal, Amir Rrahmani is favoured over Luca Marianucci, and the front three is Matteo Politano, Rasmus Højlund and Noa Lang.

Roma visit Udinese at 18:00 CET, and the paper says “Gasp knows how to hurt them with Malen”. Donyell Malen has scored seven times from just 12 shots on target, with an xG of 6.6, and has 25 goals in 31 games for the Giallorossi. Raffaele Palladino says “the positive phase must be ridden” before Bologna host Monza.

Tuttosport

“Brividerby” – “Shiver-derby”

The Turin paper calls Milan-Inter a “frightening” game on Halloween night. Amorim sees “the ghost of -10” but raises his sights: “We’re still aiming for the Scudetto. Zero envy of Chivu.” On Inter’s seven-derby drought, the paper quotes: “We haven’t won in seven derbies? We need to ask ourselves some questions.” Pulisic says, “There’s no greater emotion,” and Thuram has a lesson for Spence: “This game is madness.”

“Lucio precetta anche Yildiz” – “Lucio summons Yildiz too”

Juventus are united against the crisis. Spalletti has called every player to the JHotel retreat, including the injured Kenan Yildiz, Manuel Locatelli and Khéphren Thuram. Cambiaso is out for Napoli, Jeff Ekhator is back in group training, and a switch to 4-3-3 is not ruled out. Former striker Fernando Llorente said: “Conte is great, but changing won’t help. The problem isn’t the coach: they lack quality Italians and real champions.”

Ignazio Abate wants a battling Torino at Frosinone tomorrow: “Toro, get your hands dirty, don’t admire yourselves in the mirror. It’s like walking in the mountains: you mustn’t stop to admire the view.” Roma fly to Udine and then straight on to Manchester, with “Gasp all in on Malen”.

Andoni Iraola would not be drawn on Jamie Carragher’s claim that his Liverpool side should be considered Arsenal’s nearest challengers in this season’s Premier League.

Liverpool have bounced back impressively from their 3-0 defeat to Manchester City earlier this month, winning five straight games in all competitions.

The Reds impressively beat third-place Brighton last weekend and can now look to further close the gap on the top two when they host defending champions Arsenal at Anfield.

Writing in The Telegraph, Carragher asserted that Liverpool could rubber-stamp their title credentials with a win against the Gunners, particularly given the uncertainty surrounding City after their guilty verdict.

But Iraola suggested it was too early to engage with that kind of title talk and said his team still needed to ‘improve’.

‘I think it is too early to start looking at the standings,’ Iraola said in his pre-match press conference. ‘It is about preparing ourselves for a very difficult game.

‘We know Arsenal and how good they are. They have very good players, working a lot in synchronicity and we have to be ready for that challenge.

Liverpool boss Andoni Iraola (Picture: Getty)

‘It’s going to be a demanding game but we also trust our players, trust ourselves and in the good atmosphere we will for sure have on Sunday, I hope we will be at level to win this game.

‘It is still October,’ he continued. ‘We have to take one game at a time and I always say the reference is more or less when we’ve play against each other once so halfway through the season. That’s when you can start to look at the standings.

‘Right now, we are in a position where wneed to improve and keep getting better.

‘For fans, it is good if they have expectations or they think we can win. Normally that’s a good thing but if you ask me if I think it is still very early in the season.’

Liverpool will hope to be boosted by the return of Dominik Szoboszlai and Víctor Munoz for Sunday’s game as they continue their recoveries from hamstring and knee injuries, respectively.

‘I think they have a chance,’ Iraola added. ‘But I don’t know for sure if they will be involved or not. We still have two days, so I think they have a chance, yes.’

“Increased to 85,000 capacity” claim for London Stadium – Report | West Ham News

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

In a week during which the future of West Ham’s home hit the media again thanks to the owners desperately seeking a way out of their financial cut de sac, some pretty spectacular claims were made about the former Olympic Stadium’s ability to host huge events in the future.

West Ham fans, used to enormous queues and delays just to get inside will find the jaw-dropping suggestion that a further twenty thousand people could be crammed into the arena pretty implausible.

Yes, it coped -somehow- aided by a veritable army of volunteers and impeccably behaved ‘olympic-going’ families, with 80,000 in 2012. But they weren’t all trying to arrive at exactly the same time, were they.

Add the temporary nature of the upper tiers and the constant leaks, queues for everything and the long, post-event shuffle back to Stratford station, unlit and with the hated walking traffic lights halting progress as the rain falls whilst the misery becomes abject, and you have a sporting venue wholly unfit even for its current 62,500 capacity.

London Stadium queues: Try doing it at night, in the rain and the dark. along with another 25,000 fans. No thanks.

Yet 85,000 is the claim. According to sportscape magazine:

“London Stadium Aims for 85,000-People Boxing Spectacles to Generate Income Post-Relegation..aiming to offset £19M losses and drive £15M local economic boost.”

“…By making a formal request to the Newham Council to have boxing bouts categorised as “other leisure events” so as to facilitate the increase in the maximum capacity of the event from 60,000 to 85,000.”

Clutching. At. Straws.

You’d hope the licensing authority will see sense and deny the venue permission. Anyone who’s tried to get to, and from the stadium on a match day will understand why adding another 20,000 – presumably standing on the floor of the arena – but with the same access arrangements, the same shortage of toilets and concessions, will understand the unsuitability of the arena and it’s dreadful surrounds for further expansion.

It needs knocking down and rebuilding, West Ham fans will all tell you that. But with the Hammers’ 99 year lease, that is never going to happen. So further ‘ideas’ to try and turn a profit will surely keep failing thanks to the poor commercial design, the very temporary nature of the upper parts of the structure and the facility- free wasteland that surrounds the ground.

85,000? Well, I won’t be one of them. Not even if you paid me.

Jeff Ekhator has finally rejoined his Juventus teammates on the training pitch. The young striker, signed from Genoa in the summer, took part in a full group session at the Continassa on Friday for the first time since a run of muscle problems that has so far kept him out of every competitive game in black and white.

His return gives Luciano Spalletti one more name to consider in a week when the squad has been kept together at the training centre ahead of Sunday’s visit of Napoli.

A frustrating start in Turin

Juventus announced the permanent deal on 1 July, and Football Italia put the fee at €16m plus add ons. The 19 year old has barely been available since.

According to Gianluca Di Marzio, who credits Sky Sport Italia with the news, his troubles began in the very first friendly of pre-season against Basel. He recovered in time to be named in the squad for the second Serie A round against Parma, where he stayed on the bench, but broke down again a few days later and has spent roughly two further months on the sidelines.

Di Marzio describes the forward as fully recovered, and Friday’s session with the rest of the group was his first since that setback.

Where he fits in Spalletti’s attack

Nobody should expect Ekhator to be rushed into the starting eleven after so long without match practice. Football Italia sees him more as an extra option from the bench, giving Spalletti a third centre forward alongside Nick Woltemade and Randal Kolo Muani.

That alone matters for a side that has struggled for goals. Juventus have scored 11 times in nine league games, and the 2-0 defeat at Genoa on Wednesday, covered in our report on Spalletti’s apology, left them level on 14 points with Napoli.

A tense weekend at the Continassa

The timing of his comeback is notable. After the Genoa loss, chief executive Giovanni Carnevali met the players and the coach, and Spalletti then decided to keep the squad together at the training ground until kick off, as explained in our piece on the Juventus retreat.

Napoli come to the Allianz Stadium on Sunday 1 November for Matchday 10, with kick off at 20:45 local time. According to the club’s own match notes, it is the first time since April 2021 that the two sides meet on the same number of points in Serie A.

Whether Ekhator makes the matchday squad will become clear when Spalletti names his group. Either way, having a fit young striker back in training is a rare piece of good news for Juventus this week.
