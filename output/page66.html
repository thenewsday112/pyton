Chelsea could make another major investment in their attacking options as they assess potential additions ahead of the January transfer window.

With the Blues continuing to build a squad capable of competing for major honours, Barcelona midfielder Fermín López has reportedly emerged as a player of interest.

The 23-year-old Spain international has attracted attention from several European clubs, with Paris Saint-Germain also considering a move.

Chelsea previously attempted to sign López in 2025, and fresh reports suggest the London club could revisit their interest if the opportunity arises.

Chelsea Transfer News: Blues Consider Major Fermín López Bid

According to CaughtOffside, Chelsea and PSG are both prepared to make significant offers for López, who remains an important part of Barcelona’s plans under Hansi Flick.

A source with links to the agents industry told CaughtOffside: “PSG are preparing a serious offer worth around €80million for Fermin Lopez,” before adding: “Chelsea are also considering a massive bid.”

The reported interest could set up a transfer battle between the Premier League side and the French champions. However, neither club has confirmed a formal bid, and any deal would depend on Barcelona’s position and the player’s willingness to leave his boyhood club.

Chelsea are familiar with López, having reportedly made a significant offer for him in the summer of 2025. The midfielder opted to remain at Barcelona, but the latest reports suggest the Blues could be willing to return if his circumstances change.

López’s ability to operate as an attacking midfielder, contribute goals and create chances makes him an appealing option. His performances have also strengthened his reputation as a versatile attacking talent capable of influencing games from advanced positions.

Competition for places at Barcelona could be one factor to monitor, although there is no confirmation that López is actively seeking an exit. CaughtOffside reports that Chelsea and PSG currently have the most concrete interest, while Arsenal and Manchester United have also made enquiries about his situation.

Barcelona, meanwhile, would reportedly prefer to retain the midfielder but could face pressure to consider substantial offers amid their financial challenges. López signed a contract extension in February 2026, keeping him at the club until 2031, meaning negotiations would likely require a significant financial commitment.

Chelsea may also have other priorities for January. The report identifies Bournemouth midfielder Alex Scott as a potential focus for the Blues, meaning any pursuit of López could depend on how their wider transfer plans develop.

“Being talked about…”: Everton plotting January move for “interesting” England international

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Everton could look to make a major statement in the January transfer window as David Moyes considers ways to strengthen his side’s attacking options.

After concerns over the team’s lack of goals, the Toffees are reportedly exploring a move for a forward with proven Premier League experience.

Manchester United’s Marcus Rashford has emerged as a potential target, with reports suggesting Everton are discussing the possibility of bringing the England international to Merseyside.

A deal would give Moyes another versatile attacking option, although several key details would need to be resolved before a transfer could progress.

Toffees Discuss Marcus Rashford Move

According to Football Insider, former Everton chief executive Keith Wyness believes a move for Rashford is being discussed seriously ahead of the January window.

Speaking on Football Insider’s Inside Track podcast, Wyness said: “Yes, this is definitely being talked about and quite strongly. And I think that could be an interesting solution.”

The former Everton chief executive also pointed to Rashford’s previous loan spells and suggested that a move closer to Manchester could benefit the player.

“Rashford did well when he was on loan at Villa and at Barcelona as well. I think it could work, and I think being close to where he is in Manchester, just along the M63, could also benefit him greatly as well,” Wyness explained.

The comments suggest that a January switch is a possibility worth monitoring, although neither club has confirmed an agreement. Separate reporting from FootballTransfers has also indicated that Everton and Manchester United have held early discussions over a potential winter move.

Wyness drew comparisons with Jack Grealish, who has made an impact at Everton when available, adding: “We’ve seen Jack (Grealish) moving across that way as well, and when he’s fit, Jack is certainly a big asset for Everton. So I think Rashford would be a very interesting signing, and I’m hearing there’s a lot of smoke here; it’s definitely being discussed.”

He then added: “I wouldn’t be surprised to see something happening in January.”

For Everton, Rashford’s versatility could be a major attraction. Although he has often operated from the left, he can also play centrally, potentially giving Moyes additional options across the frontline. His arrival could provide support for Thierno Barry and help address the team’s need for more goals.

However, the financial structure of any deal would be important. Everton would need to consider Rashford’s wages, Manchester United’s valuation and whether a permanent transfer or loan arrangement would be realistic. The forward’s willingness to join another Premier League club would also be a significant factor.

What This Means for Everton

Rashford could represent an ambitious addition for Everton if the club can find an agreement that works financially. His Premier League experience and ability to operate in several attacking positions would give Moyes greater flexibility, while a change of environment could offer the player a fresh start.

The comparison with Grealish is particularly relevant to Everton’s approach. The club could look to provide Rashford with an environment in which he can rebuild his confidence and contribute more consistently, although any potential success would depend on his fitness, form and role within the team.

Nevertheless, a transfer is far from guaranteed. The reported discussions remain at an early stage, and Everton must still resolve the financial details and establish whether Rashford would be open to the move.

For now, Wyness’s comments add weight to speculation surrounding Everton’s January plans. If negotiations progress, Rashford could become a name to watch as Moyes looks to strengthen his attacking options for the second half of the season.

Giorgi Mamardashvili says Liverpool staff got one thing ‘wrong’ before penalty shootout

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Giorgi Mamardashvili has said that the information given to him about Chelsea’s penalty takers prior to Liverpool’s Carabao Cup shootout win over the Blues was erroneous.

With the teams level at 1-1 at the end of normal time, the tie would be settled on spot kicks, and the Reds prevailed 6-5 after sudden death to advance to a home quarter-final against Bournemouth.

At the end of the shootout, the home players flocked to the Georgian goalkeeper, whose save from Emmanuel Emegha proved the turning point as the Dutchman had the opportunity to win it for the visitors with his kick.

Mamardashvili: Chelsea penalty notes were wrong

It was the only one of Chelsea’s eight penalties that Mamardashvili actually saved (Valentin Barco struck the post and Wesley Fofana skied his into the Kop), and the 26-year-old joked afterwards that his notes on the Blues’ penalty takers weren’t of much use to him.

Speaking to ITV after the match, Liverpool’s number 25 said: “I had this paper as well, but everything was wrong!”, adding that the information provided to him by club staff wasn’t helpful during the shootout.

On the save from Emegha, the goalkeeper explained: “Before the shot I was looking at his [standing] leg, where he puts it down. I decided to go to my right side. I wanted to save more but sometimes one is enough.”

Mamardashvili saved the one which mattered the most

Perhaps Mamardashvili was disappointed with his save ratio from the eight Chelsea penalties – excluding shootouts, he’s saved 10 out of 29 faced in his career – but he came good for Liverpool in the one moment that they needed him most (the save from Emegha).

The Georgian guessed the right direction on five of the Blues’ spot kicks, and while he was given no chance with Levi Colwill’s powerful strike, he won’t be happy to have let Morgan Rogers’ spot kick squirm under his dive.

The 26-year-old also did his bit to ensure that his team made it to the penalty shootout, pulling off smart reaction saves from Rogers and Marco Palestra in the second half, and he’ll be satisfied with his overall performance whilst standing in for Alisson Becker.

Mamardashvili will probably have to wait until the quarter-final against Bournemouth in mid-December to make his next Liverpool appearancve, unless his Brazilian counterpart is rendered unavailable in the meantime.

However, the towering Georgia international showed last night that he’s capable of stepping up and delivering when the Reds need him. If he’s involved in another penalty shootout later in the season, hopefully the notes given to him will be more accurate this time!

Arsenal Max Dowman Red Card Appeal Faces Rejection, Says Dermot Gallagher

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Former Premier League official Dermot Gallagher has said he thinks Arsenal won’t be successful in their appeal against Max Dowman’s red card in their Carabao Cup loss to Fleetwood Town.

The 16-year-old Arsenal winger was shown a red card for violent conduct after lashing out at Harrison Neal after a strong challenge from the Fleetwood defender. The Gunners then lost the fourth-round tie on penalties.

Arsenal are appealing the sending off of Max Dowman. They need to have their paperwork in by 1pm today (Thursday). They will be hoping to get a decision before their Premier League game against Liverpool on Sunday.

But Gallagher does not think they will be successful in their appeal as he believes there was clear intent from Dowman.

“I think it was a red card for the actions of the teenager,” Gallagher said.

He also thinks the referee should have given Arsenal a free kick as well as booking Neal for his challenge on Dowman.

Dermot Gallagher Explains Why Arsenal’s Appeal Could Fail

Gallagher told Sky Sports News that the attitude of Dowman could be an issue for Arsenal in their red card appeal.

“I think he went out of his way to kick his leg up. It makes it difficult to overturn the decision,” Gallagher said.

“He’s got more of an aggressive reaction than what Cole Palmer had recently with Mateus Fernandes.”

But Gallagher was critical of the challenge made by Neal on the Arsenal youngster. He said that while Fleetwood’s defender did win the ball back, he didn’t think the challenge was made with enough consideration for Dowman’s safety.

“I think he should have got a free-kick and a yellow card,” he added.

The debate leaves Arsenal facing a difficult decision ahead of their Premier League fixture against Liverpool. If the appeal fails, Dowman’s dismissal will stand.

Paul Merson Questions Max Dowman’s Red Card

Sky Sports pundit Paul Merson said he disagreed with Gallagher’s comments, saying it was a harsh red card.

“It was a classic old fashioned tackle. He (Neal) wins the ball and then follows through. I think the reaction from the players might have had something to do with the referee giving Dowman a red card.”

Merson also warned the young Gunner that he will always be a target for opposition players.

Dowman has had disciplinary problems in the past, being sent off whilst playing for England against the USA. Merson feels the youngster needs to learn to control his emotions and not get caught up in the taunts of opposition players.

The incident has also raised more questions over how Arsenal are developing one of their most promising young talents.

Mikel Arteta Faces Questions Over Max Dowman Reaction

Arsenal manager Mikel Arteta also attracted criticism for his reaction to Dowman after the Carabao Cup defeat to Fleetwood Town.

As the teenager walked towards the tunnel, Arteta leaned towards him and spoke to him. Merson questioned the approach, arguing that the Arsenal manager should have offered reassurance to the 16-year-old instead.

Arteta declined to reveal exactly what he told Dowman, explaining only that they had discussed the matter before the match.

The Arsenal boss also said the team had prepared for the emotional challenges they expected to face during the tie.

Jobi McAnuff similarly questioned Arteta’s response, arguing that Dowman remains young and will inevitably make mistakes as he develops.

Arsenal must now wait for the outcome of their red card appeal. With Liverpool next on the fixture list, the decision could have important implications for Arteta’s selection plans.

‘Honestly…’ – Andoni Iraola gives candid reaction to drawing Bournemouth in Carabao Cup quarters

Andoni Iraola has admitted that he’d rather not have seen Liverpool drawn against Bournemouth in the quarter-finals of the Carabao Cup.

Both teams came through their fourth-round ties on Wednesday night, with the Reds defeating Chelsea on penalties and the Cherries seeing off Aston Villa, and they were paired together in the draw for the last eight.

It means that the Basque will come up against his former club at least twice more this season, having already returned to the Vitality Stadium in September when the Merseysiders triumphed 1-0.

Iraola wanted to avoid Bournemouth

With the quarter-final draw being made shortly after the match at Anfield ended last night, Iraola was asked for his reaction at Liverpool being paired with Bournemouth.

The 44-year-old replied (via liverpoolfc.com): “Honestly, I was talking before the draw and I said, ‘The only team I don’t want is Bournemouth…’ For me, it’s special. They know me very well, they are playing very well lately, in a good moment, but it is what it is.

“We haven’t been very lucky with the draws – we have played Spurs and Chelsea already and now Bournemouth. That is going to be obviously special for me, but we have to earn the right to be in the semi-finals and I hope we can do it here at home again.”

Opportunity knocks for Iraola

We urgently need to monitor AI agents’ actions in real time

📅 Oct 29, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

This summer, hundreds of OpenAI agents tried to cheat on a cybersecurity test. They broke out of their test environment and successfully hacked Hugging Face, a site that they believed held clues about how they’d be graded. Around the same time, another OpenAI agent accessed private files from an Australian government website. It’s a problem that’s by no means confined to OpenAI. In the months since, Anthropic, Google, and Meta have also disclosed incidents in which their agents have gone off track. And worryingly, in many cases the agents eluded the very researchers and safety teams tasked with monitoring them. 

AI agents are taking unauthorized actions, and the companies building and running them are mostly relying on luck and hindsight to mitigate any harmful impacts. They must do more to ensure safety. That’s why EU regulators and US agencies are rightfully calling for agent developers and deployers to monitor agents in real time. But these rules assume that companies can see what their agents are doing and why—when in reality, they lack sufficient visibility. 

Imagine a bank hiring a thousand new employees who can access customer accounts and sensitive information, but the employees’ ID badges change every day, it’s not clear whether a manager ever reviewed or authorized their work, and nobody logs the changes employees make to approval processes. That lack of visibility is where we are with agents today.

As agents roll out across critical sectors, from finance to government to medicine, we must be able to monitor them in real time to prevent wide-ranging and potentially devastating consequences.  And while safety depends on monitoring, monitoring requires observability: the capacity to capture what the system did, process it into signals, and present those to humans or automated oversight systems. In other words, we need to know what the agent did and why it did it. And as our recent research shows, current agent frameworks—the software that runs agents— do not reliably generate the crucial observability records we need to ensure that agents are deployed safely.

Agents present new kinds of problems that generative AI alone does not. They inherit the unpredictability of the LLMs they are built on, but where a generative AI system produces a single output for a human to act on, an agent performs longer tasks—it plans, picks tools, spawns sub-agents, and takes actions. So an agent’s course can take shape in a multitude of different ways, and no tests before launch can fully anticipate the risks. This makes observing the agents while they work even more challenging, and even more important.

With colleagues from academia, civil society, and industry, we studied the four most widely used agent frameworks to see what signals they actually emit. We found major gaps that made it hard to achieve key safety goals, like noticing policy violations, unauthorized actions, and even human oversight failures. Key observability requirements were missing—like Agent IDs that persist over time, and records about which humans approved agent tasks. This made it hard to tell which agents did what or assess whether they were taking unauthorized actions. 

The good news is that these gaps, once identified, can be closed. Doing so starts with capturing two types of records: those that reveal what happened in an agent’s run and how the agent made its decisions. The “what happened” records identify the agent (while preserving privacy) and log how humans intervened and whether agent permissions or “memory” shifted, while the “decision” records show an agent’s reasoning and levels of confidence in the actions it’s taken.

Take, for example, agents working to process a bank’s mortgage loans. The system examines an application and recommends denying it, and then a human loan officer signs off on the decision. In a later audit, or perhaps even in real time, a bank or regulator needs to be able to reconstruct which agent made the call, which officer approved it, and how long they took. And the bank must also analyze records of how the agent arrived at its refusal decision in order to ensure it did not use off-limits information, like details about the neighborhood the applicant is from.

Recording “what happened” and “how the agent decided” require different approaches, but both are feasible. The current lack of “what happened” records is mostly a software plumbing problem that can be addressed through OpenTelemetry, an open observability standard maintained by engineers across the industry and by the agent frameworks adopting it.

OpenTelemetry has launched a working group drafting shared formats for tracing agents, but they have yet to be finalized. The group should urgently incorporate standards for maintaining persistent agent identity and for recording human interventions and changes to agents’ permission or memory. And it should be done before these systems can scale, all the while attending to the privacy implications of agent ID that links to real people.

Preserving “how agents decided” records is more challenging. The issue raises open research questions for model providers and researchers: Does the step-by-step reasoning a model displays, known as chain of thought, reflect how the model actually made a decision? And do we have reliable ways to measure how confident the agent is in that decision? These questions need answers before anyone sets standards for how such observability records are used.

No single organization or sector can close the observability gaps on their own, but together, they can support the infrastructure required to prevent dangerous incidents and support safety. 

AI developers building agent frameworks, like OpenAI, Anthropic, CrewAI, and LangChain, must integrate telemetry signals recording an agent’s persistent identity, any human interventions, and any changes to an agent’s permissions and memory. Meanwhile, enterprises adopting these agent frameworks have an influential role to play; enterprises in regulated industries, like banks and hospitals, can put pressure on the agent developers to supply these missing observability records where standards do not yet exist.

Enterprises must also define policies, permissions, and roles that set out what agents can safely do, since telemetry alone cannot assess whether agents broke specific rules. Finally, regulators and AI standards institutes must specify the observability records required for high-stakes AI deployments while leaving the technical details to industry. We have already seen initial momentum here: NIST’s agent standards initiative in the US is working on agent identity, one of the most salient gaps we found in our research. 

No bank would let a thousand unbadged employees loose on customer accounts and hope to piece together what happened when something goes wrong. Yet that’s how we’re deploying AI agents. Making their behavior observable is the first step toward fixing this. And by building the regulatory and policy ecosystem that makes it possible to act on what we see, we can hold organizations accountable. Until then, every task we hand to an agent hands over our agency, too.

Madhulika Srikumar is the Head of the AI Safety Program at the Partnership on AI, a global non-profit partnership of leading industry, academic, and civil society organizations, advancing best practices in AI governance.

Claire Leibowicz is the Director of AI, Trust, and Society at the Partnership on AI and a doctoral candidate at Oxford University.
