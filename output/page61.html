Max Dowman suspension leaves Arsenal facing another selection headache after appeal rejection

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

The Football Association has rejected Arsenal’s appeal against Max Dowman’s red card at Fleetwood Town, meaning the 16-year-old will serve a three-match suspension.

Dowman was sent off during Tuesday’s Carabao Cup defeat, leaving Mikel Arteta with another attacking selection headache. Arsenal are already without several wide players through injury.

In the 85th minute of the match, Dowman received a red card for responding to a heavy challenge by Fleetwood’s Harrison Neal. Although he did not make a full kicking motion, his foot made contact with Neal.

The decision was a costly one for Arsenal, who were already struggling in the game against their League Two opponents. They lost the game 1-1 and then saw Fleetwood win the penalty shootout 5-3.

They lodged an appeal on Wednesday but have now been turned down, meaning Dowman will miss the next three games.

Arteta loses another attacking option as Arsenal prepare for Liverpool

Dowman’s absence adds to Arsenal’s current problems in the wide areas.

That is three injured players with Bukayo Saka, Noni Madueke and Christos Tzolis all sidelined. It means there aren’t many attacking options for Arteta to choose from.

But they have a big game coming up on Sunday when they go to Liverpool. Arteta will need to decide who he is going to play.

Dowman had another chance to shine in the game against Fleetwood. However, he only started for the second time this season. It was somewhat of a controversial night for the teenager.

This is the second time Dowman has received a red card in a short space of time having been sent off while playing for England Under-19s earlier this month.

Second red card raises questions over Dowman’s discipline

The latest dismissal has also shone a light on how Arsenal deal with one of their brightest young stars.

As Dowman made his way to the touchline, Arteta looked frustrated. He gave a serious look to the young teen and appeared to say something in anger as he exited the pitch.

This suggests it was something that had been discussed prior to the game. Arteta later said that the coaching staff had spoken about the emotional demands of the game during their preparations.

The Arsenal manager said the players had received clear instructions about what they needed to respect and control emotionally.

The dismissal was due to violent conduct. The laws of football state that a red card can be shown if a player uses or attempts to use excessive force or brutality on an opponent, outside of a challenge for the ball.

This does not necessarily mean there has to be physical contact. In this case there was contact between Dowman and Neal.

The teen will miss the next three matches as a result of his ban. The focus will be on getting Arsenal ready for their Liverpool match with a lack of attacking options at their disposal.

For Dowman, it is important to avoid any further disciplinary issues.

He has made a few senior appearances already, thanks to his talent, but avoiding disqualification is also necessary for his growth as a player.

Eating disorders are on the rise. GLP-1s could make things worse.

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are one of the biggest biotech breakthroughs of the last decade. But to some clinical psychologists, they can represent something more sinister—a potential trigger for eating disorders.

A new report suggests that the global incidence of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa shot up between 1990 and 2023—by a whopping 71%. By 2023, there were an estimated 18.1 million people living with one of these two disorders.

What’s worse is that the number is likely to be much higher than that once we factor in other eating disorders. And, disturbingly, those in the field are already starting to see GLP-1 weight-loss drugs exacerbating the issue.

Cheri Levinson is a clinical psychologist at the University of Louisville in Kentucky who has specialized in eating disorders for almost 20 years. She says she’s now seeing more patients with more severe symptoms than at any other time in her career, and that many of them are misusing GLP-1s. “This is the worst I’ve seen things,” she says.

Eating disorders are mental health conditions in which people’s eating behaviors impact their health. They include anorexia nervosa (defined by a fear of weight gain), bulimia nervosa (in which people binge and purge), binge eating disorder, and others.

It’s thought that some people have a genetic predisposition to certain eating disorders, but that some external factor is what triggers their onset. That might be stress, or social pressure to be skinny. Often, simply restricting food intake can trigger an eating disorder, says Levinson.

Eating disorders—along with the pressure to look thin—were already prevalent in the 1990s (the heroin chic era springs to mind). To find out how things might have changed since then, Ruhai Bai at the Children’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University in China and his colleagues pulled together data from studies and surveys conducted on populations around the world. The team used statistical models to fill in any data gaps.

That research allowed the researchers to estimate the annual incidence of two eating disorders—anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—for each of 204 countries and territories between 1990 and 2023. They also estimated the impact those disorders had on global health by calculating their disability-adjusted life years (one DALY represents a year of healthy life lost).

The findings, published in JAMA Psychiatry on Wednesday, are unsettling. Between 1990 and 2023, there was a 71% increase in the incidence of those two eating disorders, the team found. Those conditions had a huge impact on global health, too. In 2023, they “cost the world the equivalent of about 3.83 million years of healthy life,” says Bai.

Bai and his colleagues expect these figures to continue to rise. In 2023, an estimated 18.1 million people had either anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa; by 2050, the team predict, the two conditions could affect 21.8 million people.

It gets worse. Those two conditions represent less than half of all eating disorders, says Levinson, who was not involved in Bai’s study. The true impact of these disorders is “probably three times the rate that they’re reporting,” she says.

Bai and his colleagues didn’t explore the reasons for this rise (although Bai points out that it can’t be entirely explained by population growth). So how did we get here?

Social media may have played a role. “Social media comparisons, influencers, challenges, diet trends can all be precipitating … events for an eating disorder,” says Cynthia Bulik, a clinical psychologist at the University of North Carolina, who was not involved in the new study. Bulik has been studying and treating eating disorders since the early 1980s. “Between 1990 and 2023 there has clearly been an upsurge of those types of environmental exposures,” she says.

Many social media users are exposed to filtered and modified images of skinny influencers who may have undergone cosmetic surgery or extreme dieting and who are promoting “thinspiration” or “fitspiration” content. But users with eating disorders appear to be more likely to engage with this content—and to be fed more of it as a result.

When researchers at the University of Melbourne investigated this phenomenon a couple of years ago, they found that the algorithms of TikTok users with eating disorders delivered 4343% more “toxic eating disorder videos” than those of users who didn’t have the disorders. They also got more content focused on dieting or exercising. Many were fed over 1000 problematic videos per month. Bulik describes social media as “quicksand” for people with, or at risk of, eating disorders.

The covid-19 pandemic—and the social isolation that accompanied lockdowns—also appears to have contributed to the rise in eating disorders. Research conducted in 2021 found that the number of people hospitalized with eating disorders in the US doubled between 2018 and 2020.

Researchers don’t yet have good data on how trends in eating disorders might have progressed since 2023. But anecdotally, things seem to be getting even worse. “There are more eating disorders, more severe eating disorders… and we’re seeing earlier ages of onset,” says Levinson. “We’re seeing eight- and nine-year-olds with eating disorders.”

“From what I’m hearing and seeing, I am not going to be surprised if that number isn’t at least doubling again,” she adds.

Levinson particularly worries about the impact of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, which have exploded in popularity in recent years. A Gallup survey conducted earlier this year suggested that 15% of Americans had used one of these drugs for weight loss. And they’re becoming more accessible—drug companies are working on new formulations that are both easier to take and more effective, and many people can buy versions of these drugs from compound pharmacies or dubious online sources.

In a small study published earlier this year, Levinson and her colleagues found that 30% of people with eating disorders had used GLP-1 drugs. The drugs might benefit some of those people. But 10% appeared to have misused the drugs. And Levinson thinks that figure is also an underestimate.

Levinson says she and her colleagues have seen children develop eating disorders after being prescribed GLP-1s for medical reasons. They’ve seen others—even children as young as 11—buy versions of these drugs over TikTok.

“We’re very concerned that GLPs will lead to even faster and more extreme weight loss in people with anorexia nervosa, and that patients will come to us in even more advanced stages of illness that become difficult to reverse,” says Bulik.

Both Bulik and Levinson are continuing to study the impacts of these drugs in eating disorders. In the meantime, both urge medical professionals to consider the risks GLP-1s might pose to people with those disorders. Bulik contributed to a set of research and clinical guidelines, published earlier this month, that recommends doctors screen for eating disorders before prescribing the drugs, for example. The guidelines also recommend more patient education and more robust monitoring of people who take GLP-1s.

If you think you or someone you know might have an eating disorder, you can find information and support at the National Eating Disorders Association.

Rush Street Interactive kept its 2026 revenue guidance but said October trading points to the low end of the range, after raising its outlook a quarter earlier. With online casino at 75% of revenue, the warning is fresh operator evidence that US online casino growth is cooling heading into the fourth quarter.

The third-quarter results of BetRivers owner Rush Street Interactive came out on October 28. The guidance range it reaffirmed implies 38% to 41% growth for 2026. Adjusted EBITDA guidance stays at $245 million to $265 million, with October tracking toward the midpoint of that range.

The warning breaks a pattern from the summer. In the second quarter, reported July 29, the company posted record revenue of $393.8 million, up 46%, and raised its full-year guidance. Third-quarter sales were up from $277.9 million a year earlier but below the second-quarter total. Growth also slowed from the 46% pace of the prior quarter.

Profits outpaced revenue as casino share kept rising

Net income rose 93% to $28.7 million, from $14.8 million. Adjusted EBITDA rose 73% to $62.2 million, from $36.0 million. Sales and marketing expense grew 43% to $56.0 million, from $39.0 million a year earlier.

The online casino share of revenue rose from 72% in the second quarter. That weighting makes the company a close read on US casino demand. The 32% growth in casino revenue also includes Latin America, so it is not a pure US figure.

Player numbers kept growing. North American monthly active users reached a record 300,572, up 34% year over year. Latin American users rose 46% to 607,308, for a total of 907,881.

Management blames a slower market, not lost share

Management blamed slower growth in the North American online casino market, not lost market share, for revenue tracking toward the low end of guidance. Michigan, in their view, has also become more competitive. Statewide figures from Michigan are consistent with a softer market. In September, Michigan iGaming adjusted gross receipts came to $288.15 million, 0.9% lower than in August.

The shares fell 1.82% in regular trading on October 28 to $15.13. After hours, the stock moved below its 52-week low. The company bought back about $14 million of stock during the quarter and another $11 million after it ended. That leaves $75 million under its repurchase authorization.

Carnevali calls Juventus situation ‘not acceptable’ as squad enters retreat before Napoli

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

Juventus will stay together in a training retreat at the JTC Continassa from Friday 30 October until Sunday's game against Napoli, after a blunt meeting between Giovanni Carnevali, the players and Luciano Spalletti. That home fixture is now widely seen as decisive for the coach's future and the direction of the season.

A direct meeting with chief executive Giovanni Carnevali has put the Juventus players and their coach under heavy pressure before Napoli’s visit.

The trigger was the loss in Genoa. Kingsley Ehizibue struck in the 12th minute, and Tommaso Baldanzi added a second in the 28th. Spalletti apologised to the supporters afterwards. He said his side had been beaten in every area. He added that they had to raise their physical level and fighting spirit.

Carnevali backs the staff but demands results

The squad travelled back by train overnight. The players went to the Continassa the next morning. Management spoke to them there before training. Carnevali reminded them of their responsibilities to the owners, the club and the fans. He said the coaching staff still had the club’s trust, Adnkronos reported. He also asked for a strong and concrete reaction on the pitch. With the club’s agreement, Spalletti then chose to keep the players together at the training centre until kick-off.

Clubs rarely call this kind of retreat. It shows how serious the mood is at the Continassa. The message from the top is twofold. Public backing for the coaching staff comes paired with pressure for results. That places heavy weight on a single fixture.

Identical records hide a gap in front of goal

Both sides have four wins, two draws and three defeats from nine games, Corriere dello Sport reported. Napoli sit seventh and Juventus eighth.

The gap lies in front of goal. Napoli have scored 17 times. Juventus have managed 11. Juventus have conceded only 10, however. Those figures are consistent with a side whose problems lie more in attack than in defence. A reaction against Napoli would need goals as well as fighting spirit.

Napoli will be without their coach. Massimiliano Allegri’s two-match ban covers their league games against Monza and Juventus. Serie A sporting judge Gerardo Mastrandrea also fined him 10,000 euros. The sanction followed his abuse of match officials and later behaviour in the changing rooms during Napoli’s 3-3 draw with Roma. Napoli first announced an appeal. Allegri later said he had decided to drop it. That leaves Juventus facing a Napoli side without Allegri in charge at a moment when their own coach is under pressure.

The last meeting between the sides in Turin, on 25 January 2026, ended in a 3-0 Juventus win at the Allianz Stadium.

Juventus host Napoli at the Allianz Stadium on Sunday 1 November 2026, with kick-off at 20:45.

Three academy players scored their first goals for Athletic Club's senior side inside four minutes as Edin Terzić's team beat Prat 6-1 in the Copa del Rey. The late burst came from substitutes in a side rotated with the Basque derby in mind.

On Thursday 29 October, Athletic met Prat in the Copa del Rey round of 128. The tie was played at the Estadi La Feixa Llarga in L’Hospitalet de Llobregat. Its closing stages belonged to Athletic’s academy.

Each of the late scorers had come off the bench. Selton Sánchez finished the night with the sixth goal, teed up by Vizcay, whose own strike had started the flurry.

Aritz Conde earned an 8.2 rating from the data provider for his half-hour on the pitch. Imga, aged just 17, had made his first-team debut alongside Vizcay against Getafe the previous Sunday.

A rotated line-up had the tie settled by half-time

EFE reported that Athletic picked a starting XI shaped by the coming Basque derby, giving chances to players such as Yohaneko, Sannadi and Nico Serrano. Those changes did not slow the visitors.

Maroan Sannadi opened the scoring in the 29th minute. Robert Navarro doubled the lead two minutes later. Mikel Jauregizar provided both assists. Gustavo Reinke pulled one back for Prat in the 33rd minute, set up by Álex Plaza. Serrano restored the two-goal cushion three minutes later from a Jesús Areso pass.

The statistics show how one-sided the night was. Athletic had 68% of the ball and 20 shots from inside the box. They put 12 attempts on target, against 2 from the hosts. Prat’s goalkeeper was kept busy with six saves. Navarro earned the top rating of 9.2, with seven key passes in 86 minutes.

Miguel Ángel Motoso’s Prat came into the tie after beating San Rafael 5-1 and 5-0 in their two previous matches. The draw carried an echo for Athletic. Their run to their most recent Copa title began on 1 November 2023 against another Liga Élite Catalana side, about 22 km from L’Hospitalet.
