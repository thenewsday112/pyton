Oklahoma sweepstakes ban takes effect November 1, putting suppliers and affiliates at felony risk

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

<div><img alt="Oklahoma City canal with murals, a footbridge and downtown towers" class="attachment-medium size-medium wp-post-image" height="205" src="https://readwrite.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/oklahoma-city-canal-skyline-300x205.jpg" style="width: 100%; height: 68.38%; margin-bottom: 15px;" width="300" /></div><p class="usp-standfirst"><em>Oklahoma&#039;s SB 1589 makes running or supporting a dual-currency sweepstakes casino a Class C2 felony from November 1, after lawmakers overrode the governor&#039;s veto. Its reach into payment, geolocation and affiliate businesses raises the legal stakes for the whole sweepstakes supply chain.</em></p> <p>Oklahoma&#8217;s sweepstakes casino ban starts on November 1, and operators are not waiting for the date. In recent days, brands such as High 5, McLuck, Hello Millions, SpinBlitz, PlayFame, Zula, Sportzino and Fortune Wins have either left the state or blocked its players.</p> <p>The law&#8217;s reach down the supply chain sets it apart. Liability extends beyond operators to suppliers, payment processors, geolocation providers and affiliates. Tokens and similar currencies used in dual-currency models count as &#8220;representatives of value.&#8221; Tribal gaming approved under the federal Indian Gaming Regulatory Act is exempt.</p> <h2><span id="lawmakers_overrode_governor_stitt_to_pass_the_ban">Lawmakers overrode Governor Stitt to pass the ban</span></h2> <p>Senator Todd Gollihare authored SB 1589. It had its first Senate reading on February 2, 2026. On March 25, 2026, Representative Kyle Hilbert replaced Representative Fetgatter as its principal House author. The measure changes the scope of an existing gambling offense in the state&#8217;s crimes and punishments code.</p> <p>Stitt&#8217;s veto letter objected that the bill needlessly created a new felony. He also opposed extending criminal liability to businesses and service providers. In his veto message, he argued that Oklahoma&#8217;s gaming laws should be clear, targeted and fair. SB 1589, he said, did not meet that standard.</p> <p>The legislature disagreed. In the Senate, Gollihare moved that the bill become law despite the veto. The motion passed on a roll-call vote. The House then passed the measure over the veto by two-thirds of its elected members. That threshold is set by Article VI, Section 11 of the state constitution.</p> <p>The provisions the governor objected to are now the ones that matter most for the industry. Businesses that serve sweepstakes brands, from payment firms to geolocation vendors, now face felony exposure in the state. That raises the legal risk across the sector, not just for the brands themselves.</p> <p>Players face a different problem. The rules on remaining balances and pending redemptions are unclear. Oklahoma has no regulated online casino. As a result, legal online play in the state now goes only to tribal gaming.</p> <p><!--cr:social--></p> <div class="cr-social cr-social-x" style="margin: 1.6em 0; display: flex;"> <blockquote class="twitter-tweet"> <p dir="ltr" lang="en">BREAKING: The Oklahoma Legislature overrode Governor Stitt's veto of the bill banning dual-currency sweepstakes casinos. Pennsylvania has folded a sweepstakes ban into a broader gaming bill, HB 2801. Each violation by a sweepstakes operator could draw a fine from regulators if that bill passes. Pending bills in Pennsylvania do not carry over into 2027.</p> <p>Wyoming&#8217;s draft would ban online casino and sweepstakes games under the state&#8217;s professional gambling law. The Select Committee on Gaming sent draft bill 27LSO-0098 to the Legislature&#8217;s Management Council. The council will decide whether to sponsor it in the 2027 session. Digital office communication affects stress, creativity, and perceived tone

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<figure class="wp-block-embed is-type-video is-provider-youtube wp-block-embed-youtube wp-embed-aspect-16-9 wp-has-aspect-ratio"><div class="wp-block-embed__wrapper"> </div></figure> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Office messages used to move on paper.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">People used to type, copy, and file them by hand, but now they arrive in seconds via <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/email-phrases-annoy-everyone/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">email</a>, Slack, Microsoft Teams, <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/most-people-misjudge-their-own-text-messaging-habits/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">text</a>, and more. Trading these messages can feel like a <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/what-makes-a-conversation-engaging-its-not-what-you-think/">conversation</a>, but that feeling misleads people because the writer hears a tone of voice the reader often does not.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nicholas Epley, a behavioral scientist at the <a href="https://www.uchicago.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">University of Chicago</a>, described the problem when email was a new, fast tool.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;But what&#8217;s different in this medium is…the ease with which we can fire things back and forth. It makes text-based communication seem more informal and more like face-to-face communication than it really is,&#8221; said Epley.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Researchers in <a href="https://cas.okstate.edu/psychology" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">psychology</a>, <a href="https://business.vanderbilt.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">management</a>, and <a href="https://www.vanderbilt.edu/communications/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">communication</a> at universities in the United States have since tested the changes new tools bring to work.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most of their studies are small. Together, they cover how well tone comes through on a screen, how stressed workers feel, and how teams come up with ideas when they aren&#8217;t in the same room.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-memos-were-built-for-filing">Memos were built for filing</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">JoAnne Yates, a management professor emerita at <a href="https://www.mit.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">MIT</a> <a href="https://mitsloan.mit.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Sloan School of Management</a>, studied how American companies built their first internal communication systems. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Her book Control Through Communication follows typewriters, carbon copies, and filing systems as they changed business writing in the late 1800s and early 1900s.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cabinets that sorted papers by subject worked best for documents covering one subject each. The internal memo grew up alongside them, a one-topic document a company could copy, file, and find again.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Speed came much later. Yates worked with Wanda Orlikowski of MIT and Melissa Mazmanian of the <a href="https://uci.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">University of California, Irvine</a>, on a study of professionals who carried mobile email devices. At first, the devices gave those workers flexibility and peace of mind.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Mobile email also raised everyone&#8217;s expectations for how quickly someone should respond. The team wrote that professionals chose to work &#8220;anywhere, anytime,&#8221; but in practice they ended up working &#8220;everywhere, all the time.&#8221;</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-tone-gets-lost-in-text">Tone gets lost in text</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Epley and psychologist Justin Kruger tested how well tone travels in an experiment with 30 pairs of college students. Each student received a list of 20 short statements and gave each one a sarcastic or a serious tone.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">One student in each pair read the statements into a tape recorder, and the other emailed them. Both groups expected their partners to read the tone right about 78% of the time.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Listeners got it right about 75% of the time. Email readers managed 56%, not much better than a coin flip.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The voice messages came close to what the senders hoped, and the emails fell well short. Still, readers and listeners alike guessed they judged the tone correctly 90% of the time.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Kruger and Epley blamed egocentrism, the difficulty people have seeing things from someone else&#8217;s point of view. A writer hears the intended tone, but the reader sees only words.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;E-mail is fine if you just want to communicate content, but not any emotional material,&#8221; said Epley.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Short replies now carry signals of their own. &nbsp;In a <a href="https://www.binghamton.edu/index.php" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Binghamton University</a> study led by psychologist Celia Klin, 126 undergraduates rated one-word replies such as &#8220;Okay&#8221; and &#8220;Sure.&#8221; A period made those replies seem less sincere in a text message, but not in a handwritten note.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-email-turned-into-a-stress-signal">Email turned into a stress signal</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Stephen Barley and Debra Meyerson of <a href="https://www..stanford.edu" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Stanford University</a> and Stine Grodal of <a href="https://www.bu.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Boston University</a> studied workers at high-tech companies. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Engineers, technical writers and marketers logged every phone call, email and face-to-face conversation over a series of workdays. The team <a href="https://www.researchgate.net/publication/220520979_E-Mail_as_a_Source_and_Symbol_of_Stress" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">reported in <em>Organization Science</em></a> that people who spent more time handling email felt more overloaded.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In later interviews, Grodal noted that participants spent about 90% of their discussion on email. Questions about phone calls and meetings drew little emotional response.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;You can actually see it on the screen; it has material properties. And so email becomes your to-do list of the day,&#8221; said Grodal.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gloria Mark, an informatics professor at the University of California, Irvine, tested the opposite. Her team cut off email for 13 civilian employees at an Army research center outside Boston for five workdays. Heart rate monitors and computer software tracked the workers the whole time.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With email, the workers switched computer windows about 37 times an hour. Without it, they switched about 18 times, and Mark&#8217;s team used that switching to measure <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/multitasking-can-make-the-brain-blur-useful-and-irrelevant-information/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">multitasking</a>. Their heart rates also varied more during the email break, which the researchers read as a sign of lower stress.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-video-narrows-people-s-focus">Video narrows people&#8217;s focus</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">As more people started <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/working-from-home-changes-your-daily-habits-in-ways-you-dont-notice/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">working from home</a>, many meetings moved onto video. Melanie Brucks of <a href="https://business.columbia.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Columbia Business School</a> and Jonathan Levav of Stanford University tested what that does to <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/ai-boosts-brainstorming-but-may-slow-the-creative-process/">brainstorming</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The researchers split 602 people into pairs and gave each pair five minutes to list creative uses for a Frisbee or bubble wrap. Some pairs sat together in a rooms, and others worked over video. The video pairs came up with <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-022-04643-y" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">fewer ideas</a>, and fewer creative ones.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Eye tracking pointed to a reason. People on video spent more time looking at their partner and less time looking around the room. They also remembered fewer of the props the researchers had set out, such as a large houseplant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The same pattern appeared among 1,490 engineers at company sites in five countries, although video didn&#8217;t hurt the next step: picking the best idea.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;When you&#8217;re visually narrowing yourself… you&#8217;re putting blinders on, basically,&#8221; said Brucks.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Video can also wear people out. In a Stanford survey of more than 10,000 people, about 13.8% of women and 5.5% of men said video calls left them very to extremely tired. The team found that the subjects seeing their own faces on screen for hours helped explain that gap.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-off-hours-email-raises-anxiety">Off-hours email raises anxiety</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The pressure doesn&#8217;t end when the workday ends. William Becker of Virginia Tech, Liuba Belkin of <a href="https://www2.lehigh.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Lehigh University</a> and Samantha Conroy of <a href="https://www.colostate.edu/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Colorado State University</a> surveyed full-time employees and their partners about checking email <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/0149206319890655">outside work hours</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Workers felt more anxious when employers expected them to monitor email at night, even if the workers spent little time actually working, and their partners reported worse health and less relationship satisfaction. After-hours email is a source of <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/job-stress-is-quietly-disrupting-sleep-for-millions-of-workers/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">job stress</a> that permeates the whole household.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;If the nature of a job requires email availability, such expectations should be stated formally as a part of job responsibilities,&#8221; said Becker.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most of this research predates Slack and Teams becoming everyday tools. The tone experiment used email between college students, and the punctuation study used text messages, so these studies didn&#8217;t test workplace chat apps directly.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Grodal has said her team needs to return to workplaces and test changes such as letting workers skip after-hours replies. For now, those changes remain untested in the field.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The full study was published in the <a href="https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-3514.89.6.925" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><em>Journal of Personality and Social Psychology</em></a>.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-to-get-involved">How to get involved</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Read the <a href="https://centerforplainlanguage.org/learning-training/tools-training/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Center for Plain Language&#8217;s free guides</a> if you write messages to coworkers. &nbsp;After the General Services Administration redirected the government&#8217;s plain language website in 2025, the nonprofit reposted the Federal Plain Language Guidelines</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Explore the <a href="https://www.cdc.gov/niosh/twh/programs" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">NIOSH Total Worker Health</a> program if you help shape workplace policy. The federal program works to make jobs safe and to support workers&#8217; health and well-being.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Follow the <a href="https://www.siop.org/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Society for Industrial and Organizational Psychology</a> for research behind workplace practices. Its members study how people work, and the society shares that research with policymakers.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Try the <a href="https://www.nationaldayofunplugging.com/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">National Day of Unplugging</a> each March.
