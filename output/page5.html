While mud-coring in Baffin Bay, scientists accidentally found the marks of a mile-thick ice shelf that blocked three massive ice sheets

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Science  |  Read Source →

In July 2023, the research vessel Neil Armstrong left Woods Hole, Massachusetts, for Baffin Bay. The scientists on board planned to pull mud cores from the seafloor between Greenland and Canada.

On the way, the ship’s sonar picked up something else. Marks on the slopes of the bay point to a floating ice shelf more than a mile thick, according to the University of Florida. It filled the basin about 20,000 years ago.

The results came out on October 21 in Nature Geoscience. The thickness is the team’s interpretation of features on the seafloor, not a direct measurement of the ice.

Woods Hole to Nuuk in 33 days

The cruise, called AR76-01, ran from July 11 to August 13, 2023, and ended in Nuuk, Greenland. That is according to the official cruise report. It was part of the Baffin Bay Deglacial Experiment, or BADEX, funded by the U.S. National Science Foundation.

Robert Hatfield, an associate professor of geology at the University of Florida, was chief scientist. His co-chiefs were Shannon Klotsko of the University of North Carolina Wilmington (UNCW) and Brendan Reilly of Columbia University.

The team wanted to know whether warmer water at the base of glaciers made the Greenland Ice Sheet retreat after the last ice age. In 33 days the ship worked 27 stations and made 109 deployments. The crew brought up about 1,300 feet (396 meters) of sediment.

The report thanks Captain Kent Sheasley and his mates for their skill “in a challenging sea-ice environment.” In Melville Bugt, a bay off northwest Greenland, the ship worked beside a large ice floe. The scientists wrote that their survey lines there came out like spaghetti.

Eggland on the Upernavik slope

Two Kongsberg multibeam sonars mapped the seafloor as the ship moved. The deeper unit, an EM122, worked in water from about 1,600 to 10,800 feet (500 to 3,300 meters) deep.

Off the Upernavik trough, the sonar showed an abrupt change. Smooth seafloor in deep water gave way to a rough, hummocky surface higher up the slope. In cross section it looked a little like a basket of eggs, and people on board called it “eggland.”

Farther south, off Uummannaq, the team logged a similar boundary. It sat at about 4,330 feet (1,320 meters) of water in the north of the survey and 4,130 feet (1,260 meters) in the south.

The 2023 report does not link these features to an ice shelf, and neither the abstract nor the university release mentions eggland.

A short documentary follows the BADEX science team and crew aboard the R/V Neil Armstrong during the 2023 coring cruise in Baffin Bay. Credit: The Baffin Bay Deglacial Experiment.

Grounded ice a mile down

Back on land, the team compiled their new maps with older depth surveys of Baffin Bay. In the paper’s abstract, the authors say they found signs of ice resting on the continental slope during the last glacial period. They traced the deepest line it reached around most of the bay.

The authors read those features as the work of one ice shelf across the whole basin. After a correction for past sea level, they put its thickness at about 4,600 to 6,900 feet (1,400 to 2,100 meters). They estimated its area at about 58,000 square miles (150,000 square kilometers).

Top left, a perspective view of streamlined lineations and the ice contact boundary on the Baffin Bay slope; bottom left, a seismic profile across that boundary. On the right, conceptual diagrams of how the ice shelf and its keels shaped the slope (not to scale). Credit: Klotsko et al., Nature Geoscience (2026). Click image to enlarge.

According to Hatfield, the depth of the evidence surprised the team most. It lay more than a mile below the sea surface. He said that makes the shelf at least half a mile thicker than any ice shelf known before in the Northern Hemisphere.

“Ice doesn’t want to flow down underwater, it wants to be afloat,” Hatfield said. “It just speaks to how thick that ice shelf became.”

Buttresses for Greenland and Canada

Global ice volume peaked between about 26,500 and 19,000 years ago, and three ice sheets met in Baffin Bay, according to the abstract. The Greenland Ice Sheet lay to the east and the Innuitian Ice Sheet to the north. The Laurentide Ice Sheet lay to the west.

Left, the proposed ice conditions in Baffin Bay at the last glacial maximum, with the Greenland, Innuitian and Laurentide ice sheets around a floating ice shelf. Top right, the estimated shelf thickness at its grounding line about 19,000 years ago; bottom right, its area compared at scale with modern Antarctic ice shelves. Credit: Klotsko et al., Nature Geoscience (2026). Click image to enlarge.

The team argues that the ice shelf held all three in place and helped them grow thicker. Hatfield compared the ice sheets to “a gothic cathedral.” In his picture, floating shelves are the flying buttresses that slow the flow of land ice into the ocean.

In West Antarctica, researchers have warned that the ice shelf holding back Thwaites Glacier could break up soon. Elsewhere on the continent, ice shelves are cracking under the weight of meltwater on their surface.

In the Arctic, the shelves left today are small. On the north coast of Canada’s Ellesmere Island, an ice island the size of Bermuda broke off the Ward Hunt Ice Shelf in August 2010.

Gone within 500 to 1,000 years

Sediment cores from across the bay suggest that the ice shelf disappeared over roughly 500 to 1,000 years, the university said. After that, glaciers on land flowed faster into the sea and melted.

The release says the loss helped unlock parts of the three ice sheets. They contributed to the hundreds of feet of sea level rise that set today’s coastlines. The abstract calls the shelf an analog for how weaker buttresses could speed up ice streams in Antarctica.

Hatfield said scientists who model ice shelves and glaciers now have new data to work with. Mud from beneath Greenland has shown that its ice vanished about 416,000 years ago. Seafloor cores show that the West Antarctic Ice Sheet has collapsed many times.

Dates for the breakup still approximate

The window of 500 to 1,000 years comes from the University of Florida release, not from the abstract. The release ties it to sediment cores from across the bay but does not say which cores set it or how they were dated.

Lauren Lewright and Jacqueline Austermann of Columbia University ran the glacial isostatic adjustment analyses, the sea-level modeling behind the corrected thickness, according to the paper’s author contributions. The authors present the shelf as their reading of ice-contact features on the slope.

Earlier work came up with a thinner shelf. In 2022, Pierre-Olivier Couette of Université Laval and colleagues studied a moraine system off Baffin Island.

In Communications Earth & Environment, they argued that an ice shelf about 1,640 feet (500 meters) thick covered northern Baffin Bay.

The BADEX team has posted its mapped landforms and ice-contact points in the Marine Geoscience Data System. The raw multibeam data from the 2023 cruise sit in the Rolling Deck to Repository.

After the cruise, the mud cores from Melville Bugt and the other stations went to a repository at Oregon State University. “We didn’t go out to look for an ice shelf this big,” Hatfield said.

The full study was published in the journal Nature Geoscience.

Cardinale reiterates Amorim backing before derby but warns: “I will be crushed if we don’t win”

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

By: Isak Möller

Gerry Cardinale has reiterated his complete backing for Ruben Amorim ahead of AC Milan’s derby against Inter, but also made it clear that he will be very disappointed if they don’t win. 

An important game is on the cards for Milan this evening, hoping to bounce back from their Bologna defeat with what would be a massive derby win. Success would bring them within four points of Inter, while a defeat would increase the gap to ten points.

Cardinale, the Milan owner, will be present at the stands in San Siro after also attending the games against Udinese and Bologna. Last night, he hosted a dinner for many Milan legends and explained his project for the club to them.

Cardinale’s post-dinner thoughts

After the dinner, Cardinale stopped to talk to the journalist Carlo Pellegatti outside the restaurant (see the video at the bottom). Among other things, he reiterated his backing for Amorim and also made it clear that he really wants to win.

What is your feeling about this evening?

“Look, it was a fantastic night. We had people from Milan getting together in support of AC Milan. And that’s all I could have hoped for. It’s a big day tomorrow, a big game, as you know…”

Are emotions ramping up ahead of the game?

“My job is not to increase the emotion. My job is to keep everything steady and focused, providing the leadership so that we know what we need to do. We need to win tomorrow [today, editor’s note], not just for the first 45 minutes but also the next 45 minutes.

“It’s football, it’s sport, and the great thing about sport is that you never know. But my job is to make sure that we have a steady hand to do the best that we can do and maximise our chance of winning.

“Tonight was a really amazing event. We had legends of AC Milan who know how to win, and that means so much to me, that they came back and bought into what we do. We are bringing this passion and love of winning back.

“Let’s see how we do tomorrow. It’s a long season, but it’s the derby and it’s our home derby, so I want the win more than anything. I will be crushed if we don’t win tomorrow.”

You gave Amorim heavy backing…

“Listen, we have to come together. If we want to win, it has to be everybody. The fans, us, the press, anybody who supports AC Milan. Let’s come together and all make this possible. Ruben is doing a great job, I made the right decision on Ruben.

“I watched him today at Milanello and he really cares about his training, his managing, his coaching, and his mentoring. I met with every single one of our players and they want to win. It’s only a matter of time.

“You either want to win or you don’t want to win. You can’t fake that. I want to win, the players want to win and Amorim wants to win. I’m telling you, that I got right. Now, football is up and down, but I promise you that they want to win.”
