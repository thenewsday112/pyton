NWSL announces roster-building mechanisms for 2028 expansion teams

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Two expansion teams will play their inaugural National Women’s Soccer League seasons in 2028: Atlanta City FC and the to-be-named Columbus club. With the teams able to start building their rosters in 2027, the league announced Friday the mechanisms they can use to build their inaugural rosters, since they got rid of the expansion draft ahead of Boston Legacy FC and Denver Summit FC joining the league.

The options available to Atlanta and Columbus are relatively the same as the mechanisms Boston and Denver had but adjusted for the consistently increasing salary cap.

Each expansion club will have access to $1,430,000 in allocation money, roughly 30% of the 2028 base salary cap according to the announcement. Allocation money must be funded by the clubs and may be used to reduce salary cap charges, pay transfer and loan fees, and be used in trades with all other clubs. Unused, funded allocation money will remain available through December 31, 2030, including any funds subsequently traded to another club.

Comparatively, Denver and Boston had access to the same percentage of allocation money, but 50% of the funds were provided by the league and the rest funded by the teams at their discretion. They have until December 31, 2027, to use all of their funded allocation money. Allocation money purchased before 2024 via the mechanism’s original iteration will be phased out by the end of 2026, so it now appears that allocation money will live on as a unicorn available to expansion teams in the short term.

Atlanta and Columbus will also have access to an increased transfer fee threshold of $1.9 million over two years, compared to the threshold of $665,500 for non-expansion clubs in 2027. Starting on the first day of 2027 and through the conclusion of the 2028 secondary transfer window, the expansion teams have $1,167,950 available, then an additional $732,050 during the 2028 calendar year. Spending above the applicable threshold will incur a salary cap charge equal to 25 percent of the excess amount, same as the rest of the league.

For a more in-depth explanation of allocation money and transfer fee thresholds, check out this explainer from Jeff Kassouf.

In order to allow the expansion clubs to start signing players in 2027 ahead of their inaugural seasons, they will be subject to a limited salary cap of $628,320, equivalent to 14.28 percent of the 2027 base salary cap. High Impact Player (HIP) rule spending is not available until January 1, 2028.

The previous pair of expansion teams had a 6-month period during which they could sign players without being subject to the salary cap as long as they had a net-zero salary cap impact by being: loaned out to a team that picks up the full cost of salary and bonuses; qualified to be placed on SEI; or allocation money was used to cover the salary cap charge. Then they had a limited salary cap of $250,00 after the second transfer window opened.

Once again, expansion clubs will not be required to meet the league’s minimum roster size requirements in 2027 but must remain within the maximum roster size. So the players signed in 2027 can keep playing expansion clubs may loan them to clubs within or outside the NWSL. A new wrinkle for intra-league loans during 2027: the expansion clubs are permitted to retain up to 25% of the salary cap charge. Standard loan accounting rules and required roster compliance will resume in 2028.

2026 has been brutal when it comes to our favorite shows getting canceled.

Netflix cut ties with Boots two months after the show premiered. Based on Greg Cope White‘s memoir, the scripted series followed Cameron (Miles Heizer) as he joined the U.S. Marine Corps alongside his best friend Ray (Liam Oh) during the 1990s, the era of the military’s “don’t ask, don’t tell” policy regarding LGBTQ+ service members.

Cameron was forced to hide his sexuality while trying to make it through boot camp alongside Slovacek (Kieron Moore), Hicks (Angus O’Brien), Nash (Dominic Goodman), John (Blake Burt), Cody (Brandon Tyler Moore) and Ochoa (Johnathan Nieves).

Before being canceled, Boots found success at the network — doubling its weekly audience and rising to No. 2 on Netflix’s TV charts.

Netflix Cancels Several Shows After Only 1 Season

Prime Video’s Hotel Costiera was another major TV loss. The show, which premiered in September 2025, followed a former Marine Daniel (Jesse Williams), who worked as a fixer at a luxury hotel while also tasked with finding the owner’s daughter.

“I lost 13 pounds shooting this show and I was eating pasta and bread all the time,” Williams exclusively told Us Weekly at the time. “I hear that story time and time again. People come here or Europeans go to America and they immediately get sick and they’re eating organic [food in the United States]. It’s a different world. So I was lucky in that respect here.”

He continued: “I started thinking I was going to have to be super rigorous. But I think exhaustion [from filming] helped me not put on many [pounds]. But also the sad truth is it’s not America. So it’s real ingredients and it doesn’t bloat or inflame you so much.”

Hotel Costiera also allowed Williams to fully immerse himself in Italian culture.

“Language is a big barrier. I learned a little bit of it. But being there a few months in, I was able to get through the first two-thirds of a conversation. And I was able to surprise people in being able to get in and out of expressions and conversations,” he shared with Us. “Moving through Rome — and especially Naples — which I really grew to love, that is a place where it gives you back the love that you give and the respect that you give.”

Williams recalled when he felt more like a local, saying, “There were absolutely moments in restaurants where I am coming across people who know my face and I’ve made some impact on them. But then they hear me talk to them in Italian, which throws them off completely. They embraced me in that way. So I had a really nice welcome.”

‘Criminal Minds: Evolution’ Cast Breaks Silence on Show Ending After Season 20

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Entertainment  |  Read Source →

Fans will have to say goodbye to the BAU once again after season 20 of Criminal Minds: Evolution.

“This has happened. It’s being canceled. Then it says right there, it’s ending for now,” Kirsten Vangsness, who has played Penelope Garcia throughout the show’s 20 seasons, said via an Instagram video on Friday, October 30, responding to news that the series is coming to an end after its 2027 season.

“You know that part in Charlie and the Chocolate Factory where Charlie gets the chocolate and it’s like a little bittersweet, because he’s like, ‘Thanks so much for the chocolate,’” the actress, 54, explained, noting she is feeling similar emotions.

“Look how long, that doesn’t happen ever. But I love my job and my boss Erica Messer says there’s so many more stories to tell. So, if we get to keep telling them somewhere, that would be amazing,” Vangsness continued. “I know all of us feel the same way. … Everything’s OK. Aren’t we all in a death and rebirth cycle? Yes, so maybe this goes somewhere else? What an adventure.”

Which 'Criminal Minds' Stars Are — And Aren't — Returning After Cast Exit?

Us confirmed on Friday that Criminal Minds’ upcoming season — its 20th overall and fifth with the subtitle Evolution — will mark the franchise’s final season on Paramount+.

“It’s hard to put into words what Criminal Minds has meant to all of us who have been fortunate enough to be part of this extraordinary journey, first on CBS for 15 seasons and then on Paramount+ for five,” showrunner and executive producer Messer, 52, said in a statement on Friday. “While suddenly navigating the end of this chapter, I’m filled with gratitude for our remarkable cast, writers, directors and crew who have poured their hearts into this show, and for the incredible fans around the world whose unwavering loyalty has kept Criminal Minds going for two decades.”

Season 20’s RJ Hatanaka as Tyler Green, Joe Mantegna as David Rossi, A.J. Cook as Jennifer ‘JJ’ Jareau, Paget Brewster as Emily Prentiss, Adam Rodriguez as Luke Alvez, Kirsten Vangsness as Penelope Garcia and Aisha Tyler as Dr. Tara Lewis. Michael Becker/Paramount+

She added, “We’re looking forward to sharing season 20 with everyone, and celebrating the legacy of this special show we’ve all built together.”

Criminal Minds premiered on CBS in 2005, following a group of criminal profilers working for the FBI’s Behavioral Analysis Unit (BAU), often focusing on cases involving serial killers.

Zach Gilford's 'Criminal Minds' Character Slams Serial Killer Romanticization

The show’s original run came to an end in 2020, but it was revived by Paramount+ and rebranded as Criminal Minds: Evolution two years later.

The new iteration brought back the majority of the cast, including Vangsness, Joe Mantegna, A.J. Cook, Aisha Tyler, Adam Rodriguez and Paget Brewster all of whom reprised their original roles.

Other key characters, such as Matthew Gray Gubler’s Dr. Spencer Reid, have made cameos on the reboot.

A.J. Cook and Adam Rodriguez. Michael Becker/Paramount+

During Evolution’s first season (a.k.a. season 16 of Criminal Minds), viewers were introduced to Elias Voit, played by Zach Gilford, as the main antagonist.

The BAU ultimately linked Voit to 62 murders during his debut season, and discovered that he was the mastermind behind an online network of serial killers.

Voit remained a constant presence throughout the next three seasons, even after he was no longer considered to be a psychopath following significant memory loss. The villain even became a consultant for the BAU at one point before he was written off the show during the season 19 finale.

“We can both hope [my character Voit will return] but all I know is I’m not there filming right now,” Gilford, 44, exclusively told Us in July of his potential return for season 20. “It was hard to say goodbye. It definitely was one of my favorite characters I’ve ever gotten to play and partially because what a gift.”

Joe Mantegna, Kirsten Vangsness and Paget Brewster. Michael Becker/Paramount+

Despite being done with his story arc, the actor looked back on his time as Voit fondly.

“To give this guy some redemption and go out on a good guy note — so to speak — was nice. It’s weird,” he shared. “You wrap a show and a lot of times you’re like, ‘I don’t know if I’m coming back next year and if we’re going to have another season in general.’ On this, I did know that it was the end.”

The rest of the Evolution cast is expected to return for the farewell season.
