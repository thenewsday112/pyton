Darkbloom, which allowed Mac users to rent out their GPUs for AI inference, warns of low payouts

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p>Declining token prices have prompted Darkbloom, a distributed inference network, to reassess its business plans.</p><p><a href="https://www.darkbloom.ai">Darkbloom</a>, a project from Seattle-based tech biz Eigen Labs, has developed software that allows anyone with a sufficiently capable macOS device <a href="https://www.theregister.com/ai-and-ml/2026/08/24/conjure-cash-with-old-macs-by-linking-them-to-ai-inference-borg/5292002">to rent their GPU</a> through the Darkbloom network for interference jobs. In return, they get paid.</p><p>Through this network, 800 or so participants – including as of two days ago this reporter – have been able to collect a few cents to a few dollars per day by having their hardware handle AI model prompts submitted <a href="https://openrouter.ai/provider/darkbloom">through OpenRouter</a>, an AI model gateway.</p><p>In theory, someone running one of Apple's recently released Mac minis with an M5 Pro chip and 64 GB of RAM could earn as much as $192 per month handling incoming inference jobs, per the company's estimate. </p><p>That assumes full 24/7 utilization during this period on a sought-after model like Qwen3.6 35B A3B, which sells for $0.065/M input tokens, $0.75/M output tokens. But not every network participant has fully surrendered their Mac hardware to the network.</p><p>The median earnings of the 50 people on the leaderboard amount to about $5.26 per day, $159.86 per month, or $1,918.50 per year. So the best case estimate presented by Darkbloom looks plausible. But it may not be sustainable.</p><p>The price of tokens – the unit of AI work – has declined, reportedly <a href="https://www.axios.com/2026/10/22/ai-token-prices-demand">down 54 percent since May</a>. Ramp's <a href="https://ramp.com/data/ai-index?metric=token-price&amp;detail=blended">AI Token Price Index</a>, which tracks the price of tokens from Anthropic and OpenAI, shows similar slippage. For Anthropic, the price per million tokens went from $2.65 on May 1, 2026 to $0.62 on October 22, 2026, a decline of 77 percent.</p><p>In a message shared with Darkbloom participants on the company's Slack channel, Kydo, one of the Eigen Labs researchers working on the distributed inference network, celebrated the network's processing of its trillionth token while attempting to moderate expectations about the project's viability.</p><p>"Earnings have been low," said Kydo. "For most providers, I don't expect that it is going to recover soon."</p><p>Pointing to the computational prowess of DeekSeek's<a href="https://x.com/0xkydo/status/2114114594058707002"> DeepInfer framework</a>, he explained that structural changes in the inference market have affected the supply of tokens.</p><p>"Data centers have become cheaper," he said. "Output per NVIDIA cluster is up ~20x this year, and their margins are wide enough to cut prices 90 percent and still profit at 50 percent."</p><p>The flood of tokens in turn has washed away the price advantage Darkbloom had previously. "The market caught up to our price," Kydo explained. "We could go lower, but then most of you would earn even less nothing."</p><p>Finally, he said, the inference routing market is changing, with OpenRouter moving away from price-based routing and putting more emphasis on decode speed, quantization, and prefill speed.</p><p>For network participants with older or less powerful Mac hardware – those without top of the line Max or Ultra Apple Silicon – "inference won't be worth running," he said.</p><p>Kydo went on to outline how Darkbloom aims to adapt to the commoditized token market. This involves analyzing model demand to steer participants toward more sought-after models, and looking at ways to provide other kinds of compute work, like distributed CI/CD jobs.</p><p>For Mac hardware, the focus now appears to be on clusters of high-end Mac Studios (now available with up to 512 GB of RAM), using Apple's Remote Direct Memory Access (<a href="https://developer.apple.com/documentation/technotes/tn3205-low-latency-communication-with-rdma-over-thunderbolt">RDMA</a>) over Thunderbolt.</p><p>The CI/CD work, based on the observation that AI agents now make pull requests faster than CI/CD pipelines can handle them, will require sandboxing — something the biz expects to begin testing soon. And Darkbloom also aims to improve the performance of its network (in terms of time to first token) and to look for other token buyers beyond OpenRouter.</p><p>"The next stretch will be a fight, and I expect many machines will leave and Slack will get quieter," Kydo concluded. "But those who stay will be first in line when new earning opportunities come along. We are a driven team and well capitalized. We will not give up." ®</p>

The Justice Department has charged five people in a crackdown on "ghost student" fraud, in which scammers allegedly use stolen identities and AI to enroll fake students in colleges, complete coursework, and collect federal student loan funds. Ars Technica reports: In a press release Thursday, the Department of Justice said it had uncovered a large-scale scheme relying partly on AI. The DOJ seized more than $750,000 in cryptocurrency from "a network associated with Kenyan nationals" after data analysis helped flag likely fraudsters. Assistant Attorney General Colin M. McDonald of the DOJ's National Fraud Enforcement Division credited new fraud-detection measures implemented by the Trump administration for the arrests. He said that "this is the first international seizure of its kind in Kenya," while warning fraudsters that the crackdown has only just begun. [...] At a press conference, McDonald was joined by FBI and Department of Education officials to explain how the scheme works. It starts with bad actors purchasing personally identifiable information (PII) of Americans -- most frequently targeting high school graduates who never attended college and went straight into the workforce. Once they have that sensitive data, they fill out the Free Application for Federal Student Aid (FAFSA) while seeking to enroll in universities and community colleges. If accepted, student aid is distributed first to schools to cover tuition and fees, but any leftover money is deposited into bank accounts controlled by the fraudsters. Dozens of Americans have opened bank accounts to act as money mules for fraudsters, the DOJ said. To keep money flowing, bad actors use AI to fill out applications, complete coursework, and even make it appear that a student is attending virtual sessions. "It looks like the ghost student is actually completing the class," McDonald said at the press conference. "Some even create the appearance of a fake student in an online classroom to trick schools into believing a real person is present. The goal is to keep the ghost student in school for as long as possible, semester after semester after semester." So far, the DOE has "identified over 830,000 potentially fraudulent federal student aid accounts created by using this PII of US citizens," officials said at the press conference. HTTP/3 is faster with some work and help from Cloudflare

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p>When you're ordering food from your phone, even time spent navigating the app is time wasted. At the P99 2026 <a href="https://p99conf.io/session/http-3-in-production-what-the-numbers-actually-say/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">virtual conference</a>, <a href="https://www.linkedin.com/in/elvischidera/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Elvis Chidera</a>, a staff engineer at Germany-based global delivery service <a href="https://www.deliveryhero.com/about/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Delivery Hero</a>, explained how the company moved most of its user base to HTTP/3, and consequently cut its apps’ total end-to-end response time of the home screen by 17 percent. </p><p>Chidera, who managed the project, explained that, as promised on the tin, HTTP/3 delivers the web faster than HTTP/2, though it has slow bits that need to be adjusted to work properly. </p><h3>Uber eats Delivery Hero</h3><p>Delivery Hero is all but unknown in the US, which is dominated by Uber Eats and DoorDash. But it is a company with global reach outside the US, with an <a href="https://www.deliveryhero.com/newsroom/15-years-delivering-to-you/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">estimated</a> user base of 60 million across Europe, Latin America, Africa, the Middle East, and Asia. Uber is <a href="https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Acquisition-Offer-for-Delivery-Hero/default.aspx" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">currently acquiring</a><a href="https://investor.uber.com/news-events/news/press-release-details/2026/Uber-Announces-Acquisition-Offer-for-Delivery-Hero/default.aspx"> </a>Delivery Hero for $14.8 billion, with plans to unite the company with Uber Eats.</p><p>With this purchase, Uber would expand the number of markets where it offers both ride-hailing and delivery services, though not necessarily under the Uber Eats nameplate. Delivery Hero centrally manages separate apps for different regions, all of which it built or acquired. Asians can order poke bowls and sundries through <a href="https://www.foodpanda.com/about-foodpanda/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Foodpanda</a>. Turks can order kebabs through <a href="https://www.yemeksepeti.com/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Yemeksepeti</a>. Throughout Spain, Italy, Poland, Romania, Ukraine, and much of Africa, the <a href="https://careers.glovoapp.com/tech-glovo/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Glovo</a> app is where to ring up the late-night deliveries. And in Latin America, users dial in to <a href="https://www.pedidosya.com/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">PedidosYa</a> to get what they need from nearby farmacias, restaurantes, and mercados.</p><p>All these regions have widely varying network connectivity, a problem Delivery Hero wanted to minimize. </p><p>Last mile networking was most often the bottleneck. Apps in Pakistan, for instance, could take nearly a second (at p90) to establish a secure handshake with a server. The engineering team looked at HTTP/3 as a way to lower, even eliminate, that latency. </p><h3>A QUIC-er HTTP</h3><p>The Internet Engineering Task Force (IETF) <a href="https://www.ietf.org/blog/http-updates/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">formally introduced</a> the third version of the internet’s Hypertext Transfer Protocol in 2022. HTTP is the application layer protocol that connects browsers and other resources connected to servers over the Internet. </p><p>The previous versions of <a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/rfc/rfc9110.html" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">HTTP</a> were built on the Transmission Control Protocol (TCP), which offered guaranteed delivery and flow control. In conjunction with the Internet Protocol (IP), TCP/IP built the modern internet. </p><p>HTTP/3 replaced TCP with Quick UDP Internet Connection (QUIC),<a href="https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/07/08/media-over-quic-can-scale-real-time-streaming-and-carry-the-worlds-vids/5268101" rel="nofollow" target="_blank"> developed by Google</a>, to address some of TCP’s<a href="https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/10/01/stanford-prof-is-beating-the-drum-for-a-new-protocol-to-replace-tcp/5300629"> p</a><a href="https://www.theregister.com/networks/2026/10/01/stanford-prof-is-beating-the-drum-for-a-new-protocol-to-replace-tcp/5300629" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">erceived sluggishness</a> as a transfer protocol. </p><p>UDP stands for User Datagram Protocol and, unlike TCP, it does not guarantee packet delivery or flow control. UDP is faster because it doesn’t wait around for a signature (QUIC adds in its own flow control on top of UDP).</p><p>But the UDP-based HTTP/3 reduces, and sometimes eliminates, the round trips needed to secure connections. The app can cache a session ticket and, on a later connection, use QUIC's 0-RTT capability to start sending eligible application data while the handshake is still underway, rather than waiting for it to finish.</p><p>“Essentially, you can eliminate that one second” of latency that plagued slower networks, Chidera said. “You don't want to pay the cost of two round trips just to exchange data, if you could do it faster.”</p><p>And unlike HTTP/2, HTTP/3 doesn’t get hung up when packets are lost or delayed. There’s no head-of-line blocking because packets are sent in multiple streams, not just the single string that HTTP/2 offers. </p><h3>Multitask during initialization</h3><p>Setting up HTTP/3 turned out to be surprisingly easy, Chidera found. Delivery Hero used Cloudflare’s content delivery network to serve its global user base, so moving to HTTP/3 involved <a href="https://developers.cloudflare.com/speed/optimization/protocol/http3/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">a simple reconfiguration</a>. </p><p>Adding HTTP/3 support to the clients, mostly mobile, proved almost as straightforward. Apple has supported HTTP/3 since iOS 15 and most native browsers <a href="https://caniuse.com/?search=http%2F3" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">already support HTTP/3</a> out of the box. </p><p>Android proved a bit trickier. The engineers used Google’s <a href="https://developer.android.com/develop/connectivity/cronet" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Cronet library</a> for the networking stack, initially wired into their Android apps with the <a href="https://github.com/google/cronet-transport-for-okhttp" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Cronet Transport for OkHttp</a> wrapper to minimize rewriting their existing code, originally written for the <a href="https://github.com/lysine-dev/okhttp" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">OkHttp HTTP client</a>. </p><p>Out of the box, Cronet helped support the HTTP/3 rollout, which reduced network-level request failures. But it also came with a significant catch: the engine took approximately 800 milliseconds to initialize at P90, eating into some of HTTP/3's 0-RTT speed benefits. So the team forked the code into their own bridge using a library called <a href="https://github.com/limuyang2/okcronet" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">OKCronet</a>. They also did some additional engineering work, which involved overlapping Cronet initialization with other background tasks.</p><p>Overall, they slashed the P95 latency time from 1.2 seconds to 120 milliseconds, achieving a 10x improvement in read latency. </p><h3>Choose your congestion control algo wisely</h3><p>Overall, the engineering team moved 95 percent of the users over to HTTP/3. Those devices that couldn’t use the protocol (largely due to networks blocking UDP traffic) automatically fell back to HTTP/2. Handshake latency between the apps and the server was cut by 51 percent. Pakistan’s response time was cut in half.</p><p>One hurdle remained, however. Secondary screens on some of the apps (pages/screens that aren’t the home page) experienced latency issues. These issues only happened in certain markets that were known to be prone to packet loss, such as Pakistan. </p><p>Delivery Hero’s engineers attributed the issue to Cloudflare’s choice of the congestion control algorithm for HTTP/3, <a href="https://www.rfc-editor.org/info/rfc8312/" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">Cubic</a>, which historically has <a href="https://www.forasoft.com/learn/video-streaming/articles-streaming/congestion-control-bbr-cubic-copa" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">not handled packet loss well</a>. The engineering team tested some of its traffic with Google HTTP/3-aware servers, which use another congestion protocol, Bottleneck Bandwidth and Round-trip propagation time (<a href="https://www.emergentmind.com/topics/bottleneck-bandwidth-and-round-trip-propagation-time-bbr" rel="nofollow" target="_blank">BBR</a>), and did not get hung up on the packet loss.</p><p>Delivery Hero is currently working with Cloudflare to resolve this specific congestion algorithm issue, Chidera said. </p><p>Sometimes that fresh, delicious <a href="https://turkishdishes.net/practical-turkish-lahmacun-recipe/">lahmacun</a> just won’t get to your door as fast as it possibly can, despite all best efforts. ®</p>

AI is changing the value of a college degree, and the implications are enormous

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A college degree is supposed to be the safest investment a young person can make. Yet Palantir now recruits high school graduates directly. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Google and Apple dropped their degree requirements years ago, and IBM says more than half of its new U.S. jobs no longer need a bachelor&#8217;s degree.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yilei Shao of the Shanghai AI-Finance School at <a href="https://english.ecnu.edu.cn/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">East China Normal University</a> treats those moves as symptoms rather than fads. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The theory underneath the modern school system holds that education is an investment in a person&#8217;s future productivity, and economists call it human capital. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In a new paper, Shao argues that this theory no longer fully fits a world where artificial intelligence is making certain forms of intelligence increasingly abundant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">She wrote the paper for the journal <em>ECNU Review of Education</em>. It&#8217;s an argument, not an experiment, and it collected no data.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead, the paper revisits the five Nobel Prize winners whose ideas explain why governments pay for <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/experts-warn-that-education-systems-must-change-in-the-age-of-ai/">schools</a>. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then it asks whether their assumptions survive a world in which a <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/students-ai-physics-searches/">chatbot</a> answers an expert&#8217;s question in seconds.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-five-theories-behind-modern-education">Five theories behind modern education</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 1961, Theodore Schultz told the American Economic Association that money spent on education wasn&#8217;t consumption but investment. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gary Becker developed the theory further, showing how education could increase a person&#8217;s earning potential.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Michael Spence added a twist in 1973: even if school taught nothing, a degree would still work as a <a href="https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">signal</a>, because finishing one is easiest for the people employers want. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Paul Romer and Robert Lucas supplied the last piece: knowledge spills over, so one person&#8217;s learning raises the productivity of everyone nearby.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao identified five assumptions underlying those theories. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Education produces steady financial returns, skills retain their value over time, and individuals own the abilities they develop.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The remaining assumptions are that academic credentials reliably reflect a person&#8217;s abilities and that education generally benefits society beyond the individual student.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Governments fund schools and degree systems exist because of those five ideas about human capital, Shao wrote, and every one of them is now failing at once.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-skills-wear-out-faster">Skills wear out faster</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Depreciation fails first. Shao put the half-life of a skill, the time for half of its value to wear off, at 10 to 15 years in the industrial era. In the information age it fell to 5 to 7, and in the AI age it may be 2 or 3.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <a href="https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">World Economic Forum</a> projects that about 22% of today&#8217;s jobs will be <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/ai-is-disrupting-jobs-but-countries-are-learning-to-adapt/">disrupted</a> by 2030. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/artificial-intelligence-is-exposing-whats-wrong-with-modern-education/">school system</a> that concentrates its spending in the first two decades of life was built for a world where one round of training lasted a career, Shao wrote.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ownership fails next. A programmer working with an AI coding assistant may be 10 times as productive as one working alone. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Move that programmer to a company without the tools, though, and the output drops at once. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The ability no longer belongs to the person, Shao wrote, but to the person and the machine together.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The signal fails too. Gabriel Petersson, a Swedish high school dropout, taught himself machine learning with ChatGPT and joined OpenAI&#8217;s Sora team as a research scientist in late 2024. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Degrees certify memorization, execution and a stock of knowledge, Shao wrote, and those are the things AI copies fastest.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-intelligence-became-abundant">Intelligence became abundant</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every era&#8217;s schools were organized around whatever was in short supply, Shao wrote, and the information age was short of knowledge workers, so education expanded. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao argues that AI is making certain forms of intelligence increasingly abundant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Her proposed replacement for human capital is Intelligence Surplus: a society&#8217;s reserve of intelligent capability beyond what it needs for basic operations. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Borrowing from the sociologist Pierre Bourdieu, Shao split it into three forms: the skills people have for working with AI, the technical systems themselves, and the rules that govern them.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The difference from older capital is copying: there is only one Van Gogh painting, Shao wrote, but a single GPT model can serve hundreds of millions of people at once.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-caring-becomes-scarce">Caring becomes scarce</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao&#8217;s example is a veteran teacher who can hear, in a <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/students-who-let-ai-do-their-work-may-actually-get-worse-grades/">student&#8217;s</a> &#8220;I get it,&#8221; whether the student understood or has given up. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That judgment comes from years of experience and an understanding of the individual student. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao argues that such deeply personal insight remains difficult for AI to reproduce.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When models are trained on their own output, the rare parts of the data can disappear altogether, a problem a 2024 study in Nature called <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">model collapse</a>. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">So AI systems need a steady supply of fresh human experience, and people have to translate it into a form a machine can use. Shao calls those people First Compressors.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-school-would-become">What school would become</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao mapped three levels of change. Individuals would need what the paper calls dual literacy, technical fluency joined to judgment, ethics and a sense of meaning. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Governments would treat education as infrastructure, like railways and power grids.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Schools and employers come in between, with four shifts. Lifelong learning in place of front-loading, on the model of Singapore&#8217;s <a href="https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">SkillsFuture</a> program and its portable learning credit. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Personalized teaching, with AI handling the standardized parts.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Learning would also move to where people and algorithms meet, such as an emergency room at 3 a.m. where a nurse&#8217;s unease overrides an AI triage score. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A credential would be built from a portfolio of those encounters rather than a transcript.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-a-theory-still-waiting-for-proof">A theory still waiting for proof</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">None of this has been measured.
