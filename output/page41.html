Basking sharks may be gathering in large groups for an unexpected reason

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

Basking sharks are largely loners, and each one follows its own migration route. When big groups of them gather at the sea surface, the usual explanation is that they’ve found a rich patch of the tiny drifting animals they eat.

Food may be only part of the answer. In a new computer simulation, basking sharks formed groups that most closely resembled real gatherings when they searched for both food and each other.

The researchers read that as a sign the gatherings come from food and social attraction combined, not from food or chance alone.

Chelsea Gray, a researcher with the Irish Basking Shark Group, led the work. She is also affiliated with George Mason University’s Department of Environmental Science and Policy in Virginia.

Every shark in the study is a set of rules inside a computer. The team calls the work a proof of concept, a first test of whether this kind of simulation can mimic basking shark behavior.

Crowds put sharks at risk

Basking sharks are the second-largest fish alive, and they’re listed as endangered worldwide. They often feed right at the surface of coastal waters in the North Atlantic, which is where they’re most at risk from boats and fishing gear.

Basking shark gatherings range from pairs to large groups of hundreds. These groups often form during the busy season for boats, which raises the risk of boat strikes.

What the gatherings are for is still unknown. Scientists have suggested feeding, courtship, or both, and some sharks in these groups swim in circles or side by side instead of feeding.

Where basking sharks mate or give birth is also a mystery. Only one pregnant female has ever been recorded caught in the North Atlantic, back in 1943.

In 2022, partly because of these gatherings, Ireland listed basking sharks under its Wildlife Act. The law forbids disturbing their feeding and breeding behavior.

How sharks find food and each other

Watching the gatherings directly is hard, because it depends on finding an elusive animal. So Gray’s team built 200 virtual sharks instead.

Each one made its own choices once per simulated day, through every April-to-October season from 1982 to 2018.

Modelers call this kind of simulation an individual-based model, or IBM, and nobody had used one for basking sharks before.

“Most importantly, it shows that an IBM can realistically replicate basking shark behavior,” Gray said.

The team modeled more than 4,000 square miles (10,545 square kilometers) around the Inishowen Peninsula in County Donegal, divided into squares about 0.6 mile (1 kilometer) wide.

The sharks stayed near the surface, where the public sightings come from.

A virtual shark could sense food about 6 miles (10 kilometers) away and other sharks twice as far. If it went two weeks without enough food, it left the model and came back 20 days later.

Real basking sharks leave Irish surface waters and return later in the season, and the rule mimics that.

Earlier studies estimated the sharks’ prey from the green pigment in plant plankton, and those estimates matched shark sightings unevenly.

Gray’s team simulated the zooplankton directly, using counts from the Continuous Plankton Recorder Survey, which the model scattered in patches.

Shark gatherings in the wild

The team ran four versions of the model. In one, sharks only looked for food. In another, they only looked for other sharks, heading for any square that held at least five sharks.

A third version combined the two, with food first. In the fourth, sharks swam at random as a check.

Each version ran 50 times. The team compared the results with real sightings collected by the Irish Whale and Dolphin Group and Gray’s own group.

Anyone who saw a shark could send in a report, from fishers at sea to hikers on land.

Between 1982 and 2018, the two groups logged 2,136 sightings around Ireland, and 45% involved two or more sharks.

Around Inishowen alone there were 458 sightings, with the same share in groups.

Inishowen is a hot spot, and part of its coast is recognized internationally as an important area for sharks and rays.

To keep the comparison fair, the model also mimicked casual sightings. Each simulated day, it checked 20 random squares and logged any shark it found there.

Food and social attraction work together

Virtual sharks that only followed each other, or only swam at random, almost never formed groups bigger than two.

With nothing in the water drawing several sharks to the same spot, they kept heading toward whichever neighbors were close.

The food-only sharks had the opposite problem. They formed far more groups than the combined version did, and more large groups than the sightings recorded.

The number of groups also rose and fell in a steady seasonal rhythm.

The real sightings follow the seasons too, but unevenly. Virtual sharks that sought both food and company gathered in the same irregular way.

They also came closest to the sightings, both in how many groups formed each month and in how big those groups were.

The match wasn’t perfect. In the model, average group size tended to rise a little later than it did in the real records.

Comparing the versions, the authors concluded that food set how many groups formed in the model.

Attraction to other sharks affected how large those groups grew. Social attraction alone, they wrote, isn’t enough to explain the crowds people report.

Patchy food may draw crowds

In early tests, the model matched the sightings more closely when fewer than 20% of its squares held zooplankton.

When half or more of the squares held food, the food-only and combined versions behaved almost the same.

The authors suggested that the Inishowen coast may have especially patchy prey, and that patchy prey makes gatherings more likely.

In a sea where food is scattered, a group of sharks may work as a signal that food is nearby.

The virtual sharks could detect each other from farther away than they could detect food, so a crowd could be noticed before the meal that drew it.

Sound may attract sharks

Earlier studies have suggested that breaching, a leap clear of the water, may let basking sharks signal to each other over long distances.

Sharks can sense sound from much farther away than they may be able to pick up smells.

The authors wrote that their results may lend support to the idea that sound matters, either as a sign of prey or as a social signal.

If so, underwater noise from human activity might affect how basking sharks navigate and find food or mates, as it does for marine mammals.

Gray’s team had no local measurements of where zooplankton gathers around Inishowen, and the model scattered its prey at random each day.

For 79% of the days studied, the plankton survey had no sample at all.

The team filled those gaps with estimates that, the authors acknowledged, didn’t closely match the real samples.

Even when the model checked 20 squares a day, its simulated sightings missed many groups. Real reports depend on the public, and Gray’s own earlier survey in Inishowen found that most people didn’t know they could report a shark.

The virtual sharks have no age or sex, and they never dive below 33 feet (10 meters). Real basking sharks have been recorded feeding as deep as about 2,950 feet (900 meters).

Predicting future shark gatherings

With better plankton data, a future version might flag when large gatherings are likely, warning boaters and fishers.

“This first iteration lays the groundwork, and I see enormous potential for new developments, collaborations and experimental modeling,” she said. The model is free to download on GitHub.

The findings suggest that basking sharks may gather for more than a shared meal. Their attraction to one another could help explain why large groups form in certain coastal waters.

Although the results come from a computer simulation, they offer a promising new way to understand these mysterious gatherings.

With better data, future models could help predict where sharks are likely to congregate and reduce their risk of collisions with boats.

The full study was published in the journal Individual-based Ecology.

Crim spent $7K+ on AI services in extortion attacks, then left evidence for all to see

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

A n0n ransomware operator tried to break into 23 organizations across 14 countries, but couldn't secure its own front door, leaving its server exposed with all of its methods on view.

The criminals used a mix of new (AI) and old (stolen credentials) tooling during their data-theft spree, and in some cases not only stole and encrypted files, but also deleted data and backups, eliminating victims’ ability to recover their information.

And despite the fear and hype around the breachpocalypse, with AI finding and developing exploits for critical vulnerabilities affecting most organizations across the globe, these crooks did not exploit a single bug to gain entry into any of their victims’ IT environments.

“In every documented compromise in this investigation, access relied on weak or stolen credentials,” said Ensar Seker, chief information security officer of threat intelligence firm SOCRadar.

“What stands out is the combination of AI-assisted development and very conventional entry points,” Seker told The Register. “We found no evidence of vulnerability exploitation in the cases examined.”

The gang breaks into organizations at scale using widespread Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) scanning and automated FortiGate SSL-VPN credential stuffing. To access US-based IT managed services provider ChiBiTek, the crew used a 15-thread residential SOCKS5 proxy pool to test credentials (ip:port:user:password) against FortiGate portals (GET /remote/login).

The operators also gained entry using exposed cloud credentials and reused hosting portal sessions. In one case linked to e-commerce site Fanatics, SOCRadar found that n0n used an exposed AWS access key with permissions to list, read, and delete objects in S3 buckets. The criminals then used this key to pull data from 40 S3 buckets, with up to 110 GB per bucket, and ultimately deleted data, including bank-transaction archives, and purged keys across 12 sensitive buckets.

AI-assisted extortion

While the threat hunters didn’t find any evidence of a fully autonomous attack system, the ransomware operator did use AI to assist with these intrusions, incurring more than $7,216 in AI usage costs by October 22.

Specifically: the criminal or criminals used OpenCode, an open-source AI coding agent, configured with 154 model entries across seven providers. Models included Gemini, Mistral, Nemotron, Llama, Claude, GPT-5.x, Grok, Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Qwen, and many, many others, some with safety training removed and others marketed as “uncensored” models.

“The operator used AI to write and debug hundreds of scripts. That shows how AI can help an attacker turn basic access into a broader, tailored operation,” Seker said.

“The destructive behavior is equally significant,” he added. “The operator did more than steal and encrypt files: in some cases, it deleted data and backups, and in one case changed firewall policies to block traffic. That creates a recovery problem that a decryption key alone cannot solve.”

SOCRadar’s threat hunters found the operator’s workspace - a publicly accessible Python HTTP file server hosted in Sweden at 185.157.161[.]148 on port 8877 - last month, and documented the exposed directory in snapshots on September 25 and 26.

“Across those snapshots, we identified 1,719 files, including attack scripts, ransomware source code, extortion infrastructure, and an AI coding agent environment,” Seker said. “This gave us visibility into the operation behind the victim listings, including how the operator prepared attacks and handled stolen data.”

Meet n0n

It also provided a close look at n0n, a newcomer to the data-theft and extortion game that has claimed 108 victims since it emerged last month.

On October 12, the group added 38 maritime, port and logistics organizations across 18 countries to its leak site in a single batch. Researchers have linked at least 21 of them to customers of South Korean maritime software provider CyberLogitec, although the software provider has not confirmed an intrusion.

The crooks set an October 19 deadline for the maritime firms, with ransom demands ranging from $400,000 to $28 million, payable in Bitcoin. After the deadline, however, none of the 38 listings were marked “settled” and none of their payment addresses showed any transaction. All affected organizations’ stolen data was published.

While a handful of the gang’s claimed victims have been marked “honored - data destroyed” on n0n’s leak site, indicating the victim orgs paid the ransom demand, SOCRadar hasn’t validated any transactions on-chain. In its profile details, the security shop rates the gang a 10 out of 10 for "aggressiveness" and a zero out of 10 for “lethality.”

The group is believed to be based in Russia, although the server uncovered by SOCRadar, which it attributed to a single operator or small team, didn’t provide any definitive geographic or national attribution.

Every case that the exposed server documented followed the same lifecycle, moving from initial access to establishing persistence and credential collection via hidden admin accounts disguised as services, covert proxies, credential and session-token theft, mailboxes and emailed multi-factor authentication codes.

The crooks then collected sensitive data and sent everything to the operator’s central storage with the attack impact shifting between data-theft, encryption, destruction, and network disruption, depending on the targeted organization.

After breaking into Transcom, a Netherlands-based financial services provider, the crims sent a secondary ransom alert directly to PayPal's security team, even though the compromised data was only on Transcom's network.

Stupid mistakes and n0n security basics

Finally, the crims send ransom notes, post a countdown clock on the leak site, establish negotiation rooms and emails for victims - and then leak the stolen files or destroy backups when the organizations don’t pay up. In some cases, and to apply increased pressure on victimized companies, n0n also broadcasts automated countdown warnings to businesses’ service providers, clients, and media outlets.

Despite its aggressive tactics, the operator in this case - as with other stupid criminals The Register has gleefully documented in earlier stories - still struggles with basic security hygiene.

“There is a striking gap between operational ambition and security discipline,” Seker said. “The operator invested thousands of dollars in AI services while leaving its own workspace exposed. The locker also contained hardcoded encryption keys, with evidence suggesting reuse across victims. An operation can have serious destructive capability while still making fundamental mistakes.”®

Akanji: ‘Inter can’t afford a poor start vs. Milan, they have to work hard if they want to score’

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Sports  |  Read Source →

Manuel Akanji has urged his Inter teammates to avoid having a slow start against rivals Milan in the Derby della Madonnina on Saturday evening, insisting that the Rossoneri will have to ‘work hard’ if they want to score. 

Akanji previews Milan vs. Inter derby: ‘We can’t give away easy goals’

The first Derby della Madonnina of the season is taking place at 19.45 GMT on Saturday evening. Milan are the designated home side and are hoping to bounce back after a 4-2 defeat at home against Bologna earlier this week: Their first league defeat of the 2026-27 season. 

  • Read more: Chivu: ‘A derby is always an added motivation, but it’s not decisive’

Inter, meanwhile, sit at the top of the standings along with Lazio with their unbeaten start to the league season still intact. They have eight victories and one draw for 25 points on their record thus far. 

VENICE, ITALY – OCTOBER 28: Carlos Augusto of FC Internazionale celebrates his team’s second goal after an own goal by Redouane Halhal of Venezia FC during the Serie A match between Venezia FC and FC Internazionale at Stadio Pier Luigi Penzo on October 28, 2026 in Venice, Italy. (Photo by Maurizio Lagana/Getty Images)

Akanji, speaking in an interview with Sky Sport Italia on Friday, insists that he is ready and raring to go after starting on the bench against Venezia earlier this week. 

“I don’t feel tired physically, I had a bit of a rest during the last game. It’s never easy. Our goal is to win, that’s the most important thing, and is also the case against Milan. We hope to succeed,” said the Switzerland international. 

Akanji was asked for his take on Inter’s tendency to start slowly and concede first this season. The Nerazzurri have picked up 13 points from losing positions so far this Serie A season. 

“It’s not easy to give an explanation. It’s not that we start badly, but sometimes we make mistakes and concede goals. We can’t afford to do that, especially against the big teams,” said Akanji. “Sometimes you might get away with it, especially if the opponents don’t convert the chances they create. 

“We know that we can’t start like that against Milan, we can’t give away easy goals. They will have to work hard to score.”

MILAN, ITALY – NOVEMBER 23: Christian Pulisic of AC Milan competes for the ball with Manuel Akanji of FC Internazionale during the Serie A match between FC Internazionale and AC Milan at Giuseppe Meazza Stadium on November 23, 2025 in Milan, Italy. (Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images)

A victory for Inter on Saturday would open up a 10-point gap above their city rivals, but Akanji insists that it is far too early to start talking about the Scudetto. 

“It’s still early, but of course it would be great to win this game. The teams close to us are picking up points and it will be difficult to stay up there. For us, the derby is one of the most important games of the season, we want to get the three points.”

Akanji was also asked if the expanded 2026 World Cup has had an impact on Inter’s preparations in the early stages of the 2026-27 season. 

He replied: “It’s not just about that, we have to think about the last few years as well. Before that, there was the Club World Cup and the Euros the summer before. Fortunately, next year, there will be a proper break. When you play as much as that, starting again straight away is difficult. 

“You might be happy, maybe you had a good World Cup, maybe you need a rest, but there’s no time. The body responds to the present, the mind less so. It’s not easy. I’m 31 years old now, we have to think and play as a team. We want to finish well before the international break.”

MILAN, ITALY – MAY 17: Manuel Akanji of FC Internazionale Milano poses for a photo with the Coppa Campioni d’Italia and Coppa Italia trophies following the Serie A match between FC Internazionale and Hellas Verona FC at Giuseppe Meazza Stadium on May 17, 2026 in Milan, Italy. (Photo by Marco Luzzani/Getty Images)

Inter have not beaten Milan in any of their last seven meetings, losing five and drawing two since their last derby victory in April 2024. So, what has been the reason behind that?

“Last year, Milan showed us their low block and played intelligently. We can’t give away easy chances. We all have to defend and make things difficult for them. We have to take advantage on transitions when we have the ball.”

And what will be the most important thing for Inter to do when they step out against Milan on Saturday?

“The desire to win. That’s what counts in a derby. We have to show it from the first minute and not just bring a sense of hunger, but the desire to go and win. Mentality is what drives desire.”

As AI tools and agents have continued their march across all aspects of digital life, many companies are trying to use LLMs to improve the experience of purchasing goods and services on the Internet. Thus far, though, a vanishingly small number of people seem to be taking advantage of those capabilities, at least when it comes to using the popular food delivery app DoorDash with an outside AI agent.

In a third-quarter earnings call Thursday, DoorDash co-founder and CEO Tony Xu responded to an analyst question by noting that "AI agents are generating several hundred orders [on DoorDash] across the time that they've been up and running." In a marked understatement, Xu called this a "very low use case" for a company that handled just shy of 1 billion orders total in the third quarter of 2026 alone.

Speaking generally, Xu said he sees the potential for AI agents to be genuinely helpful for "long consideration purchases" and major decisions where agents would be "doing work on your behalf proactively in the background." However, Xu added, "when it comes to a task that's as simple as ordering food, we tend to see that come directly to DoorDash."
