Matthias Jaissle at Newcastle United: grounds for patience, reasons for concern

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Other  |  Read Source →

<a href="https://www.themag.co.uk/2026/10/matthias-jaissle-at-newcastle-united-grounds-for-patience-reasons-for-concern/" rel="nofollow" title="Matthias Jaissle at Newcastle United: grounds for patience, reasons for concern"><img alt="David Moyes Everton Sideline Jaissle Newcastle" class="webfeedsFeaturedVisual wp-post-image" height="432" src="https://www.themag.co.uk/assets/david-moyes-everton-matthias-jaissle-newcastle-united-nufc-768x432.jpg" style="display: block; margin: auto; margin-bottom: 5px;" width="768" /></a><p>The Newcastle United exit from the Carabao Cup leaves <a href="https://www.themag.co.uk/2026/10/this-is-not-an-excuse-any-more-matthias-jaissle-everton-3-newcastle-1/">Matthias Jaissle</a> facing a fair question: are we watching the uneven beginnings of something promising, or a team struggling to establish the foundations it needs?</p> <p>For now, there is evidence for both views.</p> <p>Everton should sharpen the scrutinybut it should not settle the verdict.</p> <p>Jaissle was appointed on 5 August. He has had less than three months in charge, arriving shortly before the competitive season and succeeding a manager whose achievements gave supporters both wonderful memories and legitimate expectations. That is a demanding inheritance. It is also too short a period to judge a managerial appointment conclusively.</p> <p>The league record offers some perspective. Newcastle have taken 11 points from eight matches, with three wins, two draws and three defeats. They have scored 12 goals and conceded 14. That is an uneven start, rather than evidence of either a breakthrough or a collapse.</p> <p>There are genuine glimmers of hope. Winning 2–0 at Tottenham demonstrated that this side can deliver away from home. More recently, the victory by the same score at <a href="https://www.themag.co.uk/2026/10/crystal-palace-fans-comments-before-and-after-losing-to-newcastle-united/">Crystal Palace</a> provided an encouraging response to successive league defeats. Jaissle made five changes for Palace, and several of those introduced contributed directly to the result. That suggests a willingness to reconsider his selections and an ability to produce a response.</p> <p>Availability also matters.</p> <p>For the Hull game, Newcastle had nine players missing, while Joelinton, Anthony Elanga and Jacob Ramsey have been among those working their way back. A new coach trying to establish relationships and patterns deserves some allowance for that disruption. The allowance cannot be unlimited but it should be acknowledged.</p> <p>Everton itself contained more NUFC attacking endeavour than the scoreline might suggest. Newcastle registered 28 shots against Everton’s 11 and more than twice as many touches in the opposition box. Those figures support the argument that the team continued to compete and create opportunities. They do not establish that Newcastle deserved to win: shot totals alone tell us too little about the quality of chances or control of a match.</p> <p>The strongest concern is the recurrence of damaging defeats. Losing 4–1 at Leeds, 4–1 at Coventry and now 3–1 at Everton means conceding 11 goals across these three away matches. The clean sheets at Tottenham and Palace are encouraging but the contrast exposes how inconsistent Newcastle’s defensive performance has been.</p> <p>At Coventry, Newcastle conceded an early penalty, failed to deal with a long throw for the second goal and then conceded twice after pulling themselves back into contention. At Everton, avoidable losses of possession and failures to defend decisive moments again proved costly. My concern is whether Newcastle United have sufficient collective security when something goes wrong.</p> <p>Matthias Jaissle has emphasised individual mistakes and the need for greater maturity. That is understandable but repeated mistakes eventually become a coaching question too.</p> <p>Are instructions clear? Are players receiving enough support? Does the approach suit those available? Players remain responsible for their decisions; the manager remains responsible for creating conditions in which good decisions become more likely.</p> <p>The cup exit also represents a lost opportunity. Newcastle selected a side showing only one change from the Palace victory. This was a serious attempt to progress and it failed. Supporters are entitled to feel disappointed without being accused of impatience.</p> <p>My assessment is that Matthias Jaissle deserves time, accompanied by clear expectations. Over the coming weeks, <a href="https://www.themag.co.uk/2026/10/the-elephant-in-the-room-at-newcastle-united-ross-wilson/">Newcastle United</a> need fewer damaging errors, greater resilience after conceding and performances that can be sustained from one match to the next. Another impressive afternoon will encourage; a dependable run will persuade.</p> <p>There is enough promise to support Matthias Jaissle. There is enough fragility to question him. The fairest position is to do both and judge whether the team is learning from setbacks rather than repeatedly explaining them.</p>
