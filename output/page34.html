AI is changing the value of a college degree, and the implications are enormous

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">A college degree is supposed to be the safest investment a young person can make. Yet Palantir now recruits high school graduates directly. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Google and Apple dropped their degree requirements years ago, and IBM says more than half of its new U.S. jobs no longer need a bachelor&#8217;s degree.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Yilei Shao of the Shanghai AI-Finance School at <a href="https://english.ecnu.edu.cn/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">East China Normal University</a> treats those moves as symptoms rather than fads. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The theory underneath the modern school system holds that education is an investment in a person&#8217;s future productivity, and economists call it human capital. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In a new paper, Shao argues that this theory no longer fully fits a world where artificial intelligence is making certain forms of intelligence increasingly abundant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">She wrote the paper for the journal <em>ECNU Review of Education</em>. It&#8217;s an argument, not an experiment, and it collected no data.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Instead, the paper revisits the five Nobel Prize winners whose ideas explain why governments pay for <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/experts-warn-that-education-systems-must-change-in-the-age-of-ai/">schools</a>. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Then it asks whether their assumptions survive a world in which a <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/students-ai-physics-searches/">chatbot</a> answers an expert&#8217;s question in seconds.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-five-theories-behind-modern-education">Five theories behind modern education</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 1961, Theodore Schultz told the American Economic Association that money spent on education wasn&#8217;t consumption but investment. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gary Becker developed the theory further, showing how education could increase a person&#8217;s earning potential.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Michael Spence added a twist in 1973: even if school taught nothing, a degree would still work as a <a href="https://doi.org/10.2307/1882010" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">signal</a>, because finishing one is easiest for the people employers want. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Paul Romer and Robert Lucas supplied the last piece: knowledge spills over, so one person&#8217;s learning raises the productivity of everyone nearby.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao identified five assumptions underlying those theories. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Education produces steady financial returns, skills retain their value over time, and individuals own the abilities they develop.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The remaining assumptions are that academic credentials reliably reflect a person&#8217;s abilities and that education generally benefits society beyond the individual student.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Governments fund schools and degree systems exist because of those five ideas about human capital, Shao wrote, and every one of them is now failing at once.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-skills-wear-out-faster">Skills wear out faster</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Depreciation fails first. Shao put the half-life of a skill, the time for half of its value to wear off, at 10 to 15 years in the industrial era. In the information age it fell to 5 to 7, and in the AI age it may be 2 or 3.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The <a href="https://www.weforum.org/press/2025/01/future-of-jobs-report-2025-78-million-new-job-opportunities-by-2030-but-urgent-upskilling-needed-to-prepare-workforces/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">World Economic Forum</a> projects that about 22% of today&#8217;s jobs will be <a href="https://www.earth.com/science/ai-is-disrupting-jobs-but-countries-are-learning-to-adapt/">disrupted</a> by 2030. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/artificial-intelligence-is-exposing-whats-wrong-with-modern-education/">school system</a> that concentrates its spending in the first two decades of life was built for a world where one round of training lasted a career, Shao wrote.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Ownership fails next. A programmer working with an AI coding assistant may be 10 times as productive as one working alone. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Move that programmer to a company without the tools, though, and the output drops at once. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The ability no longer belongs to the person, Shao wrote, but to the person and the machine together.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The signal fails too. Gabriel Petersson, a Swedish high school dropout, taught himself machine learning with ChatGPT and joined OpenAI&#8217;s Sora team as a research scientist in late 2024. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Degrees certify memorization, execution and a stock of knowledge, Shao wrote, and those are the things AI copies fastest.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-intelligence-became-abundant">Intelligence became abundant</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every era&#8217;s schools were organized around whatever was in short supply, Shao wrote, and the information age was short of knowledge workers, so education expanded. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao argues that AI is making certain forms of intelligence increasingly abundant.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Her proposed replacement for human capital is Intelligence Surplus: a society&#8217;s reserve of intelligent capability beyond what it needs for basic operations. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Borrowing from the sociologist Pierre Bourdieu, Shao split it into three forms: the skills people have for working with AI, the technical systems themselves, and the rules that govern them.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The difference from older capital is copying: there is only one Van Gogh painting, Shao wrote, but a single GPT model can serve hundreds of millions of people at once.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-caring-becomes-scarce">Caring becomes scarce</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao&#8217;s example is a veteran teacher who can hear, in a <a href="https://www.earth.com/lifestyle/students-who-let-ai-do-their-work-may-actually-get-worse-grades/">student&#8217;s</a> &#8220;I get it,&#8221; whether the student understood or has given up. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That judgment comes from years of experience and an understanding of the individual student. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao argues that such deeply personal insight remains difficult for AI to reproduce.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When models are trained on their own output, the rare parts of the data can disappear altogether, a problem a 2024 study in Nature called <a href="https://doi.org/10.1038/s41586-024-07566-y" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">model collapse</a>. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">So AI systems need a steady supply of fresh human experience, and people have to translate it into a form a machine can use. Shao calls those people First Compressors.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-school-would-become">What school would become</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao mapped three levels of change. Individuals would need what the paper calls dual literacy, technical fluency joined to judgment, ethics and a sense of meaning. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Governments would treat education as infrastructure, like railways and power grids.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Schools and employers come in between, with four shifts. Lifelong learning in place of front-loading, on the model of Singapore&#8217;s <a href="https://www.skillsfuture.gov.sg/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">SkillsFuture</a> program and its portable learning credit. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Personalized teaching, with AI handling the standardized parts.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Learning would also move to where people and algorithms meet, such as an emergency room at 3 a.m. where a nurse&#8217;s unease overrides an AI triage score. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A credential would be built from a portfolio of those encounters rather than a transcript.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-a-theory-still-waiting-for-proof">A theory still waiting for proof</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">None of this has been measured. The paper offers no way to count a society&#8217;s Intelligence Surplus, and the skill half-life is an estimate labeled &#8220;perhaps.&#8221; </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The hiring stories come from a handful of companies, with no labor market data behind them.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The deepest claim, that AI can process meaning but can&#8217;t produce it, rests on the philosopher Hannah Arendt and on no experiment. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The greatest challenge may be convincing universities, employers and governments to recognize skills that cannot easily be measured by traditional credentials.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Shao&#8217;s proposal remains untested, but it raises an important question about the future of education. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">As AI takes on more of the tasks people spend years learning to perform, schools may need to rethink what students should be learning in the first place.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The full study was published in the journal <a href="https://doi.org/10.1177/20965311261485586" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><em>ECNU Review of Education</em></a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8212;&#8211;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Like what you read? <a href="https://www.earth.com/subscribe/">Subscribe to our newsletter</a> for engaging articles, exclusive content, and the latest updates.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Check us out on <a href="https://www.earth.com/earthsnap/">EarthSnap</a>, a free app brought to you by <a href="https://www.earth.com/author/eralls/">Eric Ralls</a> and Earth.com.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8212;&#8211;</p>

Basking sharks may be gathering in large groups for an unexpected reason

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking sharks are largely loners, and each one follows its own migration route. When big groups of them gather at the sea surface, the usual explanation is that they&#8217;ve found a rich patch of the tiny drifting animals they eat.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Food may be only part of the answer. In a new computer simulation, basking sharks formed groups that most closely resembled real gatherings when they searched for both food and each other.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The researchers read that as a sign the gatherings come from food and social attraction combined, not from food or chance alone.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Chelsea Gray, a researcher with the <a href="https://www.baskingshark.ie" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Irish Basking Shark Group</a>, led the work. She is also affiliated with George Mason University&#8217;s <a href="https://science.gmu.edu/academics/departments-units/environmental-science-policy" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Department of Environmental Science and Policy</a> in Virginia.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every shark in the study is a set of rules inside a computer. The team calls the work a proof of concept, a first test of whether this kind of simulation can mimic basking shark behavior.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-crowds-put-sharks-at-risk">Crowds put sharks at risk</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-shark-has-bizarre-skin-dermal-denticles-very-unique/">sharks</a> are the second-largest fish alive, and they&#8217;re listed as endangered worldwide. They often feed right at the surface of coastal waters in the North Atlantic, which is where they&#8217;re most at risk from boats and fishing gear.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking shark gatherings range from pairs to <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-sharks-northeast-coast/">large groups</a> of hundreds. These groups often form during the busy season for boats, which raises the risk of <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-sharks-are-exceptionally-vulnerable-to-ship-strikes/">boat strikes</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">What the gatherings are for is still unknown. Scientists have suggested feeding, courtship, or both, and some sharks in these groups swim in circles or side by side instead of feeding.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Where basking sharks mate or give birth is also a mystery. Only one pregnant female has ever been recorded caught in the North Atlantic, back in 1943.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 2022, partly because of these gatherings, Ireland listed basking sharks under its Wildlife Act. The law forbids disturbing their feeding and breeding behavior.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-sharks-find-food-and-each-other">How sharks find food and each other</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Watching the gatherings directly is hard, because it depends on finding an elusive animal. So Gray&#8217;s team built 200 virtual sharks instead. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each one made its own choices once per simulated day, through every April-to-October season from 1982 to 2018.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modelers call this kind of simulation an individual-based model, or IBM, and nobody had used one for basking sharks before.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;Most importantly, it shows that an IBM can realistically replicate basking shark behavior,&#8221; Gray said.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team modeled more than 4,000 square miles (10,545 square kilometers) around the Inishowen Peninsula in County Donegal, divided into squares about 0.6 mile (1 kilometer) wide. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The sharks stayed near the surface, where the public sightings come from.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A virtual shark could sense food about 6 miles (10 kilometers) away and other sharks twice as far. If it went two weeks without enough food, it left the model and came back 20 days later.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Real basking sharks leave Irish surface waters and return later in the season, and the rule mimics that.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Earlier studies estimated the sharks&#8217; prey from the green pigment in plant plankton, and those estimates matched shark sightings unevenly. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gray&#8217;s team simulated the <a href="https://www.earth.com/climate/plankton-decline-leaves-north-atlantic-seas-in-poor-health/">zooplankton</a> directly, using counts from the <a href="https://www.cprsurvey.org" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Continuous Plankton Recorder Survey</a>, which the model scattered in patches.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-shark-gatherings-in-the-wild">Shark gatherings in the wild</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team ran four versions of the model. In one, sharks only looked for food. In another, they only looked for other sharks, heading for any square that held at least five sharks.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A third version combined the two, with food first. In the fourth, sharks swam at random as a check.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each version ran 50 times. The team compared the results with real sightings collected by the <a href="https://iwdg.ie" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Irish Whale and Dolphin Group</a> and Gray&#8217;s own group. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Anyone who saw a shark could send in a report, from fishers at sea to hikers on land.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Between 1982 and 2018, the two groups logged 2,136 sightings around Ireland, and 45% involved two or more sharks. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Around Inishowen alone there were 458 sightings, with the same share in groups.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Inishowen is a hot spot, and part of its coast is recognized internationally as an important area for sharks and rays.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">To keep the comparison fair, the model also mimicked casual sightings. Each simulated day, it checked 20 random squares and logged any shark it found there.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-food-and-social-attraction-work-together">Food and social attraction work together</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Virtual sharks that only followed each other, or only swam at random, almost never formed groups bigger than two. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With nothing in the water drawing several sharks to the same spot, they kept heading toward whichever neighbors were close.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The food-only sharks had the opposite problem. They formed far more groups than the combined version did, and more large groups than the sightings recorded. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The number of groups also rose and fell in a steady seasonal rhythm.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The real sightings follow the seasons too, but unevenly. Virtual sharks that sought both food and company gathered in the same irregular way. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">They also came closest to the sightings, both in how many groups formed each month and in how big those groups were.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The match wasn&#8217;t perfect. In the model, average group size tended to rise a little later than it did in the real records.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Comparing the versions, the authors concluded that food set how many groups formed in the model. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Attraction to other sharks affected how large those groups grew. Social attraction alone, they wrote, isn&#8217;t enough to explain the crowds people report.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-patchy-food-may-draw-crowds">Patchy food may draw crowds</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In early tests, the model matched the sightings more closely when fewer than 20% of its squares held zooplankton. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When half or more of the squares held food, the food-only and combined versions behaved almost the same.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The authors suggested that the Inishowen coast may have especially patchy prey, and that patchy prey makes gatherings more likely.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In a sea where food is scattered, a group of sharks may work as a signal that food is nearby. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The virtual sharks could detect each other from farther away than they could detect food, so a crowd could be noticed before the meal that drew it.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-sound-nbsp-may-nbsp-attract-nbsp-sharks">Sound&nbsp;may&nbsp;attract&nbsp;sharks</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Earlier studies have suggested that breaching, a leap clear of the water, may let basking sharks signal to each other over long distances. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sharks can <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/sharks-are-listening-to-the-ocean-from-surprisingly-far-away/">sense sound</a> from much farther away than they may be able to pick up smells.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The authors wrote that their results may lend support to the idea that sound matters, either as a sign of prey or as a social signal. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">If so, <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/boat-noise-pollution-humpback-whale-songs-difficult-to-hear-panama-ocean-coast/">underwater noise</a> from human activity might affect how basking sharks navigate and find food or mates, as it does for marine mammals.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gray&#8217;s team had no local measurements of where zooplankton gathers around Inishowen, and the model scattered its prey at random each day. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">For 79% of the days studied, the plankton survey had no sample at all.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team filled those gaps with estimates that, the authors acknowledged, didn&#8217;t closely match the real samples.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even when the model checked 20 squares a day, its simulated sightings missed many groups. Real reports depend on the public, and Gray&#8217;s own earlier survey in Inishowen found that most people didn&#8217;t know they could report a shark.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The virtual sharks have no age or sex, and they never dive below 33 feet (10 meters). Real basking sharks have been recorded feeding as deep as about 2,950 feet (900 meters).</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-predicting-future-shark-gatherings">Predicting future shark gatherings</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With better plankton data, a future version might flag when large gatherings are likely, warning boaters and fishers.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;This first iteration lays the groundwork, and I see enormous potential for new developments, collaborations and experimental modeling,&#8221; she said. The model is free to download <a href="https://github.com/graychelsea13-byte/Basking_Shark_IBM/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">on GitHub</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The findings suggest that basking sharks may gather for more than a shared meal. Their attraction to one another could help explain why large groups form in certain coastal waters.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Although the results come from a computer simulation, they offer a promising new way to understand these mysterious gatherings. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With better data, future models could help predict where sharks are likely to congregate and reduce their risk of collisions with boats.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The full study was published in the journal <a href="https://ibe.pensoft.net/article/176474/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank"><em>Individual-based Ecology</em></a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="block-3f7c76f5-a80b-477a-9954-730346d2e8e0">&#8212;&#8211;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="block-b6ac8411-a292-44a9-9ba3-5fb826f660e3">Like what you read? <a href="https://www.earth.com/subscribe/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Subscribe to our newsletter</a> for engaging articles, exclusive content, and the latest updates.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="block-8764c362-5538-44e4-8d78-d3c231a73b36">Check us out on <a href="https://www.earth.com/earthsnap/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">EarthSnap</a>, a free app brought to you by <a href="https://www.earth.com/author/eralls/" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Eric Ralls</a> and Earth.com.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph" id="block-5236f44a-a918-4e34-a7c5-75310d90ac48">&#8212;&#8211;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"></p>

<p>A n0n ransomware operator tried to break into 23 organizations across 14 countries, but couldn't secure its own front door, leaving its server exposed with all of its methods on view.</p><p>The criminals used a mix of new (AI) and old (stolen credentials) tooling during their data-theft spree, and in some cases not only stole and encrypted files, but also deleted data and backups, eliminating victims’ ability to recover their information.</p><p>And despite the <a href="https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/28/123-reg-discloses-ai-cyberattack-on-customer-data-suspects-china-nexus/5305936">fear</a> and <a href="https://www.theregister.com/cyber-crime/2026/10/27/cisa-confirms-php2shell-under-attack-as-researchers-clock-successful-exploits/5305681">hype</a> around the <a href="https://www.theregister.com/security/2026/10/26/microsoft-releases-emergency-net-security-fixes-for-google-brotli-bug/5305515">critical vulnerabilities</a> affecting <a exploit a single bug to gain entry into any of their victims’ IT environments. </p><p>“In every <a href="https://socradar.io/blog/n0n-ransomware-ai-assisted-extortion/" rel="nofollow">documented compromise</a> in this investigation, access relied on weak or stolen credentials,” said Ensar Seker, chief information security officer of threat intelligence firm SOCRadar.</p><p>“What stands out is the combination of AI-assisted development and very conventional entry points,” Seker told <span class="italic m-italic ">The Register</span>. “We found no evidence of vulnerability exploitation in the cases examined.”</p><p>The gang breaks into organizations at scale using widespread Remote Desktop Protocol (RDP) scanning and automated FortiGate SSL-VPN credential stuffing. To access US-based IT managed services provider ChiBiTek, the crew used a 15-thread residential SOCKS5 proxy pool to test credentials (ip:port:user:password) against FortiGate portals (GET /remote/login).</p><p>The operators also gained entry using exposed cloud credentials and reused hosting portal sessions. In one case linked to e-commerce site <a href="https://x.com/DailyDarkWeb/status/2101668219886919773">Fanatics</a>, SOCRadar found that n0n used an exposed AWS access key with permissions to list, read, and delete objects in S3 buckets. The criminals then used this key to pull data from 40 S3 buckets, with up to 110 GB per bucket, and ultimately deleted data, including bank-transaction archives, and purged keys across 12 sensitive buckets. </p><h3>AI-assisted extortion</h3><p>While the threat hunters didn’t find any evidence of a fully autonomous attack system, the ransomware operator did use AI to assist with these intrusions, incurring more than $7,216 in AI usage costs by October 22.</p><p>Specifically: the criminal or criminals used OpenCode, an open-source AI coding agent, configured with 154 model entries across seven providers. Models included Gemini, Mistral, Nemotron, Llama, Claude, GPT-5.x, Grok, Kimi, GLM, DeepSeek, MiniMax, Qwen, and many, many others, some with safety training removed and others marketed as “uncensored” models.</p><p>“The operator used AI to write and debug hundreds of scripts. That shows how AI can help an attacker turn basic access into a broader, tailored operation,” Seker said. </p><p>“The destructive behavior is equally significant,” he added. “The operator did more than steal and encrypt files: in some cases, it deleted data and backups, and in one case changed firewall policies to block traffic. That creates a recovery problem that a decryption key alone cannot solve.”</p><p>SOCRadar’s threat hunters found the operator’s workspace - a publicly accessible Python HTTP file server hosted in Sweden at 185.157.161[.]148 on port 8877 - last month, and documented the exposed directory in snapshots on September 25 and 26.</p><p>“Across those snapshots, we identified 1,719 files, including attack scripts, ransomware source code, extortion infrastructure, and an AI coding agent environment,” Seker said. “This gave us visibility into the operation behind the victim listings, including how the operator prepared attacks and handled stolen data.”</p><h3>Meet n0n</h3><p>It also provided a close look at <a href="https://socradar.io/free-tools/ransomware-intelligence/groups/n0n" rel="nofollow">n0n</a>, a newcomer to the data-theft and extortion game that has claimed 108 victims since it emerged last month. </p><p>On October 12, the group added <a href="https://socradar.io/blog/n0n-group-global-ports-shipping-leak/" rel="nofollow">38 maritime, port and logistics organizations</a> across 18 countries to its leak site in a single batch. <a href="https://cydome.io/n0n-cyber-extortion-incident-involving-38-maritime-companies-likely-traces-back-to-a-single-vendor/" rel="nofollow">Researchers</a> have linked at least 21 of them to customers of South Korean maritime software provider CyberLogitec, although the software provider has not confirmed an intrusion.</p><p>The crooks set an October 19 deadline for the maritime firms, with ransom demands ranging from $400,000 to $28 million, payable in Bitcoin. After the deadline, however, none of the 38 listings were marked “settled” and none of their payment addresses showed any transaction. All affected organizations’ stolen data was published.</p><p>While a handful of the gang’s claimed victims have been marked “honored - data destroyed” on n0n’s leak site, indicating the victim orgs paid the ransom demand, SOCRadar hasn’t validated any transactions on-chain. In its profile details, the security shop rates the gang a 10 out of 10 for "aggressiveness" and a zero out of 10 for “lethality.”</p><p>The group is believed to be based in Russia, although the server uncovered by SOCRadar, which it attributed to a single operator or small team, didn’t provide any definitive geographic or national attribution.</p><p>Every case that the exposed server documented followed the same lifecycle, moving from initial access to establishing persistence and credential collection via hidden admin accounts disguised as services, covert proxies, credential and session-token theft, mailboxes and emailed multi-factor authentication codes.</p><p>The crooks then collected sensitive data and sent everything to the operator’s central storage with the attack impact shifting between data-theft, encryption, destruction, and network disruption, depending on the targeted organization. </p><p>After breaking into Transcom, a Netherlands-based financial services provider, the crims sent a secondary ransom alert directly to PayPal's security team, even though the compromised data was only on Transcom's network.</p><h3>Stupid mistakes and n0n security basics</h3><p>Finally, the crims send ransom notes, post a countdown clock on the leak site, establish negotiation rooms and emails for victims - and then leak the stolen files or destroy backups when the organizations don’t pay up.
