Scientists find microplastics falling with snow in the Sierra Nevada

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Scientists measuring microplastic deposition across the Sierra Nevada during snowstorms found plastic particles at every single site they sampled.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The sites included the most remote locations, accessible only by chairlift or on skis, far beyond any road.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The findings matter well beyond the mountains. The <a href="https://www.earth.com/climate/effects-of-global-warming-may-cause-sierra-nevada-glaciers-to-vanish-this-century/">Sierra Nevada</a> snowpack supplies roughly 30% of California’s freshwater.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">What falls with the snow eventually makes its way into that supply.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-skiing-to-the-sampling-sites">Skiing to the sampling sites</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Lead researcher Aleksandra Karapetrova, an environmental toxicologist at the <a href="https://www.ucr.edu/">University of California, Riverside</a>, collected snow at seven locations across the Sierra Nevada during active storms. Getting there was difficult.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">“One site was accessed by chairlift. I had to ski to the rest of the remote sites. That’s how isolated they are,” she said. “But no matter how far out the site, I always found microplastics there.”</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The expectation had been that at least some of the most isolated sites would show little or no contamination. That expectation did not survive contact with the data.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Earlier research had already established that microplastics occur in Western US <a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/snowfall-pattern-discovered-that-could-change-forecasting-forever/">snow</a>. What this study added was a storm-by-storm accounting.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The scientists measured exactly how much plastic was being deposited during individual weather events. Such a method makes it possible to connect specific particle loads to specific atmospheric conditions.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-snow-sweeps-plastic-from-the-sky">How snow sweeps plastic from the sky</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/airborne-microplastics-where-they-come-from-and-where-they-go/">Microplastics</a> enter the atmosphere through mundane routes. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Clothing sheds fibers during daily wear or through dryer vents, wind lifts small fragments from the ground, and plastic products break down into particles small enough to become airborne. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Once suspended, they can travel considerable distances before anything brings them down.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Snow is particularly effective at collecting them. As snowflakes fall, they scavenge particles from the air around them.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">This process helps explain why the researchers measured substantially more plastic arriving with snowfall than during dry weather.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The storm is not just bringing water. It is sweeping the atmosphere clean of whatever is suspended in it, plastic included.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-what-the-analysis-revealed">What the analysis revealed</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">At remote sampling locations during the height of the storms, snow deposited between 270 and 3,200 plastic particles per square meter per day.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">At one site in the Mammoth Mountain ski area, where human activity is more concentrated and snowfall accumulation greater, the figure ran between 2,600 and 7,500 particles &#8211; roughly three times the remote-site range.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">“I didn’t think the plastics would stay suspended so much in the town,” Karapetrova said.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Even at that upper end, the actual mass of plastic is small. At the highest estimated deposition rate, snowfall over an acre would deliver about 1.5 grams of plastic per day.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The particle counts are what make the numbers striking, not the weight. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The findings suggest that even the Sierra Nevada&#8217;s most isolated locations are not beyond the reach of airborne plastic pollution.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-where-the-plastic-comes-from">Where the plastic comes from</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">To trace the origins of the particles, the researchers followed the air masses that brought each sampled storm into the Sierra Nevada.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Most had passed over Fresno and Madera counties in the Central Valley before arriving at the mountain sites.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That suggests the valley may be a significant source of the airborne plastic, though computer models could not pinpoint exact origins. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The Central Valley is one of the most intensively farmed and heavily trafficked regions in the American West. Plastic is used in <a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/agricultural-soil-is-now-the-worlds-biggest-plastic-dumping-ground/">agriculture</a>, packaging, and transport, generating abundant material for the wind to pick up and carry.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Identifying the particles required more than visual inspection under a microscope.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team used infrared spectroscopy to read each particle’s chemical signature. They then matched those signatures against a reference library to distinguish plastic from soil and plant material.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The most common types found were polyethylene, polypropylene, and polyester, or PET. Cellulose derivatives, chemically modified plant-based materials used in consumer products, also turned up.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-beyond-the-mountains">Beyond the mountains</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The study didn’t assess what fraction of this plastic reaches <a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/nearly-100-million-americans-exposed-to-contaminated-drinking-water/">drinking water</a>, nor did it evaluate health effects directly.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Although some plastic-associated chemicals have been linked to harmful biological effects, the study did not determine whether the particles found in Sierra Nevada snow pose risks to human health or <a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/pesticides-travel-far-and-wide-endangering-aquatic-ecosystems/">aquatic ecosystems</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">What the study makes clear is that atmospheric microplastic deposition is not confined to cities or coastlines or industrial zones. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">It is happening continuously, across an entire mountain range, carried by weather systems that have no regard for how remote a location is.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">“We are at a point where microplastics are in our atmosphere, not just the ocean, not just <a href="https://www.earth.com/environment/alarming-levels-of-microplastics-found-in-every-major-european-river/">rivers</a>. It comes down with the snowfall, making its way to places humans typically don’t go,” Karapetrova said.&nbsp;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">“We’ve reached a point where plastic pollution is falling out of the sky every day, across this entire region, even when it isn’t snowing.”</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The research is published in the journal <em><a href="https://pubs.acs.org/esthag/article-abstract/doi/10.1021/acs.est.6c02757/5458893/Event-Scale-Deposition-of-Atmospheric?redirectedFrom=fulltext">Environmental Science &amp; Technology</a></em>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8212;</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Like what you read? <a href="https://www.earth.com/subscribe/">Subscribe to our newsletter</a> for engaging articles, exclusive content, and the latest updates.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Check us out on <a href="https://www.earth.com/earthsnap/">EarthSnap</a>, a free app brought to you by <a href="https://www.earth.com/author/eralls/">Eric Ralls</a> and <a href="http://earth.com/">Earth.com</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8212;&#8212;-</p>

Over 25% of people lie to get antibiotics—and their lies aren't even good

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

<p>A significant number of patients in the US lie to their doctors to get antibiotics they likely don't need, according to two new studies. While that may not be a startling revelation, the studies drills down on the phenomenon, finding that the lies patients tell are generally bad ones—and yet, they work almost all the time.</p> <p>In the <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2856032">two</a> linked <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2856033">studies</a> published Friday in JAMA Network Open, a team of researchers led by Alistair Thorpe at the University of Utah analyzed online survey data from over 1,000 adults. They found that over a quarter of people reported having lied to a clinician at least once to get an antibiotic prescription for themselves or a child in their care. Patients lied by either exaggerating symptoms, making up symptoms, or—most often—both.</p> <p>But interestingly, the lies people told to get antibiotics involved making up or exaggerating symptoms of infections that wouldn't be treated with antibiotics. For example, the most common falsehoods were symptoms associated with colds, such as fever, cough, and sore throat. Basking sharks may be gathering in large groups for an unexpected reason

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

<p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking sharks are largely loners, and each one follows its own migration route. When big groups of them gather at the sea surface, the usual explanation is that they&#8217;ve found a rich patch of the tiny drifting animals they eat.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Food may be only part of the answer. In a new computer simulation, basking sharks formed groups that most closely resembled real gatherings when they searched for both food and each other.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The researchers read that as a sign the gatherings come from food and social attraction combined, not from food or chance alone.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Chelsea Gray, a researcher with the <a href="https://www.baskingshark.ie" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Irish Basking Shark Group</a>, led the work. She is also affiliated with George Mason University&#8217;s <a href="https://science.gmu.edu/academics/departments-units/environmental-science-policy" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Department of Environmental Science and Policy</a> in Virginia.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Every shark in the study is a set of rules inside a computer. The team calls the work a proof of concept, a first test of whether this kind of simulation can mimic basking shark behavior.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-crowds-put-sharks-at-risk">Crowds put sharks at risk</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-shark-has-bizarre-skin-dermal-denticles-very-unique/">sharks</a> are the second-largest fish alive, and they&#8217;re listed as endangered worldwide. They often feed right at the surface of coastal waters in the North Atlantic, which is where they&#8217;re most at risk from boats and fishing gear.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Basking shark gatherings range from pairs to <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-sharks-northeast-coast/">large groups</a> of hundreds. These groups often form during the busy season for boats, which raises the risk of <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/basking-sharks-are-exceptionally-vulnerable-to-ship-strikes/">boat strikes</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">What the gatherings are for is still unknown. Scientists have suggested feeding, courtship, or both, and some sharks in these groups swim in circles or side by side instead of feeding.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Where basking sharks mate or give birth is also a mystery. Only one pregnant female has ever been recorded caught in the North Atlantic, back in 1943.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In 2022, partly because of these gatherings, Ireland listed basking sharks under its Wildlife Act. The law forbids disturbing their feeding and breeding behavior.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-how-sharks-find-food-and-each-other">How sharks find food and each other</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Watching the gatherings directly is hard, because it depends on finding an elusive animal. So Gray&#8217;s team built 200 virtual sharks instead. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each one made its own choices once per simulated day, through every April-to-October season from 1982 to 2018.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Modelers call this kind of simulation an individual-based model, or IBM, and nobody had used one for basking sharks before.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">&#8220;Most importantly, it shows that an IBM can realistically replicate basking shark behavior,&#8221; Gray said.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team modeled more than 4,000 square miles (10,545 square kilometers) around the Inishowen Peninsula in County Donegal, divided into squares about 0.6 mile (1 kilometer) wide. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The sharks stayed near the surface, where the public sightings come from.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A virtual shark could sense food about 6 miles (10 kilometers) away and other sharks twice as far. If it went two weeks without enough food, it left the model and came back 20 days later.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Real basking sharks leave Irish surface waters and return later in the season, and the rule mimics that.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Earlier studies estimated the sharks&#8217; prey from the green pigment in plant plankton, and those estimates matched shark sightings unevenly. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gray&#8217;s team simulated the <a href="https://www.earth.com/climate/plankton-decline-leaves-north-atlantic-seas-in-poor-health/">zooplankton</a> directly, using counts from the <a href="https://www.cprsurvey.org" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Continuous Plankton Recorder Survey</a>, which the model scattered in patches.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-shark-gatherings-in-the-wild">Shark gatherings in the wild</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team ran four versions of the model. In one, sharks only looked for food. In another, they only looked for other sharks, heading for any square that held at least five sharks.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A third version combined the two, with food first. In the fourth, sharks swam at random as a check.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Each version ran 50 times. The team compared the results with real sightings collected by the <a href="https://iwdg.ie" rel="noreferrer noopener" target="_blank">Irish Whale and Dolphin Group</a> and Gray&#8217;s own group. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Anyone who saw a shark could send in a report, from fishers at sea to hikers on land.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Between 1982 and 2018, the two groups logged 2,136 sightings around Ireland, and 45% involved two or more sharks. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Around Inishowen alone there were 458 sightings, with the same share in groups.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Inishowen is a hot spot, and part of its coast is recognized internationally as an important area for sharks and rays.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">To keep the comparison fair, the model also mimicked casual sightings. Each simulated day, it checked 20 random squares and logged any shark it found there.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-food-and-social-attraction-work-together">Food and social attraction work together</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Virtual sharks that only followed each other, or only swam at random, almost never formed groups bigger than two. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With nothing in the water drawing several sharks to the same spot, they kept heading toward whichever neighbors were close.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The food-only sharks had the opposite problem. They formed far more groups than the combined version did, and more large groups than the sightings recorded. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The number of groups also rose and fell in a steady seasonal rhythm.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The real sightings follow the seasons too, but unevenly. Virtual sharks that sought both food and company gathered in the same irregular way. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">They also came closest to the sightings, both in how many groups formed each month and in how big those groups were.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The match wasn&#8217;t perfect. In the model, average group size tended to rise a little later than it did in the real records.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Comparing the versions, the authors concluded that food set how many groups formed in the model. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Attraction to other sharks affected how large those groups grew. Social attraction alone, they wrote, isn&#8217;t enough to explain the crowds people report.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-patchy-food-may-draw-crowds">Patchy food may draw crowds</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In early tests, the model matched the sightings more closely when fewer than 20% of its squares held zooplankton. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When half or more of the squares held food, the food-only and combined versions behaved almost the same.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The authors suggested that the Inishowen coast may have especially patchy prey, and that patchy prey makes gatherings more likely.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In a sea where food is scattered, a group of sharks may work as a signal that food is nearby. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The virtual sharks could detect each other from farther away than they could detect food, so a crowd could be noticed before the meal that drew it.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading" id="h-sound-nbsp-may-nbsp-attract-nbsp-sharks">Sound&nbsp;may&nbsp;attract&nbsp;sharks</h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Earlier studies have suggested that breaching, a leap clear of the water, may let basking sharks signal to each other over long distances. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Sharks can <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/sharks-are-listening-to-the-ocean-from-surprisingly-far-away/">sense sound</a> from much farther away than they may be able to pick up smells.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The authors wrote that their results may lend support to the idea that sound matters, either as a sign of prey or as a social signal. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">If so, <a href="https://www.earth.com/animals/boat-noise-pollution-humpback-whale-songs-difficult-to-hear-panama-ocean-coast/">underwater noise</a> from human activity might affect how basking sharks navigate and find food or mates, as it does for marine mammals.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Gray&#8217;s team had no local measurements of where zooplankton gathers around Inishowen, and the model scattered its prey at random each day. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">For 79% of the days studied, the plankton survey had no sample at all.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The team filled sightings missed many groups. Real reports depend on the public, and Gray&#8217;s own earlier survey in Inishowen found that most people didn&#8217;t know they could report a shark.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The virtual sharks have no age or sex, and they never dive below 33 feet (10 meters). Eating disorders are on the rise. GLP-1s could make things worse.

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Health  |  Read Source →

<div></div> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">GLP-1 weight-loss drugs are one of the biggest biotech breakthroughs of the last decade. But to some clinical psychologists, they can represent something more sinister—a potential trigger for eating disorders.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">A new report suggests that the global incidence of anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa shot up between 1990 and 2023—by a whopping 71%. By 2023, there were an estimated 18.1 million people living with one of these two disorders.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">What’s worse is that the number is likely to be much higher than that once we factor in other eating disorders. And, disturbingly, those in the field are already starting to see GLP-1 weight-loss drugs exacerbating the issue.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Cheri Levinson is a clinical psychologist at the University of Louisville in Kentucky who has specialized in eating disorders for almost 20 years. She says she’s now seeing more patients with more severe symptoms than at any other time in her career, and that many of them are misusing GLP-1s. “This is the worst I’ve seen things,” she says.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Eating disorders are mental health conditions in which people’s eating behaviors impact their health. They include anorexia nervosa (defined by a fear of weight gain), bulimia nervosa (in which people binge and purge), binge eating disorder, and others.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">It’s thought that some people have a genetic predisposition to certain eating disorders, but that some external factor is what triggers their onset. That might be stress, or social pressure to be skinny. Often, simply restricting food intake can trigger an eating disorder, says Levinson.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Eating disorders—along with the pressure to look thin—were already prevalent in the 1990s (the&nbsp;<a href="https://www.cosmopolitan.com/lifestyle/a73344972/fashion-week-smoking-skinny-heroin-chic/">heroin chic</a> era springs to mind). To find out how things might have changed since then, Ruhai Bai at the Children’s Hospital of Nanjing Medical University in China and his colleagues pulled together data from studies and surveys conducted on populations around the world. The team used statistical models to fill in any data gaps.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">That research allowed the researchers to estimate the annual incidence of two eating disorders—anorexia nervosa and bulimia nervosa—for each of 204 countries and territories between 1990 and 2023. They also estimated the impact those disorders had on global health by calculating their disability-adjusted life years (one&nbsp;<a href="https://www.who.int/data/gho/data/themes/mortality-and-global-health-estimates/global-health-estimates-leading-causes-of-dalys/">DALY</a> represents a year of healthy life lost).</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The findings, <a href="https://media.jamanetwork.com/media-releases/10.1001/jamapsychiatry.2026.3457">published in <em>JAMA Psychiatry</em></a> on Wednesday, are unsettling. Between 1990 and 2023, there was a 71% increase in the incidence of those two eating disorders, the team found. Those conditions had a huge impact on global health, too. In 2023, they “cost the world the equivalent of about 3.83 million years of healthy life,” says Bai.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Bai and his colleagues expect these figures to continue to rise. In 2023, an estimated 18.1 million people had either anorexia nervosa or bulimia nervosa; by 2050, the team predict, the two conditions could affect 21.8 million people.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">It gets worse. Those two conditions represent less than half of all eating disorders, says Levinson, who was not involved in Bai’s study. The true impact of these disorders is “probably three times the rate that they’re reporting,” she says.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Bai and his colleagues didn’t explore the reasons for this rise (although Bai points out that it can’t be entirely explained by population growth). So how did we get here?</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>Social media may have played a role.</strong> “Social media comparisons, influencers, challenges, diet trends can all be precipitating … events for an eating disorder,” says Cynthia Bulik, a clinical psychologist at the University of North Carolina, who was not involved in the new study. Bulik has been studying and treating eating disorders since the early 1980s. “Between 1990 and 2023 there has clearly been an upsurge of those types of environmental exposures,” she says.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Many social media users are exposed to filtered and modified images of skinny influencers who may have undergone cosmetic surgery or extreme dieting and who are promoting “thinspiration” or “fitspiration” content. But users with eating disorders appear to be more likely to engage with this content—and to be fed more of it as a result.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When researchers at the University of Melbourne&nbsp;<a href="https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S1740144524001293">investigated this phenomenon</a> a couple of years ago, they found that the algorithms of TikTok users with eating disorders delivered 4343% more “toxic eating disorder videos” than those of users who didn’t have the disorders. They also got more content focused on dieting or exercising. Many were fed over 1000 problematic videos per month. Bulik describes social media as “quicksand” for people with, or at risk of, eating disorders.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The covid-19 pandemic—and the social isolation that accompanied lockdowns—also appears to have contributed to the rise in eating disorders. Research conducted in 2021 found that the number of people hospitalized with eating disorders in the US <a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamanetworkopen/fullarticle/2786185"><em>doubled</em> between 2018 and 2020</a>.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Researchers don’t yet have good data on how trends in eating disorders might have progressed since 2023. But anecdotally, things seem to be getting even worse. “There are more eating disorders, more severe eating disorders… and we’re seeing earlier ages of onset,” says Levinson. “We’re seeing eight- and nine-year-olds with eating disorders.”</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>“From what I’m hearing and seeing, I am not going to be surprised if that number isn’t at least doubling again,” she adds.</strong></p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Levinson particularly worries about the impact of GLP-1 weight-loss drugs, which have exploded in popularity in recent years. A <a href="https://news.gallup.com/poll/712157/glp-usage-reaches-new-high.aspx">Gallup survey</a> conducted earlier this year suggested that 15% of Americans had used one of these drugs for weight loss. And they’re becoming more accessible—drug companies are working on new formulations that are both easier to take and more effective, and many people can buy versions of these drugs from compound pharmacies or dubious online sources.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">In&nbsp;<a href="https://jamanetwork.com/journals/jamapsychiatry/fullarticle/2850584">a small study</a> published earlier this year, Levinson and her colleagues found that 30% of people with eating disorders had used GLP-1 drugs. The drugs might benefit some of those people. But 10% appeared to have misused the drugs. And Levinson thinks that figure is also an underestimate.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Levinson says she and her colleagues have seen children develop eating disorders after being prescribed GLP-1s for medical reasons. They’ve seen others—even children as young as 11—buy versions of these drugs over TikTok.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><strong>“We’re very concerned that GLPs will lead to even faster and more extreme weight loss</strong> in people with anorexia nervosa, and that patients will come to us in even more advanced stages of illness that become difficult to reverse,” says Bulik.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Both Bulik and Levinson are continuing to study the impacts of these drugs in eating disorders. In the meantime, both urge medical professionals to consider the risks GLP-1s might pose to people with those disorders. Bulik contributed to a set of&nbsp;<a href="https://onlinelibrary.wiley.com/doi/10.1002/wps.70096">research and clinical guidelines</a>, published earlier this month, that recommends doctors screen for eating disorders before prescribing the drugs, for example. The guidelines also recommend more patient education and more robust monitoring of people who take GLP-1s.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>If you think you or someone you know might have an eating disorder, you can find information and support at</em> <a href="https://www.nationaleatingdisorders.org/"><em>the National Eating Disorders Association</em></a><em>.</em></p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><em>This article first appeared in The Checkup, </em>MIT Technology Review’s<em> weekly biotech newsletter. The 12 strangest science stories we can’t stop thinking about
