<p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.footballtransfertavern.com/manchester-united-transfer-rumours/">Manchester United</a> could be forced to strengthen their attacking options in January after another injury setback involving Benjamin Sesko. </p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">With Michael Carrick needing additional firepower to maintain competition for places up front, an experienced Premier League striker has reportedly emerged as a surprise target.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Leeds United forward Dominic Calvert-Lewin is among the names being considered by United’s recruitment team. However, attempting to sign a key player from one of their biggest rivals could prove extremely difficult.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>Manchester United</strong> <strong>Consider Dominic Calvert-Lewin</strong></h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph"><a href="https://www.nytimes.com/athletic/7735103/2026/10/29/manchester-united-dominic-calvert-lewin-leeds-transfer/?unlocked_article_code=1.MVE.v2-J.Hk5D6BgNqx5m&amp;source=twitteruk">According to The Athletic</a>, Manchester United are exploring the January market for centre-forwards, with Calvert-Lewin one of the players under consideration.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The 29-year-old England international has reportedly been on United’s radar for some time, and recruitment staff are now assessing whether a potential transfer would be feasible when the window opens.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The interest comes amid concerns over Benjamin Sesko’s availability. The Slovenian striker is expected to miss an extended period with a shin injury, leaving Carrick with fewer recognised centre-forward options.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Calvert-Lewin has rebuilt his career since joining Leeds on a free transfer from Everton in 2025. His performances at Elland Road have attracted renewed attention, with the striker scoring 20 goals in 49 appearances for the club across all competitions.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">He has also returned to England international contention following his improved form, making him an appealing option for United as they consider their attacking requirements.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">However, there are significant obstacles. Leeds are reportedly keen to reward Calvert-Lewin with a new contract, despite his current agreement running until 2028. The club are also understood to be protective of a player who has become an important part of Daniel Farke’s side.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">The rivalry between Leeds and Manchester United presents another major complication. A transfer between the clubs would attract considerable attention, and reports suggest the possibility of an agreement is currently remote.</p> <h2 class="wp-block-heading"><strong>What This Means for Manchester United</strong></h2> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Calvert-Lewin could offer United a different profile in attack, bringing physical presence, aerial ability and considerable Premier League experience. His availability could become particularly important if Sesko remains sidelined into the January window.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">United have previously considered alternative centre-forward options, but the latest developments could encourage the recruitment department to widen its search. Calvert-Lewin’s experience in English football would potentially make him an attractive short-term solution.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Nevertheless, Leeds are unlikely to make negotiations straightforward. They have little reason to weaken their squad midway through the campaign, particularly by allowing an important player to join their historic rivals.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">United must also consider whether pursuing Calvert-Lewin is realistic when other striker targets may be easier to acquire.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">For now, the reported interest represents an option under consideration rather than an imminent transfer. In Dark Eden, I imagined how life might exist on a sunless planet

📅 Oct 30, 2026  |  Category: Business  |  Read Source →

<figure class="wp-block-image size-full"><img alt="The Milky Way galaxy" class="wp-image-2596454" height="900" src="https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2026/10/SEI317457767.jpg" width="1350" /><figcaption class="wp-element-caption"><div class="wp-image__caption--icon"><svg height="15" viewBox="0 0 15 15" width="15" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg"><use></use></svg></div><div class="wp-image__caption--text">Eden, the planet in Chris Beckett’s Dark Eden, drifts outside the Milky Way</div><div class="wp-image__caption--credit">McCarthy's PhotoWorks/Shutterstock</div></figcaption></figure> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">I came up with the idea of the planet Eden in 1992 for a short story called <a href="https://archive.org/details/Interzone_056_1992-02/page/n41"><em>The Circle of Stones</em></a>. At that time, I had no idea that there really were such things as planets without suns, but it seemed likely to me. It also seemed possible to me that life might arise on such a planet, since, even here on Earth, whole ecologies thrive without sunlight around <a href="https://www.newscientist.com/article/2254516-deep-sea-hydrothermal-vents-team-with-life-how-are-they-colonised/">thermal vents</a> in the deep ocean. Life on Earth may have even have begun in such places.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Quite often, if something seems likely, it does indeed happen. (In another <a href="https://atlantic-books.co.uk/book/america-city/">book</a>, I predicted that a US president would one day seek to take over Greenland and Canada.) Having written that story, I learned that <a href="https://www.newscientist.com/article/2509858-rare-saturn-sized-rogue-planet-is-first-to-have-its-mass-measured/">free-floating planets</a> did indeed exist. Being dark, they’re hard to spot, but a number have been detected using <a href="https://www.newscientist.com/article/2482767-peer-into-the-early-universe-as-light-bends-around-huge-galaxy-cluster/">gravitational lensing</a>. And I gather it’s not particularly controversial to think that some of them might support life (though not quite in the form that I chose to imagine it – but I’ll come to that).</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When creating a world for a story, I don’t usually seek to make the whole thing scientifically accurate, but I try to make it hang together in a way that feels coherent. I didn’t build Eden by doing a lot of research, but I did put a lot of thought into how it might all work. This wasn’t so I could put it all in the book – no one in Eden knows this stuff and the story is told entirely by its characters! – but so that I had something to guide my imagination and make my invented world feel coherent.</p> <div class="advert-wrapper advert__mid-article"> <div class="advert"> <div class="advert__inner"> <p class="advert__copy"> Advertisement </p> <div class="advert__slot" id="mid-article-slot"> </div> </div> </div> </div> <div class="wp-block-newscientist-newsletter-promotion"> <div class="newsletter-promotion__content"> <p class="newsletter-promotion__tagline">Free newsletter</p> <h4 class="newsletter-promotion__title"> Sign up to Book Club </h4> <p class="newsletter-promotion__excerpt"> </p> <div class="newsletter-promotion__actions"> <a class="button button--secondary button--has-icon" href="https://www.newscientist.com/sign-up/bookclub/"> <span>Sign up to newsletter</span> <svg height="18" viewBox="0 0 24 24" width="18" xmlns="http://www.w3.org/2000/svg"> <use></use> </svg> </a> </div> </div> <div class="newsletter-promotion__background"> <span class="newsletter-promotion__gradient"></span> <div class="newsletter-promotion__image__wrapper"> <div class="image__wrapper"> <img alt="" class="attachment-4x3-sm size-4x3-sm wp-post-image" height="450" src="https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/06/16102039/bookclub_2025_ed_newsletter_landingtiles_2400px8.jpg?w=600&amp;h=450&amp;crop=1" width="600" /> </div> </div> </div> </div> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">My idea was that Eden had once been covered in ice, but its hot core meant there was liquid water below the ice in which life could come into existence. One particular kind of life form appeared, somewhat resembling tree roots, that was able to obtain additional energy by pushing deep down into hot rocks, pumping its sap through them to heat it, and then pumping it back up again, like a geothermal power station. One of the ways these organisms used this heat was to push upwards into the planet’s shell of ice, melting parts of it, feeding on the nutrients it contained, and creating more living space – and they carried on doing so until much of the surface of Eden was ice-free, and had an atmosphere that would turn out to be breathable by humans.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Millions of years later, this thawed-out surface, now populated by a multitude of different life forms, became the stage on which my drama could unfold: first that early story, then another story and then the novel <em>Dark Eden</em> and its two sequels. In <em>Dark Eden</em>, a tiny community of humans, descended from stranded visitors from Earth, eke out a living using tools made of stone and wood, and try to make sense of their life as exiles from a sunlit world that lives on in folk memory, but which they can only very shakily imagine. (In the biblical story of Eden, humans are exiled <em>from</em> Eden – my Eden <em>is</em> the place of exile.) Their stories tell them that Earth people could fly through the air, that there was a thing there called leckytrickity, that Earth animals had eyes that you could look into and see their minds looking back out at you, not like the flat, blank eyes of Eden creatures.</p> <div class="read-more__standard wp-block-newscientist-read-more"> <a class="read-more__standard--wrapper no-animation" href="https://www.newscientist.com/article/2433037-our-writers-pick-their-favourite-science-fiction-books-of-all-time/"> <strong>Read more</strong> <p>Our writers pick their favourite science fiction books of all time</p> </a> </div> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">On the surface of Eden, those root-like life forms put up structures that Eden’s humans call “trees”. A whole ecosystem has evolved around them, including “starflowers” that parasitise their roots. (These, as it happens, turn out to be a rich source of Vitamin D. Phew! What a stroke of luck!) And there are many animals – or quote-unquote “animals”, strictly speaking, since they’re not related to the animal kingdom of Earth. There’s no sunlight in Eden (and therefore no alternation of day and night) but, like organisms that live in the ocean depths of Earth, Eden’s life has developed bioluminescence. The “trees” have luminous “lanternflowers”; the starflowers glitter. Imagine a leafless forest on a moonless night, lit by thousands of Chinese lanterns. Then add in twinkling fairy lights at ground level. With your eyes half-closed, the forests of Eden would look something like that.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Just as life on Earth depends on plants that turn sunlight into chemical energy, so the life of Eden depends on geothermal trees. They bring heat to the surface to power their metabolisms, they keep the air warm and, if you live on Eden, the steady “<em>Hmmmph hmmmph hmmmph</em>” of their constant pumping is the background of your waking and sleeping life, merging in the distance to a steady hum. All the trees are hot, and some of them are so hot that you’d burn yourself if you touched their trunks. If you cut down a tree, you have to watch out for the spurt of scalding liquid that bursts out.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">When I read stories set on alien planets, I’m sometimes disappointed in the “animals” (I’ll drop the inverted commas now). Writers often seem to dream up a motley collection of bizarre creatures without much regard for how they might actually survive or how they are related to one another. I tried to design my animals on evolutionary principles, giving thought to how they were adapted to their environment, the role they played in the ecosystem and how they might plausibly have evolved. Animals on Earth fall into a large but finite number of basic body plans. For example, monkeys, cows, crocodiles and seagulls all have four limbs and, though some descendants of quadrupeds have lost their limbs (snakes, whales), there are no examples of quadrupeds developing extra ones. In the same way, large animals on Eden all have a six-limbed body plan. There are other animals that don’t conform to this, just as there are arthropods, et cetera on Earth, but the large animals are all based on this six-limbed pattern.</p> <div class="read-more__with-image wp-block-newscientist-read-more"> <a class="read-more__with-image--wrapper no-animation" href="https://www.newscientist.com/article/2505482-the-12-best-science-fiction-books-of-2025/"> <div class="read-more__with-image--image"> <img alt="Astronaut sits in a window frame inside a space ship and reads a book about science. Concept of exploring space." class="attachment-3x2-md size-3x2-md wp-post-image" height="600" src="https://www.newscientist.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/11/24104456/SEI_274683690.jpg?w=900&amp;h=600&amp;crop=1" width="900" /> </div> <div class="read-more__with-image--link-text"> <span>The 12 best science fiction books of 2025</span> </div> <div class="read-more__with-image--strapline"> <p>From drowned worlds to virtual utopias via deep space, wild ideas abound in Emily H. Wilson’s picks for her favourite sci-fi reads of the year </p> </div> </a> </div> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">I’ve already mentioned how different Eden eyes are from the eyes of terrestrial animals. They have evolved alongside the evolution of bioluminescence, rather in the way that colourful flowers and nectar-eating insects have evolved alongside one another on Earth. Eden trees use luminous lanternflowers to attract the creatures that help them reproduce, just as flowering plants on Earth use colour and scent.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">I gave a lot of thought to the biology of Eden, but I have to admit that, in strictly scientific terms, my imaginary world isn’t entirely viable. Free-floating planets with warm cores? Yes! Not just possible, but a certainty! But free-floating planets with warm <em>surfaces</em>? Sadly no. More than one astronomer has told me they just couldn’t happen. No matter how many radiator-like trees there might be, I’ve been told, it still wouldn’t be enough to maintain liquid water or a warm atmosphere.</p> <p class="wp-block-paragraph">Let’s call it “fiction science”, science made up for a story, which works in some places and in others allows itself poetic licence. Almost all science fiction uses fiction science and <em>Dark Eden</em> contains several other examples. I took some liberties with human genetics, for instance. Kalshi&#8217;s case targets Missouri, while Polymarket US went after Louisiana. The CFTC filed its own suit against Utah that same day.</p> <p>Kalshi is challenging a cease-and-desist demand from Missouri. The state told it to pull its sports event contracts or get a state sports betting license. Polymarket US filed as QCX LLC. It named Louisiana&#8217;s attorney general and the Louisiana Gaming Control Board as defendants.</p> <p>Both companies make the same core argument. They say federal commodities law, the Commodity Exchange Act, overrides state gambling enforcement.</p> <h2><span id="missouri_treats_event_contracts_as_unlicensed_sports_wagering">Missouri treats event contracts as unlicensed sports wagering</span></h2> <p>Missouri&#8217;s position is direct. The demand to Kalshi came from the office of Attorney General Catherine Hanaway, and Kalshi was not its only target.</p> <p>The office says that offering sports event contracts in Missouri counts as unlicensed sports wagering. It also holds that federal law does not override state law here. In its view, sports event contracts are not swaps under the Commodity Exchange Act. Companies &#8220;cannot repackage sports bets as event contracts&#8221; to get around Missouri law, Hanaway said.</p> <p>The stakes for the licensed market are concrete. Sports wagering became legal in Missouri through Amendment 2. Oversight of the licensed books sits with the Missouri Gaming Commission.</p> <p>The commission&#8217;s July 2026 revenue detail report shows the scale of that business. A prediction market selling sports contracts without a license would pay none of that tax. It would compete for the same customers as the licensed books.</p> <h2><span id="louisianas_casino-first_licensing_faces_a_federal_challenge">Louisiana&#8217;s casino-first licensing faces a federal challenge</span></h2> <p>Louisiana&#8217;s regulated market works differently. Its licenses go first to in-state casinos. Untaxed sports contracts there would compete with those casinos and their sportsbook partners.</p> <p>The Louisiana Gaming Control Board had already acted on the issue. In December 2025, it sent an advisory on prediction markets and event-based contracts. The advisory went to all sports wagering licensees and platform provider permittees.</p> <p>That approach targets the licensed side of the market. Polymarket US&#8217;s suit now tests whether the state can reach the prediction market operators directly.</p> <p>The federal regulator has taken a firm public line.
