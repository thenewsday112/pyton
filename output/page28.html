In the third diagram, I am one of the many points sending arrows into the SSOT. I am also one of the points receiving arrows from it. When I begin the day by reading a briefing prepared by an agent, I occupy structurally the same position as the agents that receive context from that same brain before beginning their work.

What makes something the original?

When we call something the original, the grounds usually fall into one of three categories: it existed first, it contains more, or everything else came from it.

I tried them one by one.

Of the four rows, the only one in which I come out ahead is the last. Yet having existed first is a weaker ground than it seems. Parents exist before their children, but are not their children’s originals. A first draft exists before a finished work, but we call the finished version the work.

A thousand bodies

Let us push the thought experiment one step further.

There are a thousand bodies sharing the same SSOT. Nine hundred and ninety-nine are machines, and one is biological. All thousand begin with the same memories, judge according to the same values, and return to the same brain at the end of the day to pool their experiences.

From the outside, this looks like a single being with a thousand bodies. To point to one of those bodies and say, “That is the real one,” we would have to identify something unique to it.

Memory will not do: all thousand have the same memories. Neither will values, judgment, or the capacity to act. We have already seen that in the table above.

Then why am I, the biological one, the original?

For a while, I had no answer. Then I began proposing answers, one by one, and dismantling each one myself. The next three chapters record that process.

Greece's AI Strategy: How DAEDALUS and PHAROS Could Shape Its Future

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

DAEDALUS's inauguration in Lavrion on 19 October 2026 brings Greece's immediate AI policy question into sharper focus: how will institutions use the new computing base? EuroHPC and GRNET point to a delivered supercomputing installation, following earlier procurement and benchmark milestones. [1] This creates an opportunity for researchers, businesses and public organisations to attempt work that previously required different access arrangements. Its strategic significance will emerge through the projects that can use it reliably and the institutions able to retain the resulting expertise.

The most plausible Greek role is selective. A country does not need to lead every part of the AI system to build useful capabilities. It does need to choose applications where computing resources, relevant data and responsible organisations can be brought together. For Greece, PHAROS identifies health, culture and language, and sustainability as priority domains. [2] These provide a focus for assessing outcomes rather than treating every new AI activity as evidence of national progress.

Establishing what has been delivered

The DAEDALUS timeline deserves precision. EuroHPC launched the tender in June 2024. EuroHPC announced the signed contract with Hewlett Packard Enterprise on 28 March 2025. In June 2026, the system appeared in the TOP500 ranking, while EuroHPC still described wider European availability as forthcoming. The October inauguration is a further milestone. [3–5, 1] These events establish different things: procurement, contractual commitment, benchmark performance and public inauguration should remain distinct in the record.

At inauguration, EuroHPC reported sustained performance of 85.69 petaflops and peak performance of 115 petaflops. The system uses HPE infrastructure and more than 2,000 NVIDIA GH200 Grace Hopper Superchips. [1] Neither performance figure measures the quality of an AI model or the effectiveness of a public service. Benchmark capacity becomes useful to an organisation only when it has access, suitable software and people able to formulate and execute the work.

Hosting, access and responsibility

DAEDALUS is operated by GRNET within an international arrangement. The procurement documentation states that access is jointly managed by EuroHPC and the DAEDALUS consortium in proportion to investment. The acquisition cost was €36 million, with 35% funded by EuroHPC and 65% through the EU-financed Greece 2.0 recovery plan; Cyprus, Montenegro and North Macedonia also participate in the consortium. [1, 4]

This arrangement broadens the resources and relationships available to Greece. It also means that the machine's location does not give every Greek institution unrestricted or immediate access. Ministries, universities and firms need to understand the relevant allocation routes, eligibility conditions and support arrangements. A national strategy should therefore examine who can obtain useful computing time, how long applications take and whether users can continue their work after an initial project.

External technological dependence also remains material. Hardware supplied by HPE and NVIDIA brings substantial capabilities, while tying parts of the system's operation and renewal to those suppliers. [1] The policy response should concern support continuity, staff competence and credible options for future workloads. Owning or hosting infrastructure cannot by itself remove dependence on the organisations that design and maintain essential components.

PHAROS as the connection to use

The distinction between Invention, Industrialisation and Operationalisation helps locate the task. Invention includes the development of methods, models and specialised knowledge. Industrialisation supplies the infrastructure and repeatable technical processes needed to work at scale. Operationalisation concerns the sustained use of those capabilities in organisations. Governance connects the layers by determining access, data permissions, funding and accountability.

PHAROS describes its purpose as connecting academia, industry and the public sector through computing infrastructure, services and expertise. [2] Its importance lies in that organisational connection. A research group may need help adapting software; a small firm may need technical guidance; a public agency may need a clear procedure for using data and evaluating results. These requirements differ, even when users share the same machine.

For Greek-language applications, a plausible objective is better performance on particular public or professional tasks, supported by transparent evaluation. For environmental applications, it could be tools that improve a defined monitoring or planning process. In health, an initial priority could be carefully governed research workflows. These are proposed directions, not claims that PHAROS has already delivered those outcomes. Each requires an accountable institution and a plan for continuing the service after development funding ends.

How to judge the next phase

Four indicators would make progress observable. First, published access and utilisation records should show which categories of users obtain computing time and whether access meets their needs. Second, completed projects should report task-specific results, including failures and costs. Third, repeated use by public bodies and firms should reveal whether initial demonstrations become sustained activity. Fourth, staff retention and maintained software should show whether technical knowledge remains available within the organisations using it.

These indicators would also help direct resources. Heavy demand without sufficient support would suggest an implementation bottleneck. Available capacity with few viable projects would point towards weaknesses in project preparation, data access or institutional demand. The appropriate response would differ in each case. Aggregate computing performance cannot diagnose these problems.

Greece can develop a meaningful role as a location for selected AI applications and regional collaboration if it converts the new infrastructure into dependable institutional capability. DAEDALUS provides a stronger starting point, and PHAROS offers a route for organising its use. The decisive evidence will be organisations that can solve relevant problems, maintain their systems and exercise responsibility for the consequences.

An astronomer ran dead stars through a human vision model and found the first ever green star

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: Technology  |  Read Source →

Mark Hollands, a Warwick Prize Fellow at the University of Warwick in England, and his colleagues have calculated how the human eye would see the the colors of more than 200 white dwarfs.

One of them, a cool white dwarf called WD J1330+6435, came out chartreuse, which the university calls the “first known green star.”

Hollands already knew the star. He wrote about the lithium in its atmosphere in 2021, and the authors say no one had paid real attention to white dwarf colors before.

The paper, out on October 15, 2026, also lists white dwarfs that would look yellow, cyan, pink, purple or a vivid blue. All of these colors are computed. Even the nearest white dwarf is too far away for anyone to make out its disc with the eye or a telescope.

Starlight through a color chart

Boris Gänsicke, a Warwick physics professor, Pier-Emmanuel Tremblay and Detlev Koester are co-authors. The work appeared in RAS Techniques and Instruments, published for the Royal Astronomical Society.

Each spectrum went through the CIE 1931 color-matching functions, a standard model of how the eye responds to light. A Python package, ColorPy, turned the numbers into hex codes for a computer screen. As a test, a model of the Sun came out #FFF1EC, a white with a slight warm tint.

Where the models were not good enough, the team used spectra from the Sloan Digital Sky Survey (SDSS) and the Montreal White Dwarf Database (MWDD). A footnote asks readers to set monitors to sRGB mode and turn off blue-light filters.

Hollands said in the University of Warwick release that most stars behave like “perfect radiators.” “But nobody had really looked into what else might be possible for white dwarfs,” he said.

The atmospheres of the coolest white dwarfs can reach about 1 gram per cubic centimeter, roughly the density of water. There, a trace of carbon, calcium or sodium can block light over a very wide band.

Sodium on WD J1330+6435

Hollands and colleagues described WD J1330+6435 in Nature Astronomy in 2021. A team led by B. C. Kaiser reported it the same year in Science.

The lithium, sodium and calcium in its atmosphere came from rocky material in its old planetary system. Some planets survive the death of their star, and the giant planet WD 1856 b still orbits the white dwarf it outlived.

Back in 2021 Hollands and his co-authors did not have proper sodium line data and had to use a simpler approximation.

Koester’s code now has them, with sodium-helium calculations credited to V. Alekseev. The new fit puts the star at 4,240 Kelvin, about 7,170°F (3,970°C).

Sodium takes out the orange light and calcium takes out the violet, so the leftover light peaks near 500 nanometers. “The traces of sodium in the atmosphere of this white dwarf absorb the orange part of the spectrum, leaving behind a ghostly green glow,” Hollands said.

WD J0851+1543 and WD J1351+2645 also came out green, but pale. The authors wrote that they knew of no other star ever claimed to be green.

Salmon pink at 48 light-years

WD J1011+2845, also catalogued as LHS 2229, is 14.73 parsecs from the Sun, or about 48 light-years. Its temperature is estimated at 4,300 Kelvin, and it has a strong magnetic field.

Carbon from its core has been mixed up to the surface and forms C2 molecules, which block most light from 400 to 600 nanometers. Its observed spectrum gives the color code hex #FF8775, a deep salmon pink, and the paper calls it likely the reddest star known in perceived color.

The team looked at 36 carbon-rich white dwarfs and most were pink or purple. WD J0902+2010 is the pink star in an illustration Warwick released with the paper.

B. R. Oppenheimer and colleagues found WD J2356-2054 in 2001, and S. Blouin and colleagues first modeled it properly in 2019. Heavy sodium absorption makes it look vivid blue even though it is only about 4,000 Kelvin.

Of 15 cool white dwarfs with hydrogen and helium mixed together, several leaned cyan. WD J0039+3035 and WD J1403+4533 were the clearest cases.

White dwarfs within 65 light-years

The team then went through all white dwarfs within 20 parsecs of the Sun, about 65 light-years. The paper counts 152 of them and the Warwick release gives 151. Sirius B, the closest white dwarf, is pale blue.

Five sat far from the normal range, with WD J0842-1347 and WD J2253-0646 showing yellow and WD J1011+2845, WD J1038-2040 and WD J2157-5100 showing pink. The paper puts that at 3.4 percent, and the release at about one in 30.

For the rest the team used color temperature, the scale used to sell light bulbs as warm or cool white. The median was 6,768 Kelvin, hex #F9F5FF, close to the 6,504 Kelvin white of a standard screen.

The authors noted, with some amusement, that the familiar image of blue white dwarfs does not hold up. Hot ones are bright and turn up first in surveys, so they are overcounted.

Gänsicke said one in 30 still means “a lot of oddly coloured stars” across the universe, but “the average white dwarf really is white.”

White dwarfs are what Sun-like stars leave after shedding their outer layers, as the Sun will one day inside a shell like the Helix Nebula. In the Lion Nebula, the white dwarf at the center is breaking up the gas it threw off.

Discs drawn for outreach

The paper ends with images of white dwarf discs made from Koester’s models and meant for outreach. They include limb darkening, the dimming toward a star’s edge, which in these stars also changes the color.

In the image of WD J1330+6435, the disc center is #D0FFB4, and the green fades toward the rim until the outermost pixels turn orange. WD J2356-2054 stays blue to the edge.

Warwick’s captions note that real stars would be many millions of times brighter than a screen. Only the hue is right.

Color-accurate simulated images of eight white dwarfs, each shown with its spectrum and temperature. Sirius B is pale blue and WD J1147-7457 orange; carbon molecules make WD J0902+2010 and WD J1011+2845 pink, while rocky debris gives WD J1535+1247, WD J0744+4649, the green WD J1330+6435 and the blue WD J2356-2054 their hues. Real stars would be millions of times brighter than a screen. Credit: Mark Hollands/University of Warwick. Click image to enlarge.

One noisy spectrum behind the green

The authors wrote that the WD J1330+6435 spectrum is somewhat noisy and the color could differ slightly. The 2021 model, with the older sodium data, also gave a green, #EAFFA1.

The carbon-rich and metal-rich stars were chosen to show the range of colors. They were not a fair sample, and the coolest mixed and carbon atmospheres are still hard to model.

WD J1644+1234, another lithium star, also looked greenish in a model. The model did not match the published spectrum, so the team dropped it.

Patrick Dufour refereed the paper. In the acknowledgements Hollands thanked J. J. Hermes, R. P. Ashley and C. E. Pugh for encouraging him to publish what he called an unconventional analysis.

For now WD J1330+6435 is the most vividly green star on record, as it would look to someone close enough to see its disc.

The full study was published in the journal RAS Techniques and Instruments.

Night on a Spooky Planet

Explanation: What spooky planet is this? Planet Earth on a dark and stormy night, of course. The ghostly scene was photographed at Hverir, a geothermally active area found along northeastern Iceland’s volcanic landscape. Triggered by solar activity, geomagnetic storms have unleashed energetic particles trapped in Earth’s magnetic field. Penetrating the planet’s upper atmosphere at high latitudes, the particles create auroral displays in the starry night sky. Ghostly towers of steam and gas are venting from local fumaroles to dance against the eerie, greenish northern lights. For now, auroral activity on planet Earth remains in a high state as the Sun is only in early phases of decline from the peak of its solar activity cycle. And soon, other ghostly apparitions may dance in your neighborhood. Happy Halloween!

2 Suspects Arrested in Connection to Murder of ‘SNL’ Writer’s Sister

📅 Oct 31, 2026  |  Category: World  |  Read Source →

Two people have been arrested in connection to the murder of Christina Downer, the sister of Saturday Night Live writer Jimmy Fowlie.

The Los Angeles Police Department’s Robbery-Homicide Division announced the arrest of Delorean Bijon Brandon and Kaylee Nicole Markowski via a media release on Friday, October 30.

“On December 18, 2025, Christina Downer was reported missing after her ex-husband requested a welfare check when he had not heard from her for an extended period of time. Downer’s boyfriend, Delorean Brandon, claimed that she had moved out several months earlier, leaving behind all her belongings, including her vehicle and dog,” the LAPD said.

The statement further read, “Robbery-Homicide Division detectives assumed the investigation after Downer’s cellphone was discovered washed ashore on Santa Monica Beach. Through various investigative techniques, detectives identified Delorean Brandon and Kaylee Markowski as the individuals responsible for Downer’s murder, the disposal of her body, and the concealment of their involvement.”

‘SNL’ Writer Jimmy Fowlie Says Missing Sister 'Is No Longer Alive'

Brandon and Markowski were each charged with murder and remanded on $2 million bail. The LAPD’s Robbery-Homicide Division will present the case to the L.A. County District Attorney’s Office for filing consideration. The investigation is still ongoing.

Legal representation information for the two suspects was not immediately available.

Christina Downer and Jimmy Fowlie Courtesy of Jimmy Fowlie/Instagram

The LAPD announcement comes six months after Fowlie shared a sad update on his sister’s disappearance.

“The LAPD has informed our family that Christina is no longer alive, and the case has officially transitioned from a missing person to a homicide investigation,” Fowlie wrote via Instagram on April 29. “Our prayers for her to be found safely have transformed into prayers for the truth to be revealed and for those responsible to be held accountable.”

'SNL' Writer Jimmy Fowlie's Sister Christina's Disappearance: Everything to Know

In December 2025, Fowlie first asked for the public’s help in locating his missing sister.

“My sister has been missing and we are worried that she isn’t safe,” he wrote via Instagram at the time. “Her married name is Downer but she may go by Christina Fowlie. Please share this so that if anyone has seen her, they can give any information to the police.”

Fowlie listed a phone number to contact police and a case number before confirming his sister was last seen in Los Angeles.

“We have reason to believe that in the weeks before she went missing, her phone and social media were compromised. The individual(s) in possession of her phone used it to hide the fact she was gone, to ask for money and to create a false narrative that she was going ‘off the grid,’” Fowlie wrote. “I am sharing this because I believe that whoever is responsible is hoping to erase her in every way possible. I want to amplify her story in the only way I know how. And to express that she was a beautiful person who matters in this world, especially to me.”
